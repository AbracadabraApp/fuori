## Models

```
What every card is:
- One topic. Nothing under it: no tip, no second topic, no "try this next time".
- Bite-sized, but not sparse: about three lines. Two or three phrases, about five words, a fact of two or three sentences. A saying, a local word or a small word may add one plain English line.
- Lines in the language, each with its English under it. Whole sentences people really say, no blanks.
- Lines in the order they're said: the question, then the answer.
- Plain: what's right and useful to know. Never a mistake, a wrong form, or a correction.
- Nothing about the next visit, no city name, no day of the week.

The models (kind; headline; lines as "language | English"; a "Note:" line is a plain English line and stays in its place among the lines):

1. when-you
Headline: When you introduce yourself
Mi chiamo Joshua. | My name is Joshua.
Come si chiama? | What's your name?
Piacere. | Nice to meet you.
Why it works: a situation the learner meets, as the two or three lines that carry it, in spoken order.

2. formal-informal
Headline: Formal and informal
Come si chiama? | What's your name? · formal
Come ti chiami? | What's your name? · informal
Why it works: the same sentence twice, one above the other, each marked.

3. about-this-place
Headline: About this garden
Note: The giant fig trees came from Australia in the 1800s. Their roots hang down from the branches and grow into new trunks.
Why it works: one fact about where you stood that you'd be glad to know, in plain English.

4. vocabulary
Headline: Vocabulary
il cannolo | the cannolo
la ricotta | ricotta
il banco | the counter
prendere | to take, to have
pagare | to pay
Why it works: the words from one scene, each with its English: nouns with their article, verbs in the form you'd look up, in any mix.

6. culture
Headline: Culture
Note: In the early evening many people go out for the passeggiata, a slow walk along the main street. It's for seeing friends and being seen, not for getting anywhere.
Why it works: one fact about how people live there, not about a building, in two or three sentences.

7. a-saying
Headline: A saying
Chi va piano va sano e va lontano. | Who goes slowly goes safely and goes far.
Note: Slow and steady gets you there.
Con calma: chi va piano va sano e va lontano. | Take it easy: slow and steady gets you there.
Why it works: the saying, its words in English, what it means, then the saying used in a sentence.

8. a-word-from-here
Headline: A word from here
amuninni | let's go, in Sicilian
Amuninni, è tardi! | Let's go, it's late!
Note: You hear it all over Palermo, even from people speaking {language}.
Why it works: one local word, the language it comes from when that isn't {language}, a sentence that uses it, and where you hear it.

9. this-or-that
Headline: This or that
Al banco o al tavolo? | At the counter or at a table?
Al banco, grazie. | At the counter, thanks.
Al tavolo, per favore. | At a table, please.
Why it works: the question that offers the choice, then the two answers, built alike so they read as a pair. A choice of two that isn't formal and informal.

10. learn-it
Headline: Learn it
Term: ma dai
Note: One phrase, several feelings: no way, I've had it, I can't believe it. The tone tells you which.
Ma dai, davvero? | No way, really?
Ma dai, basta! | Oh come on, I've had it!
Ma dai, non ci credo! | I can't believe it!
Why it works: a word or phrase people use all the time: first why it matters, then one example for each meaning people really use. One word per card.

A card of a new kind is welcome when the material calls for one: give it a short kind name and a plain headline, and hold it to the same standard.
```
