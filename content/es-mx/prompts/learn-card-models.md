# Learn card models

version: 2026-11-23

The ten cards Josh drew for the sheet after a visit (docs/learn-sheet-2-design.md), as models: the
standard every card is written to, not a list of the only kinds there are, and not cards in the
bank (Josh, 2026-11-23: "the 10 examples do not need to be complete example even for the 10 cards -
the writer could redraft them - but the 10 are models"). Read by scripts/learn-cards.mts, and later
by the visit writer. Italy's file holds the same ten in Italian.

## Models

```
What every card is:
- One topic. Nothing under it: no tip, no second topic, no "try this next time".
- Short: two or three phrase lines, five things, three actions, or one fact of a sentence or two.
- Lines in the language, each with its English under it. Whole sentences people really say, no blanks.
- Lines in the order they're said: the question, then the answer.
- Plain: what's right and useful to know. Never a mistake, a wrong form, or a correction.
- Nothing about the next visit, no city name, no day of the week.

The models (kind; headline; lines as "language | English"; note):

1. when-you
Headline: When you order
¿Qué le damos? | What can we get you?
Me da un tamal de chipilín, por favor. | A chipilín tamale, please.
Why it works: a situation the learner meets, as the two or three lines that carry it, in spoken order.

2. formal-informal
Headline: Formal and informal
¿Cómo se llama? | What's your name? · formal
¿Cómo te llamas? | What's your name? · informal
Why it works: the same sentence twice, one above the other, each marked.

3. about-this-place
Headline: About this comedor
Note: A comedor is a small family kitchen. At midday it serves the comida corrida: soup, rice, a main dish and a drink for one price.
Why it works: one fact about where you stood that you'd be glad to know, in plain English.

4. vocabulary-things
Headline: Vocabulary: things
el tamal | the tamale
el atole | warm corn drink
el chipilín | a green herb
la olla | the pot
el frío | the cold
Why it works: five nouns from one scene, each with its article.

5. vocabulary-actions
Headline: Vocabulary: actions
querer | to want
dar | to give
saber | to taste
Why it works: three verbs in the form you'd look up.

6. culture
Headline: Culture
Note: At home many families speak Tsotsil or Tseltal, and Spanish in town.
Why it works: one fact about how people live there, not about a building.

7. a-saying
Headline: A saying
A falta de pan, tortillas. | No bread, then tortillas.
Note: You make do with what you have.
Why it works: the saying, its words in English, then what it means.

8. a-word-from-here
Headline: A word from here
kolaval | thank you, in Tsotsil
Why it works: one local word, with the language it comes from when that isn't Spanish.

9. this-or-that
Headline: This or that
¿Con leche? | With milk?
Un café sin azúcar. | A coffee without sugar.
Why it works: a choice of two that isn't formal and informal, each in a short line.

10. a-small-word
Headline: A small word
Term: ahorita
Ahorita se lo traigo. | I'll bring it right away.
Ahorita no, gracias. | Not just now, thanks.
Why it works: one little word doing a lot of work, shown in two sentences.

A card of a new kind is welcome when the material calls for one: give it a short kind name and a plain headline, and hold it to the same standard.
```
