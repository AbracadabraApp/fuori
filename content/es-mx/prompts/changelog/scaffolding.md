# scaffolding.md changelog

Newest first. One entry per version: what changed, why, what prompted it. The prompt is `../scaffolding.md` (called beginner-2.md until 2026-11-10b).

### 2026-11-11b: Level 1 turns are two or three sentences, not "a few"
- **Changed:** "Keep each turn short: a few sentences" → "two or three sentences", in the Level 1 frame. No other words.
- **Why:** Josh, 2026-11-11, after playing Beginner 2 in Mexico with the intro live: "each turn felt about as long as the intro" (the intro is six to eight sentences). The turns had grown: the cluster-1 runs on 10s read three to four pieces a turn, and with the answers and the shape note the lines ran longer still. "A few" is the loosest word in the brief; "two or three" is what Beginner 1's S1 said. Measured with tests/experiments/b2-frame-line-live.mts (words a turn) before the commit.

### 2026-11-11a: the answers to say back come back to cluster 1, on the one-line reply
- **Added:** "Reply: line and answers", the reply format for Beginner 2 on the Intermediate reply (cluster 1 since 10s): the line and its English as always, then `answers`, each in {language} with its English, shown as bubbles and not said. The turn's setup line (opener, two answers, two questions to ask, goodbye) goes in brackets at the end of the newest message, as it did in the parts format; the Answers rules go in the brief. `lib/conversation/take-turn.ts` → cluster 1 asks for the answers (`runTurn` with `answers`), and sends them to the app as answer parts; the app keeps her line as the subtitle when the parts are only answers.
- **Why:** since 10s cluster 1 had no answers to say back (the Intermediate reply panel stood in). Josh, 2026-11-11: "getting answer bubbles working again first". Not a trial: the frame Josh judged is unchanged; the answers and their rules are the ones every other cluster already asks for.

### 2026-11-10s: Level 1 is the frame brief as the list used it, on the Intermediate reply (one line), not the parts format
- **Changed:** "Level: 1" is now the brief that made the list Josh judged (`tests/experiments/frame-mix.mts`, F1), with no mention of parts: a bilingual local talking mostly in English, the {language} inside the English sentences with its English beside it, two or three a turn, the question in {language} at the end with its English. What goes in: set phrases and greetings, what you'd tell them to do, a price, one short sentence that does something; a thing's name only when it's in front of you (Josh, 2026-11-10: the {language} in the live transcript "are likely things like nouns"). And `lib/conversation/take-turn.ts` runs cluster 1 on the Intermediate reply (`runTurn` with this block as the brief), not on the parts format.
- **Why:** Josh played 10q live: "all turns - spanish only on question"; then "why isn't it just using the frame". The list that he judged ("These are strong") was made by the Intermediate path with this brief; 10q–10r put the brief into the parts format, where the character pulls the {language} out of the sentence into its own box, and three wordings failed three ways. This uses the path that made the list. On screen: the whole line as the subtitle, as at Intermediate; the two answers to say back come later (the one-line-plus-spans format, docs/beginner-2-step-2.md), the reply panel Intermediate has in the first conversations meanwhile.

### 2026-11-10r: Level 1 asks for two or three things in the language every turn, inside the English sentences
- **Changed:** "the one short {language} sentence that matters" → two or three things in {language} a turn besides the question, each inside an English sentence where it falls (word part with its gloss, english parts around it), "never a row of {language} phrases on their own". The first wording of 10r ("Every turn has two or three things in {language}…") gave 33% and 2.0 phrases a turn but as rows of labels between the English ("La silla. El cepillo."), often unglossed: the parts format invites the {language} into its own box.
- **Why:** Josh, playing Beginner 2 cluster 1 live (2026-11-10, the night 10q went out): "speaking straight english except question". The connection runs had shown the lean (one {language} phrase a turn plus the question, 20–24% of spoken words; one opener with only the question): the character read "the one short sentence" as one thing in the language per turn. The list that Josh judged (frame-mix.mts) had 30% and two to four phrases a turn.

### 2026-11-10q: Level 1 is the English frame with the language slotted in (the frame mix)
- **Changed:** "Level: 1" no longer asks for short {language} sentences each followed by its English. It now describes a bilingual local talking mostly in English to someone with no {language} yet, putting the {language} in where it fits (names of things, set phrases, the one short sentence that matters), each as a word part with its English as the gloss, and the question in {language} with its English.
- **Why:** Josh, 2026-11-10, on the echo: "Spanish is followed by English sentence by sentence… too structured". After cards B2-E to B2-E5 (short glosses, woven English, a rewriter, a summary, an English thought inside the line), the code-switching research (Poplack's equivalence constraint, Myers-Scotton's matrix language frame) pointed to flipping the frame language by level: English holds the frame at the bottom of the ladder, so comprehension follows the frame and the {language} heard is whole phrases in place. A list of 8 lines from this brief (`tests/experiments/frame-mix.mts`, Mexico, Don Chucho, Memo, Mateo, Doña Rosario): Josh: "These are strong. For early learners - these are better than the echo or summary"; "they are all consistently good". The sandwich repeat (say it, English, say it again) was in the first list and Josh found it weird: not in the line. With this, the code's English after every phrase (glossAll) and the summary (2026-11-10p) are off in cluster 1; the question's English stays by code (Josh: "Final Question always gets English echo").

### 2026-11-10p: the Summary block: one English remark in her voice after each stretch of her talk (cluster 1)
- **Added:** "Summary", the prompt of a side call (lib/beginner-2.ts → writeSummaries) that writes one English sentence per stretch of her talk (a stretch closes past 20 words or at 2 sentences). In cluster 1 the code says it after the stretch instead of the literal English after every phrase (glossAll); her question keeps its full English. Late or failed, the literal English plays as before.
- **Why:** Josh, 2026-11-10, on the echo: "Spanish is followed by English sentence by sentence… too structured". Card B2-E5 (heard, not read): with the bare line, half the summaries narrated her in the third person and Josh preferred the literal; with "in her own words, as she would say it" narration fell from 5 of 16 turns to 1 of 16, the summaries stayed shorter than the literal on 16 of 16 (0.76 of its words) and arrived in 2.1 s. Nothing else in the line, by Josh's rule. The Level 1 text is unchanged: her per-phrase English is still asked for and is the fallback.

### 2026-11-10o: the learner line without status: no "Beginner", no "living here", no "new or where from"
- **Also:** "Setup: two answers" now asks "about what's here or what you're doing": the two-answers turns needed an easy yes/no question, and "Is it your first time here?" was the easiest (10 of 13 times the question came on a two-answers turn).
- **Removed:** the assumption that the learner is a newcomer who has come to live in the country ("They're living in {country} for a while", "a person who has recently come to live in {city}", "the way a newcomer politely would", and the like). The learner is someone learning about the country, its language and culture; nothing is assumed about where they're from, where they live or for how long, and characters know only what the learner has told them.
- **Why:** Josh, 2026-11-10: characters kept asking whether the learner is new, from here or how long they're staying ("cada personaje pregunta lo mismo"; Doña Carmen: "¿Es porque veo africano?"); "these are all patches that compounded the problem… The game should work for someone regardless of where they are from or how long. Imagine an Italian of African background is playing and our game always asks if they are a newcomer or how long they are visiting." The persona came from the game's beginning (someone staying in Rome with a landlady); patches since kept reinforcing it. The same change in every prompt that carried it, both countries, and the city files' "helps newcomers". Measured with automated replies before release (docs/automated-replies.md).
