# Fuori

Voice-first Italian learning game. **The goal is to learn the country, Italy or Mexico (Josh, 2026-11-04): its language and its culture, food, traditions, history and geography, so the learner can discuss real things with real people in their language.** "What the learner learns" below means all of that; textbooks are the model for teaching the language part. **It's a language-learning game, not a language-generating game (Josh, 2026-10-19):** when teaching conflicts with speed, garbled-speech handling, character traits or liveliness, teaching wins. Judge a change first by what the learner learns; the rest are guardrails. Textbooks are the model for how a language is taught. Read `README.md` and the numbered files in `docs/` before making product or architecture decisions; they are the source of truth for the design. Use the words in `docs/glossary.md` (Learn, scaffolding, and the rest as Josh settles them).

- `prototype/index.html` is the original claude.ai artifact demo. Keep it working and don't refactor it into the app; port ideas from it instead.
- The learner is someone learning about the country, its language and culture (Josh, 2026-11-10). The game assumes nothing about where they're from, where they live or for how long: no newcomer, no visitor, no "staying for a while". Characters know only what the learner has told them, and remember them across days. (Replaces the original persona, "someone who loves Italy and is staying for a while… treated as a newcomer", which made characters ask everyone whether they were new or where they were from: Josh: "Imagine an Italian of African background is playing and our game always asks if they are a newcomer or how long they are visiting.")
- The world is open-ended: Claude generates places, people and scenes during play, and every day comes with 3–4 suggestions plus a change of scenery. Don't hand-write scripted scenes; hand-written content is limited to city pantries, Magda and level rules. That rule was written for the game before it had a beginner level, so it doesn't rule out scripting beginner scaffolding levels 1–3, where the learner only taps a reply (Josh, 2026-10-17; on hold until there's a spec, see `docs/06-roadmap.md` → 4b). No character is special: everyone except Magda comes from the city files the same way (Josh; Giulia and Rita's "anchor" code removed 2026-10-16).
- Characters speak Italian, forgive speech-recognition noise, and correct real errors by recasting in character. When the learner asks for help, in English or in Italian, a character helps in English as much as it takes (a word, a phrase, a short explanation), then goes back to Italian; English nobody asked for is the thing to avoid (Josh, 2026-10-16: this already works well in the game; the policy follows it). **Beginners are different (N5, Josh, 2026-10-25; first playable version):** characters speak to a Beginner in parts: a sentence or two of spoken English to set the scene and explain (heard, not shown), then in the language the words that matter, one question, and whole-sentence answers to say back (Beginner level dialogue, `lib/beginner.ts`, `docs/beginner-scaffolding.md` → Goals). For Intermediate and Advanced, the rest of this paragraph holds. Everyone teaches, always in role: characters help the learner the way a real person in their role would (slowing down, repeating, rephrasing, offering a word and its English, recasting a mistake); none steps out of role to sound like a language teacher with a lesson plan. Explicit lessons belong to Magda, and Magda just teaches in lessons (Josh, 2026-11-07): she appears only in Learn's lessons, never in a city, a place or an ordinary conversation, and there are no schools in the game. Lessons don't depend on places: her studio comes from her own content (`lib/tutor.ts`), and the server answers her only inside a lesson. Teaching mode, not immersion: English and the language freely, and she uses the learner's record to choose what to work on (`docs/learn.md`). For M1, `docs/M1-CONVERSATION-MODEL.md` is the source of truth for conversations and wins over `docs/04-conversation-engine.md`; keep the prompt short and the reply to `{ line, en }`.
- API keys are server-side only: `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (Claude), `GROQ_API_KEY` (Whisper on Groq), and later the TTS key. Never call these APIs from the browser and never commit `.env*` files.
- Speech in is Whisper on Groq (`/v1/transcribe`), not on-device speech recognition. Speech out is ElevenLabs through `/v1/speak`, falling back to the phone's own voice (`AVSpeechSynthesizer`) when it fails. The app never holds API keys or calls those services directly.
- The primary target is the native iPhone app in `ios/` (SwiftUI, iOS 26; plan in `docs/12-native-app.md`). The website is switched off (Josh, 2026-10-26): `proxy.ts` answers 404 to everything but `/v1` (the app), `/api/admin` and `/healthz`, since the web `/api` routes had no sign-in; the web code stays in the repo, unused (delete `proxy.ts` to bring it back). Hosting is Railway, which deploys the `live` branch (Josh, 2026-11-06); work and push on `main`, and `npm run release` puts `main` live (below).
- Keep `progress.md` current at the end of each session: what was done, decisions, open issues, next actions.
- Hand-written content (city pantries, Magda, level rules) lives in `content/it/` as data, not inside components. Generated characters and scenes are stored with learner data.
- Keep other languages possible without building them: nothing Italian-specific hard-coded in code or prompt templates (language name, speech-to-text code, voices, UI words and prompt examples come from `content/it/language.ts`), and all Claude calls go through `lib/claude.ts`. See "Keeping other languages possible" in `docs/05-architecture.md`; `npm run test:language` enforces it.
- Don't build on predicting what learners or characters will say: no phrase lists or keyword filters; prefer signals that work in any language (recording length, silence, a judge call).

## Testing
- Run `npx tsc --noEmit` after editing TypeScript or content files. Never claim a task is done until type checking passes.
- Check the dev server compiles without errors before showing work.
- For UI changes, verify the page loads correctly with `curl http://localhost:3000` or browser check.
- Don't modify or weaken existing tests to make them pass without approval.
- When fixing bugs, add a test that would have caught it.

## Definition of done
Added 2026-10-13 after M2 was reported "complete" while its core features had never run. The full account is in `docs/11-lessons-learned.md`.
- Done means used. Before calling anything done, complete or live, run it end to end the way a learner would (`npm run smoke:live`, and `COUNTRY=es-mx npm run smoke:live` for Mexico, or a browser) and say what was run. If it wasn't run, the commit says "not verified".
- Releasing is `npm run release` (from `~/fuori`, which is linked to Railway): it moves `live` to `main`, waits for both services if anything Railway builds changed, runs `smoke:live` in both countries once and prints one line. Then read `railway logs` for errors from the new code. Release only with a new app build on Josh's phone or TestFlight, or when Josh says "release" (Josh, 2026-11-06: "only release for new builds on phone or test flight"); never mid-task, and run it in the background. Server work waits on `main` until then.
- No silent failures: don't hide a failing call in a fire-and-forget `.catch()`; call server code directly, not over HTTP to the app itself.
- No placeholders in shipped code: no `TODO` stand-ins, demo ids or `localhost` URLs.
- Before reusing a database column, field, id or file, search for what already uses it.
- Test the connection, not just the parts: the place finds its character, ending a conversation stores what it should, the screen calls a route that exists.
- No test flags for now (Josh, 2026-10-21): the users are Josh and his family, so new features ship on, not behind a setting. A release changes what they play; anything that could break a conversation still needs to be run first (`smoke:live`). A flag is still right when code needs something production doesn't have yet (a column before its migration).
- Never stash, clean or reset files you didn't create (`git stash -u`, `git clean`, `git checkout -- .`); another session may be mid-task in the same folder. Commit generated files promptly.

## iPhone app
One app for every country (Fuori in Italy, Afuera in Mexico; the country is chosen in onboarding): nothing in Swift assumes Italian, and every screen is checked in both countries. Follow Apple's Human Interface Guidelines strictly: use iOS's own components and behaviours and style on top of them. Full rules, decisions and reasons: `docs/13-ios-conventions.md` (from the HIG; `npm run hig` downloads a local copy).
- Must hold on every screen: the standard back button and swipe, and every exit (back, swipe, "Sali", background) saves through one leave function; text sizes from text styles (`Font.custom(_:size:relativeTo:)`, `@ScaledMetric`), so Dynamic Type and Bold Text work; colours and sizes only from the tokens (`design/tokens.json`, `npm run tokens`), text contrast at least 4.5:1; light-only until the night-paper palette (N2); the settings button (Fuori's own icon, `SettingsIcon`) top right on every screen; words in the learned language and any country default (a starting city) from `/v1/content`, never in Swift; no AI notes in the game: explanations only in onboarding and Settings → About Fuori.
- Conversation screen: standard navigation bar, then the approved paper name band, then the portrait. Subtitles type out in step with the voice; the English translation never shows until the character has finished speaking. The character speaks by himself, then the mic opens by itself (hands-free for everyone, Josh, 2026-10-26: "hands free on 5s") and silence sends the turn, about 2.5 s (a tap sends early); with nothing said for 5 s the mic closes and it's still the learner's turn, a tap away. No Hands-free setting (hidden 2026-10-23). The bottom bar is one circle in two colours: green for you (mic, then your wave), terracotta for her (sending, then her wave) and the Learn link on the right; no Pause or Resume (Josh, 2026-10-22: "complexity before anyone asks for it"; `docs/bottom-bar.md`). Safeguards: calls, Siri, AirPods out and backgrounding return the learner to their turn and the mic never reopens without a tap; tap-to-talk when VoiceOver is on (no "more time to answer" setting: Josh, 2026-10-16, it would confuse; tap to talk has no timer).
- Testing a screen (Josh, 2026-11-06: "we do not need to optimize for non mic and huge text - its slowing everything down"; "put reasonable testing somewhere it will be followed its a language app that uses the mic and voice"; then, the simulator having taken 141 minutes in two days, "rarely do I get value from you using the simulator", and yes to this). Say in the commit what was checked:
  - A Swift change must build: `cd ios && make build` or XcodeBuildMCP, and CI (`.github/workflows/ios.yml`) passes.
  - A new or changed screen: Josh sees one quick look first (a mockup or a single screenshot) and judges it before it's built out. The menu card passed the simulator check and failed on sight ("This looks like crap").
  - On Josh's iPhone, for anything he'd notice (the voice, the mic, a conversation's flow, the look): played out loud with the mic, after his yes to install (never without it). This is the real test. A call, Siri, AirPods in and out, backgrounding and locking were checked by Josh 2026-10-27 ("works"); recheck only when audio handling changes.
  - No simulator otherwise: no routine screenshots, no turns played in the simulator, no other text sizes, VoiceOver or Reduce Motion passes. Only when Josh asks, or to debug something that only shows on screen; then one simulator, your own by its id (never `booted`), closed when done. Keep building with text styles, tokens and language-tagged text so the rest doesn't break.
  - Prompt and content changes are checked by the variety gate and `smoke:live`, not the simulator.
- Builds: `cd ios && make build` or XcodeBuildMCP; CI (`.github/workflows/ios.yml`) must pass. When the simulator is needed (above), to get past onboarding: `npm run dev:sim -- it beginner` (a warmed local server on port 3100, the newest local learner moved past onboarding; steps in `scripts/dev-sim.sh`), then launch with `-FuoriServer http://localhost:3100`, `-FuoriMute YES` (no voice, so screenshots don't wait) and `-FuoriOpen <city>/<place>`. Waiting for a deploy: `npm run deploy:wait`.

## Working across sessions
- Several Claude sessions work on this repo: Claude Code on Josh's laptop (sometimes two at once, in the same folder) and a cloud session in the Claude app's "Fuori" project. None can see another's uncommitted work.
- Start every session with `git pull --rebase origin main`. End every session by committing and pushing to `main`, and update `progress.md`.
- Never leave work unpushed overnight. If it isn't on GitHub, the other session and Railway can't see it.
- Coordinating laptop sessions (Claude, 2026-11-05, Josh: "this is your domain"; prompted by deploys cancelling each other: two sessions pushed server changes minutes apart, and Railway builds only the newest push, so of six deploys one finished):
  - Each laptop session works in its own git worktree (`git worktree add ../fuori-<topic> -b <topic>`), not in `~/fuori` alongside another session, so local servers, half-done files and `next-env.d.ts` don't collide. Merge into `main` and push when a piece of work is done; delete the worktree when the topic is.
  - Pushing to `main` deploys nothing (2026-11-06: Railway deploys `live`; in two days each service had built 59 times, once per push, a few cut short by another session's push). Push to `main` freely; release once with `npm run release` alongside the next phone or TestFlight build (above). Railway's watch patterns skip `ios/`, `docs/`, `design/`, `sketches/`, `tests/`, `.github/` and top-level `.md` files, and the release script says when nothing deploys.
  - Don't wait on a deploy you didn't release; `npm run release` waits once, for everyone's work on `main`.
  - Before merging a change to a shared file (`docs/prompt-trials.md`, `progress.md`, a prompt file), tell the other laptop session (ListAgents, SendMessage) so it isn't surprised by the conflict.
- Pictures (2026-11-06): the JPEG masters aren't in git; they're in R2 at `masters/` (`scripts/image-masters.mjs`). A session that makes new pictures but can't reach R2 (the cloud) commits the new JPEGs with `git add -f`; the laptop uploads them (`npm run images:upload`), then runs `scripts/image-masters.mjs --untrack` and commits. `docs/12-native-app.md` → Content and images.
- Model IDs live only in `lib/claude.ts`. Don't hard-code them elsewhere.
- Before writing a new prompt, read `docs/prompt-trials.md`: reuse an existing prompt if it does the job, and check whether the idea was already tried. Add a row there for every prompt trial, kept or dropped, in the same commit.
- **Cards are for experiments, not product development** (Josh, 2026-11-12). An experiment, a question answered by measuring one prompt against another, gets a card in `docs/prompt-trials.md`, run by a script that calls `startTest` (`tests/experiments/common.mts`), which refuses to run without the reviewed card; method: `docs/prompt-testing.md`; a test is chosen from its queue, not started on the spot. Building or changing a feature by Josh's play (an intro, a shape, a step of the ladder, a screen's words) is product development: it ships on his word with a changelog entry saying what changed, why and what prompted it, checked by the suites and a live run, and a card only when a decision hinges on a number. (Replaces "No prompt change without a test", 2026-11-07, which read ten product changes in one day as ten violations.)
- The character prompt lives in `content/it/prompts/character.md`, not in code. To change it: edit it, bump its `version`, and add a changelog entry in `content/it/prompts/changelog/character.md` (the changelogs moved out of the prompt files, 2026-11-08) saying what changed, why, and what prompted it. `npm run test:prompt` enforces the changelog. Compare with `npm run test:conversations`.
- Commit messages explain the change for someone who wasn't there: what changed, why, what prompted it (bug, test result, Josh's feedback), and anything deliberately not done. A one-line message is only for trivial changes.
