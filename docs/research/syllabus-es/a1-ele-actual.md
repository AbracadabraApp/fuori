# ELE Actual A1 (Libro del alumno): syllabus summary

A summary of the book's progression, for Afuera's own syllabus. ELE Actual A1 is published by SM (Spain; authors Virgilio Borobio and Ramón Palencia) and follows the Instituto Cervantes curriculum. Written in our own words; the Spanish phrases are ordinary everyday chunks, not copied dialogue. Page numbers are the printed student-book pages. The book has 14 short lessons, an optional 15th lesson on the past, and three review lessons; it calls them *lecciones*, not units. In the CSV, unit 15 = Lección opcional.

The book teaches the Spanish of Spain. Where a form in the book is one a Mexican speaker wouldn't use, the frames table gives the book form, then an arrow and the Mexican form, e.g. "¿Tenéis hijos? → ¿Tienen hijos? (MX)". The CSV uses the Mexican form. Each lesson also notes Spain-only vocabulary that isn't in a frame. The section "Spain vs Mexico across the book" at the end collects them.

## Lección 1: Saludos y presentaciones

**Pages:** Student book pp. 10–17

**What the learner can do**

- Greet someone and answer a greeting at any time of day
- Say your name and surname and ask someone theirs
- Spell a name letter by letter
- Ask for help in class: say you don't understand, ask for a repeat, check your spelling
- Say goodbye

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡Hola! | Hi! |
| Buenos días. / Buenas tardes. / Buenas noches. | Good morning. / Good afternoon. / Good evening. |
| Me llamo… | My name is… |
| ¿Cómo te llamas? | What's your name? |
| ¿Y tú? | And you? |
| ¿Cómo te apellidas? | What's your surname? |
| ¿Cómo se escribe? | How do you spell it? |
| No entiendo. | I don't understand. |
| ¿Puedes repetir, por favor? | Can you say that again, please? |
| ¿Está bien así? | Is this right? |
| ¡Adiós! | Bye! |
| Hasta mañana. | See you tomorrow. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Alphabet and spelling (including ch, ll, ñ)
2. Subject pronouns yo, tú
3. Present of llamarse and apellidarse (yo, tú only)

**Vocabulary themes:** Greetings and goodbyes; first names and surnames; the alphabet; classroom instructions; words Spanish shares with other languages.

**Settings and situations:** First day of class; introducing yourself to classmates; spelling a name on a form; famous Spanish-speaking names in headlines.

## Lección 2: Origen y procedencia

**Pages:** Student book pp. 18–27

**What the learner can do**

- Say where you are from and ask others
- Name countries and nationalities, masculine and feminine
- Say which languages you speak and which are spoken where
- Say you don't know; ask how to say or spell a word; ask someone to speak slower or louder
- Count from 0 to 20
- Recognise a handful of survival words for a bar (coffee, beer, water, how much is it?)

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿De dónde eres? | Where are you from? |
| Soy estadounidense, de… | I'm American, from… |
| ¿De dónde es? | Where is he/she from? |
| Es mexicana. | She's Mexican. |
| ¿Qué idiomas hablas? | What languages do you speak? |
| Hablo inglés y un poco de español. | I speak English and a little Spanish. |
| ¿Qué idioma se habla en…? | What language do they speak in…? |
| No sé. | I don't know. |
| ¿Cómo se dice … en español? | How do you say … in Spanish? |
| Más despacio, por favor. | Slower, please. |
| Más alto, por favor. | Louder, please. |
| Un café con leche, por favor. | A coffee with milk, please. |
| ¿Cuánto es? | How much is it? |
| Perdón. | Sorry. / Excuse me. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Subject pronouns él, ella
2. Present of ser and hablar (singular)
3. Gender of nationality adjectives (-o/-a; consonant + a; -e and -í invariable)
4. Question words ¿dónde?, ¿qué?
5. Numbers 0–20
6. Pronunciation: word stress and the written accent

**Vocabulary themes:** Countries and nationalities; languages; asking for help with words; numbers 0–20; a short list of bar and travel words (café con leche, cerveza, agua, bocadillo, habitación, ¿cuánto es?).

**Settings and situations:** Classmates comparing where they are from; guessing celebrities' nationalities; a traveller's survival phrases; Spanish as a world language.

## Lección 3: Información personal

**Pages:** Student book pp. 28–37

**What the learner can do**

- Say what you do for a living or what you study, and where
- Give your address, phone number and email, and ask for someone else's
- Fill in a simple form with personal details
- Count from 21 to 100 and use ordinal numbers for floors

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué haces? ¿Estudias o trabajas? | What do you do? Do you study or work? |
| Soy médico. / Soy estudiante. | I'm a doctor. / I'm a student. |
| ¿Dónde trabajas? | Where do you work? |
| Trabajo en un hospital. | I work in a hospital. |
| ¿Qué estudias? | What are you studying? |
| Estudio Medicina. | I'm studying medicine. |
| ¿Dónde vives? | Where do you live? |
| Vivo en la calle… | I live on … Street. |
| ¿En qué número? | What number? |
| ¿Qué teléfono tienes? | What's your phone number? |
| Es un móvil. → Es un celular. (MX) | It's a mobile number. |
| ¿Cuál es tu correo electrónico? | What's your email? |
| No tengo… | I don't have… |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Gender of nouns (masculine/feminine)
2. Indefinite article, singular: un, una
3. Present singular of trabajar, estudiar, vivir and tener
4. Question words ¿qué? and ¿cuál?
5. Numbers 21–100; ordinals 1st–10th
6. Pronunciation: intonation of questions and answers

**Vocabulary themes:** Jobs and workplaces (bar, hospital, shop, school, office, newspaper); subjects of study; street, square, avenue and their abbreviations; phone, mobile, fax, email; numbers to 100.

**Settings and situations:** Asking a new acquaintance about work; postal addresses; business cards from Madrid and Santiago de Chile; useful phone numbers (taxi, police, airport); a school enrolment form; immigrants working in Madrid.

## Lección 4: ¿Tú o usted?

**Pages:** Student book pp. 38–47

**What the learner can do**

- Choose between tú and usted and greet someone in each register
- Introduce one person to another and reply when introduced
- Ask for a person by name (at a reception desk or on the phone) and say "that's me"
- Check something you think is true (…, ¿no?)
- Use señor, señora, señorita with and without the article

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Buenos días, señora López. | Good morning, Mrs López. |
| ¿Qué tal está? | How are you? (formal) |
| ¿Qué tal estás? / ¿Qué tal? | How are you? / How's it going? (informal) |
| Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y usted? | Very well, thanks. And you? (formal) |
| Mire, le presento a… | Let me introduce… (formal) |
| Mira, este es Alberto, un amigo. | Look, this is Alberto, a friend. (informal) |
| Encantado. / Encantada. | Pleased to meet you. |
| Mucho gusto. | Nice to meet you. |
| ¿El señor Cortés, por favor? | Mr Cortés, please? (asking for someone) |
| Sí, soy yo. | Yes, that's me. |
| Un momento, por favor. | One moment, please. |
| Eres estadounidense, ¿no? | You're American, aren't you? |
| ¿Cómo se llama usted? | What's your name? (formal) |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Subject pronouns tú, usted
2. Definite article, singular: el, la; contraction al
3. Article with titles (el señor Pérez) and without it when addressing someone (señor Pérez)
4. Demonstrative pronouns este, esta
5. Tú vs usted forms of ser, estar, llamarse, hablar, trabajar, estudiar, vivir, tener
6. Pronunciation: r and rr

**Vocabulary themes:** Formal and informal greetings; titles (Sr., Sra., Srta.); introductions; tú, usted and vos across the Spanish-speaking world (vos forms such as hablás, tenés, sos).

**Settings and situations:** An office where a director introduces a new secretary; a bus stop, a law office, a gym and a bar in a comic about formal and informal address; a formal party where you meet strangers; how people greet in different Latin American countries.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Useful for Mexico: the book itself points out that Latin America uses usted more than Spain, and that vos is not used in Mexico. Ustedes is the only plural "you" in Mexico, formal or not.

## Lección 5: Mi familia

**Pages:** Student book pp. 48–59

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about your family: who they are, what they do, how old they are
- Say whether you are married or single
- Describe what someone looks like (is / has / wears)
- Describe someone's character
- Identify someone in a photo
- Say thank you

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Estás casado? / ¿Estás casada? | Are you married? |
| No, estoy soltero. | No, I'm single. |
| ¿A qué te dedicas? | What do you do for a living? |
| ¿Tenéis hijos? → ¿Tienen hijos? (MX) | Do you (two) have children? |
| Tenemos una hija. | We have a daughter. |
| ¿Cuántos años tiene? | How old is he/she? |
| ¿Tienes hermanos? | Do you have brothers or sisters? |
| Mi padre es abogado. | My father is a lawyer. |
| ¿Cómo es? | What's he/she like? |
| Es alto y lleva gafas. → Es alto y usa lentes. (MX) | He's tall and wears glasses. |
| Tiene los ojos marrones. → Tiene los ojos cafés. (MX) | He/she has brown eyes. |
| Es muy simpática. | She's really nice. |
| ¿Quién es este? | Who's this? |
| Es mi hermano mayor. | It's my older brother. |
| Mira, una foto de mi familia. | Look, a photo of my family. |
| Muchas gracias. | Thanks very much. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Present of estar and tener
2. Present plural (3rd person) of ser, hablar, trabajar, estudiar, vivir
3. Plural of nouns and adjectives
4. Possessives mi(s), tu(s), su(s)
5. Adjective agreement in gender and number
6. Question words ¿quién?, ¿cuántos/-as?, ¿cómo?
7. Quantifiers muy, bastante
8. Pronunciation: intonation of questions and answers

**Vocabulary themes:** Family members; marital status; age; physical description (tall, short, slim, young, hair, eyes, beard, moustache, glasses); colours; character adjectives (nice, funny, shy, serious, hard-working); ways of living (in a couple, alone, divorced, sharing a flat).

**Settings and situations:** A family tree; a street survey about family and jobs; a newspaper story about a runaway wife and a butler's description; showing family photos; an email to a Spanish student who will stay with your family; the population of Latin America; the family in Spain.

## Lección 6: Objetos

**Pages:** Student book pp. 64–73

**What the learner can do**

- Say what there is somewhere (hay…)
- Ask for things in a shop and ask to see one
- Point to things with this/that
- Ask the price and understand it
- Count from 101 to 10,000; talk about money and currencies

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hay un periódico en la mesa. | There's a newspaper on the table. |
| Buenos días. ¿Qué desea? | Good morning. What can I do for you? (shop assistant) |
| ¿Tienen cuadernos? | Do you have notebooks? |
| Quería una agenda. | I'd like a planner. |
| Quiero… | I want… |
| ¿Puedo ver ese rojo? | Can I see that red one? |
| ¿Este? — Sí, ese. | This one? — Yes, that one. |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta? | How much is it? |
| ¿Cuánto cuestan estas gafas? → ¿Cuánto cuestan estos lentes? (MX) | How much are these glasses? |
| Un euro con setenta y cinco céntimos. → Cuarenta pesos con cincuenta centavos. (MX) | One seventy-five (euros). / Forty pesos fifty. |
| Vale. Me lo llevo. → Está bien. Me lo llevo. (MX) | OK, I'll take it. |
| ¿Cuál es la moneda de tu país? | What's the currency in your country? |
| Tengo un bolígrafo y un diccionario. → Tengo una pluma y un diccionario. (MX) | I've got a pen and a dictionary. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Indefinite articles, singular and plural: un, una, unos, unas
2. Hay + indefinite article + noun
3. Demonstrative adjectives and pronouns este/ese (and plurals)
4. Question words ¿cuál?, ¿cuánto?
5. Numbers 101–10,000
6. Pronunciation: the syllable

**Vocabulary themes:** Everyday objects (stamps, envelopes, postcards, pens, planner, dictionary, keys, lamp, computer, mobile phone); shops (bookshop, stationer's, tobacconist's); euros, coins and notes; world currencies.

**Settings and situations:** Objects on a desk; a stationer's where a customer asks to see a notebook; a sunglasses and watch counter; Peruvian craft markets (ponchos, pottery, Andean flutes).

**Spain vs Mexico:** Spain-only words in this lesson's vocabulary: ordenador → computadora (MX); móvil → celular (MX); bolígrafo → pluma (MX, though bolígrafo is understood); estanco → no Mexican equivalent (stamps come from the post office, cigarettes from any shop); céntimos → centavos, euros → pesos.

## Lección 7: Mi pueblo, mi ciudad

**Pages:** Student book pp. 74–83

**What the learner can do**

- Say where a town or city is (north of, near, on the coast)
- Describe a town or city
- Say how many people live there, roughly
- Ask and say what a country's capital is
- Give a reason (porque…) and say what a place is famous for

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Dónde está Oaxaca? | Where is Oaxaca? |
| Está en el sur del país. | It's in the south of the country. |
| Está al norte de… | It's north of… |
| Está cerca de… / lejos de… | It's near… / far from… |
| ¿Cómo es? | What's it like? |
| Es una ciudad muy bonita y tranquila. | It's a very pretty, quiet city. |
| Es pequeña, pero tiene muchos monumentos. | It's small, but it has lots of monuments. |
| Tiene playa y un puerto importante. | It has a beach and an important port. |
| ¿Cuántos habitantes tiene? | How many people live there? |
| Más de dos millones. / Casi un millón. | Over two million. / Almost a million. |
| ¿Cuál es la capital de…? | What's the capital of…? |
| Es famosa por el café. | It's famous for its coffee. |
| ¿Por qué vives aquí? | Why do you live here? |
| Porque es muy tranquilo. | Because it's very quiet. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Ser vs estar: ser to describe places and give identity, estar to say where they are
2. Prepositions and adverbs of place: en, cerca (de), lejos (de), al norte de…
3. Question words ¿cuántos?, ¿dónde?, ¿cómo?, ¿por qué?, ¿cuál?
4. Porque vs por (famosa por…)
5. Numbers from 10,001 into the millions; approximations (más de, casi, unos…)
6. Pronunciation: z/ce/ci vs ca/que/qui/co/cu

**Vocabulary themes:** Compass points; city and village (pueblo); coast, island, river, forest, desert; adjectives for places (big, small, old, modern, quiet, boring, touristy); population; capitals; Latin American geography (Andes, Amazon, Caribbean).

**Settings and situations:** Maps of Spain and Central America; Barcelona and Antigua (Guatemala); guessing a city by yes/no questions; places famous for wine, coffee, tobacco, beaches or fiestas; a conversation about someone's home town; Madrid.

**Spain vs Mexico:** The pronunciation activity teaches the Spain distinction between z/ce/ci (/θ/, like English "th") and s. Mexican Spanish has no /θ/: z, ce, ci and s all sound like s (seseo). Afuera should model Mexican pronunciation and not treat seseo as an error.

## Lección 8: Mi casa y mi habitación

**Pages:** Student book pp. 84–93

**What the learner can do**

- Describe a flat or house: where it is, what it's like, what it has
- Describe a room and what's in it
- Say where things are (next to, under, in front of, between…)

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cómo es tu casa? | What's your home like? |
| Es antigua y bastante grande. | It's old and quite big. |
| ¿Cuántas habitaciones tiene? → ¿Cuántas recámaras tiene? (MX) | How many bedrooms does it have? |
| Está cerca del centro. | It's near the centre. |
| ¿Está bien comunicada? | Is it well connected (for transport)? |
| Tiene mucha luz. | It gets lots of light. |
| No tiene ascensor. → No tiene elevador. (MX) | It doesn't have a lift. |
| Vivo en un piso de alquiler. → Rento un departamento. (MX) | I rent a flat. |
| Comparto piso con dos amigas. → Comparto departamento con dos amigas. (MX) | I share a flat with two friends. |
| ¿Qué hay en el salón? → ¿Qué hay en la sala? (MX) | What's in the living room? |
| Hay una mesa y cuatro sillas. | There's a table and four chairs. |
| ¿Dónde está el sofá? | Where's the sofa? |
| Está enfrente de la ventana. | It's opposite the window. |
| Está al lado de… / encima de… / debajo de… | It's next to… / on top of… / under… |
| Está en el frigorífico. → Está en el refri. (MX) | It's in the fridge. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Ser for descriptions (mi casa es antigua)
2. Estar for location (la cocina está al lado del salón)
3. Hay vs está(n): hay + indefinite article for what exists, definite article + está(n) for where a known thing is
4. Prepositions and adverbs of place: delante de, detrás de, encima de, debajo de, a la izquierda/derecha de, dentro/fuera de, al lado de, enfrente de, alrededor de, entre… y…
5. Pronunciation: the stressed syllable

**Vocabulary themes:** Rooms (living room, bedroom, bathroom, dining room, kitchen, study); features of a flat (heating, air conditioning, lift, garage, terrace, light, wooden floors); furniture and appliances (sofa, armchair, wardrobe, shelves, bed, shower, bath, washing machine, microwave, fridge); a flat-for-sale ad.

**Settings and situations:** A flat-for-sale ad; a friend describing her new flat; hide-and-seek in a house plan; an email to the Spanish student describing your home and his room; housing in Spain; how five people live (rented, shared, with parents, a rented house outside Madrid, alone).

**Spain vs Mexico:** Spain-only home words: piso → departamento (MX); salón → sala (MX); dormitorio and habitación → recámara (MX, for bedroom); cuarto de baño → baño; frigorífico → refrigerador / refri (MX); ascensor → elevador (MX); alquiler → renta (MX); chalé → casa (MX).

## Lección 9: Gustos

**Pages:** Student book pp. 94–103

**What the learner can do**

- Say what you like and don't like, and how much
- Ask someone what they like
- Agree or disagree about likes (me too, me neither, I do, I don't)
- Talk about someone else's likes

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Te gusta el fútbol? | Do you like football? |
| Me gusta mucho el cine. | I really like films. |
| Me gustan las películas de aventuras. | I like adventure films. |
| No me gusta nada… | I don't like … at all. |
| ¡Me encanta! | I love it! |
| ¿Y a ti? | And you? (about likes) |
| A mí también. / A mí tampoco. | Me too. / Me neither. |
| A mí sí. / A mí no. | I do. / I don't. |
| A mi hermano le encanta bailar. | My brother loves dancing. |
| No me gustan los ordenadores. → No me gustan las computadoras. (MX) | I don't like computers. |
| A vosotros os gusta…, ¿verdad? → A ustedes les gusta…, ¿verdad? (MX) | You (all) like…, right? |
| Nos gusta viajar. | We like travelling. |
| Es precioso. / Es horrible. | It's lovely. / It's awful. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Gustar and encantar: gusta + singular noun or infinitive, gustan + plural noun
2. Indirect object pronouns me, te, le, nos, os, les (with a mí, a ti…)
3. También, tampoco, sí, no for agreeing and disagreeing
4. Degrees of liking: me encanta, me gusta mucho, me gusta, no me gusta, no me gusta nada
5. Pronunciation: the stressed syllable

**Vocabulary themes:** Free-time activities (dancing, TV, radio, football, tennis, skiing, reading, music, cinema, theatre, going out, internet, chatting online, travelling, running); likes and dislikes; Latin American music (salsa, tango, ranchera, cumbia, Andean music, Cuban music); sport in Spain.

**Settings and situations:** Comparing likes with a classmate; a likes poem; a logic puzzle; reacting to a painting and to music; a comic about Spaniards and sport.

## Lección 10: Mi barrio, horarios públicos y el tiempo

**Pages:** Student book pp. 104–115

**What the learner can do**

- Describe your neighbourhood: what's there, how much of it, what you like most and least
- Say your favourite place
- Ask and tell the time
- Ask when shops and services open and close
- Use days of the week, months and seasons
- Talk about the weather and the climate

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| En mi barrio hay muchas tiendas. → En mi colonia hay muchas tiendas. (MX) | There are lots of shops in my neighbourhood. |
| Hay pocas zonas verdes. | There aren't many green spaces. |
| Es tranquilo y está muy bien comunicado. | It's quiet and has good transport. |
| Lo que más me gusta de mi barrio es… → Lo que más me gusta de mi colonia es… (MX) | What I like most about my neighbourhood is… |
| Mi lugar favorito es una plaza pequeña. | My favourite place is a little square. |
| ¿Qué hora es? | What time is it? |
| Es la una y cuarto. | It's quarter past one. |
| Son las tres y media. | It's half past three. |
| Son las cinco menos diez. → Son diez para las cinco. (MX) | It's ten to five. |
| ¿A qué hora abren las tiendas? | What time do the shops open? |
| Abren a las nueve y media. | They open at half past nine. |
| Los sábados por la tarde está cerrado. | It's closed on Saturday afternoons. |
| ¿Qué tiempo hace? | What's the weather like? |
| Hace calor. / Hace frío. / Hace sol. | It's hot. / It's cold. / It's sunny. |
| Llueve mucho. / Está nublado. | It rains a lot. / It's cloudy. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Hay / es / está / tiene for describing a place
2. Quantifiers with nouns: mucho/-a/-os/-as, bastante(s), algún/alguna/-os/-as, poco/-a/-os/-as
3. Mi sitio preferido es…; Lo que más / menos me gusta es (que)…
4. Telling the time (es la una / son las…, y cuarto, y media, menos…)
5. Prepositions a, de, por with times (a las cinco, de la tarde, por la mañana)
6. Days of the week, months and seasons
7. Llover and nevar: llueve, nieva
8. Muy vs mucho

**Vocabulary themes:** Places in a neighbourhood (metro station, park, church, school, library, shopping centre, bus stop, hospital, cinema, supermarket, town hall, pharmacy, car park, pedestrian street, cash machine); clock times; days, months, seasons; weather.

**Settings and situations:** A neighbourhood street plan; a woman describing her old central neighbourhood in Madrid; three Madrid neighbourhoods compared; a shop's opening-hours sign; public opening hours in Spain (shops close 2–5 pm, banks mornings only); a weather map; an email to a foreign friend choosing a neighbourhood.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Mexican city neighbourhoods are colonias; barrio exists but usually means an old, traditional quarter. Also aparcamiento → estacionamiento (MX). Spanish opening hours (long lunch closure) do not match Mexico. In Mexico the time is also given as "faltan diez para las cinco".

## Lección 11: Un día normal

**Pages:** Student book pp. 120–129

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about your daily routine
- Ask and say what time you do things
- Describe someone else's typical day

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿A qué hora te levantas? | What time do you get up? |
| Me levanto a las siete. | I get up at seven. |
| Me ducho y desayuno. → Me baño y desayuno. (MX) | I shower and have breakfast. |
| ¿Comes en casa? | Do you have lunch at home? |
| No, en el trabajo. | No, at work. |
| Empiezo a trabajar a las nueve. | I start work at nine. |
| Termino de trabajar a las seis. | I finish work at six. |
| Vuelvo a casa a las siete. | I get home at seven. |
| Ceno sobre las diez. → Ceno como a las diez. (MX) | I have dinner around ten. |
| ¿Qué haces por la tarde? | What do you do in the afternoon? |
| Voy a clase de música. | I go to music class. |
| Me lavo los dientes. | I brush my teeth. |
| Me acuesto a las once, más o menos. | I go to bed around eleven. |
| Todos los días salgo con mi perro. | Every day I go out with my dog. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Present singular of regular verbs (desayunar, comer, terminar, cenar, leer)
2. Irregular present singular: ir (voy, vas, va); e→ie empezar; o→ue volver, acostarse; -g- hacer (hago), salir (salgo)
3. Reflexive verbs and pronouns, singular: me, te, se (levantarse, ducharse, lavarse, acostarse)
4. Pronunciation: stress in present-tense singular forms

**Vocabulary themes:** Daily actions (get up, shower, have breakfast, start work, have lunch, finish, go home, have dinner, read, go to bed); times of day.

**Settings and situations:** A cut-away building at 8 a.m. showing what each neighbour is doing; a night-worker's day; a nephew describing his aunt's day; cartoons by Forges; the timetable of the average Spaniard (late dinner, mid-morning snack at the bar).

**Spain vs Mexico:** Spanish meal times in the book (lunch 2–3 pm, dinner 9–10 pm) are close to Mexico's comida/cena pattern, but the snack break "tomar algo en el bar" is a Spain custom.

## Lección 12: El fin de semana

**Pages:** Student book pp. 130–139

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about what you do at the weekend, alone and with others
- Ask a couple or a group what they do
- Say how often you do things (always, usually, often, sometimes, never)
- Talk about housework and going out

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué haces los fines de semana? | What do you do at weekends? |
| ¿Qué hacéis los fines de semana? → ¿Qué hacen los fines de semana? (MX) | What do you (all) do at weekends? |
| Los sábados nos levantamos tarde. | On Saturdays we get up late. |
| Hacemos la compra. → Hacemos el súper. (MX) | We do the grocery shopping. |
| Limpiamos la casa. | We clean the house. |
| Cenamos fuera. | We eat out in the evening. |
| Luego vamos de copas. → Luego vamos por unos tragos. (MX) | Then we go out for drinks. |
| Voy al campo muchos fines de semana. | I go to the countryside a lot of weekends. |
| Montamos en bicicleta. → Andamos en bici. (MX) | We go cycling. |
| Siempre… / Normalmente… | Always… / Usually… |
| Voy al cine a menudo. → Voy al cine seguido. (MX) | I often go to the cinema. |
| A veces como con mi familia. | Sometimes I have lunch with my family. |

| Nos acostamos pronto. → Nos acostamos temprano. (MX) | We go to bed early. |
| ¿Y vosotros? → ¿Y ustedes? (MX) | And you (all)? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Present singular and plural of regular verbs
2. Irregular present, full paradigm: ir, volver, acostarse, hacer, salir
3. Reflexive pronouns, plural: nos, os, se
4. Frequency adverbs: siempre, normalmente, a menudo, a veces, nunca (and no… nunca)
5. Pronunciation: diphthongs ai/ei; stress in plural present forms

**Vocabulary themes:** Weekend activities (shopping, concerts, laundry, cleaning, walking, cycling, cooking, countryside, mountains, skiing, eating out, groceries, going for drinks, exhibitions, exercise); TV habits.

**Settings and situations:** A couple's and a divorced doctor's weekends; two couples comparing country and city weekends; imagining flatmates' weekends; TV addiction in Europe; a weekend in the life of Mónica and Enrique.

## Lección 13: El trabajo

**Pages:** Student book pp. 140–149

**What the learner can do**

- Say what you do at work and what your conditions are (hours, days off, holidays)
- Say what you like most and least about your job
- Say how you get to work or class and how long it takes
- Say how often you do things (once a day, twice a week, every three days)
- Run a short survey interview

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué haces en tu trabajo? | What do you do in your job? |
| Atiendo a los clientes. | I deal with customers. |
| ¿Cuántas horas trabajas al día? | How many hours a day do you work? |
| ¿Qué horario tienes? | What are your hours? |
| De nueve a tres. | From nine to three. |
| ¿Trabajas los fines de semana? | Do you work weekends? |
| Lo que más me gusta es hablar con la gente. | What I like most is talking to people. |
| Lo que menos, que gano poco. | What I like least is that I earn very little. |
| Estoy en paro. → Estoy desempleado. / No tengo trabajo. (MX) | I'm out of work. |
| ¿Cómo vas al trabajo? | How do you get to work? |
| En coche. / En metro. / Andando. → En carro. / En metro. / Caminando. (MX) | By car. / By metro. / On foot. |
| ¿Cuánto tardas en llegar? | How long does it take you to get there? |
| Unos veinte minutos. | About twenty minutes. |
| Cojo el metro dos veces al día. → Tomo el metro dos veces al día. (MX) | I take the metro twice a day. |
| Una vez a la semana. | Once a week. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Quantifiers mucho, bastante, poco after a verb (trabajo mucho y gano poco)
2. Question word ¿cómo? for manner (¿cómo vienes a clase?)
3. Present of venir; ir vs venir
4. Frequency expressions: una vez al día, dos veces por semana / a la semana, cada tres días
5. Prepositions de… a, desde… hasta, en (en moto), a (a la semana), por (por semana)
6. Pronunciation: intonation

**Vocabulary themes:** Jobs (taxi driver, photographer, teacher, flight attendant, hairdresser, musician, businesswoman, civil servant); work conditions (hours, days off, holidays, salary); transport (metro, bus, train, on foot, plane, car, bike, motorbike); frequency.

**Settings and situations:** People describing their jobs; how classmates get to class; a radio survey about working conditions; a job-satisfaction quiz; travelling in Peru (buses, the Panamericana, river boats, the Andes railway).

**Spain vs Mexico:** Spain-only transport and work words: coger el autobús/metro → tomar (MX; coger is vulgar in Mexico); coche → carro (MX, coche is understood); andando → caminando (MX); en paro → desempleado (MX); azafata → sobrecargo / aeromoza (MX); conducir → manejar (MX).

## Lección 14: ¿Sabes nadar?

**Pages:** Student book pp. 150–159

**What the learner can do**

- Say what you can and can't do (skills)
- Say how well you do something
- Say what and whom you know (saber vs conocer)
- Say you don't know something
- Give an opinion, agree, disagree and add a counter-argument

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Sabes nadar? | Can you swim? |
| Sí, ¿y tú? — Yo también. / Yo no. | Yes, can you? — Me too. / I can't. |
| Sé tocar la guitarra. | I can play the guitar. |
| No sé conducir. → No sé manejar. (MX) | I can't drive. |
| ¿Cocinas bien? | Are you a good cook? |
| Regular. / Así, así. | So-so. |
| Ni bien ni mal. | Not good, not bad. |
| Sí, bastante bien. | Yes, pretty well. |
| ¿Conoces Oaxaca? | Have you been to Oaxaca? |
| No conozco a tu hermana. | I haven't met your sister. |
| No sé qué día es hoy. | I don't know what day it is. |
| Yo creo que hablar idiomas es muy útil. | I think speaking languages is very useful. |
| Pues yo creo que es difícil. | Well, I think it's hard. |
| Sí, pero también es… | Yes, but it's also… |
| Es muy interesante. / Es aburrido. | It's very interesting. / It's boring. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Saber + infinitive (ability) and saber + noun (knowledge)
2. Conocer + noun (places, people with a), and saber vs conocer
3. También, tampoco, sí, no (review, now for abilities)
4. Verb + bien / mal / regular / así, así
5. Creer (yo creo que…)
6. Ser + adjective to rate actions (es útil, es necesario, es aburrido)
7. Pronunciation: intonation of statements and questions

**Vocabulary themes:** Skills (swimming, singing, cards, chess, driving, basketball, skiing, painting, playing an instrument); the internet and websites; rating adjectives (interesting, important, good, fun, difficult, necessary, bad, boring, easy, useful); Latin American Nobel Prize winners.

**Settings and situations:** A cartoon about playing the guitar; finding out what a classmate can do; arguing about whether learning languages is useful; Latin American Nobel laureates (Mistral, Asturias, Neruda, García Márquez, Vargas Llosa, Menchú); a ten-question interview.

## Lección opcional: ¿Qué hiciste ayer?

**Pages:** Student book pp. 160–169

**What the learner can do**

- Say what you did yesterday, in order and with times
- Ask someone what they did
- Tell a very short story about meeting someone

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué hiciste ayer? | What did you do yesterday? |
| ¿Saliste? | Did you go out? |
| No, me quedé en casa. | No, I stayed home. |
| Vimos una película. | We watched a film. |
| Quedé con Gloria. → Me vi con Gloria. (MX) | I met up with Gloria. |
| Fuimos a dar una vuelta. | We went for a walk. |
| Tomamos algo. | We had a drink. |
| Volví a casa un poco tarde. | I got home a bit late. |
| Ayer me levanté a las ocho. | Yesterday I got up at eight. |
| Estuve en el parque. | I was at the park. |
| A las seis fui al gimnasio. | At six I went to the gym. |
| Hice un examen. | I took an exam. |
| Me acosté muy pronto. → Me acosté muy temprano. (MX) | I went to bed very early. |
| Ayer conocí a… | Yesterday I met… (for the first time) |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Pretérito indefinido (simple past) of regular -ar, -er, -ir verbs
2. Irregular preterites hacer, venir, ir/ser, estar
3. Pronunciation: the stressed syllable in preterite forms (tomé, volvió)

**Vocabulary themes:** Yesterday's activities (stayed home, watched TV, went out, met friends, went for a walk, had a drink, went to the gym, did homework); times of day.

**Settings and situations:** Two classmates comparing yesterday evening; guessing what someone did from the times; a picture story of an old lady's busy day; meeting her and going to a salsa concert; the origin of the name Argentina.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Lucky for Mexico: the only past tense in the book is the pretérito indefinido, which is also the everyday past in Mexico. Spain often uses the perfect (he comido) for "today" events; Mexico uses the preterite (comí), so the book's choice suits Afuera. Quedar con alguien (to arrange to meet) is Spain-only; Mexico says quedar de verse con / verse con.

## Order and pacing

- **Lessons 1–2 (pp. 10–27):** names, spelling, nationalities, languages, numbers to 20. Only singular present forms of llamarse, ser and hablar. A short list of bar words (café con leche, cerveza, ¿cuánto es?) appears in Lesson 2 as vocabulary, not as a conversation.
- **Lessons 3–5 (pp. 28–59):** jobs, addresses and phone numbers, tú vs usted, family and describing people. The present tense is built up person by person: singular first (Lessons 2–4), then the 3rd person plural (Lesson 5). Nosotros forms appear inside dialogues (tenemos) before they are taught. Possessives mi/tu/su only.
- **Lesson 6 (pp. 64–73):** the first service encounter is **buying something in a shop** (asking for it, asking to see it, the price), at about a third of the way through. Hay and demonstratives arrive here.
- **Lessons 7–8 (pp. 74–93):** ser vs estar for places, hay vs está, prepositions of place. Describing a city and a home.
- **Lesson 9 (pp. 94–103):** gustar and encantar with indirect object pronouns, and también/tampoco/sí/no.
- **Lesson 10 (pp. 104–115):** neighbourhood, telling the time, opening hours, days, months, seasons and weather. The time is taught very late (two-thirds of the way through).
- **Lessons 11–12 (pp. 120–139):** the full present tense with the main irregular verbs (ir, empezar, volver, acostarse, hacer, salir) and reflexive verbs, singular then plural. Daily routine and weekends; frequency adverbs.
- **Lessons 13–14 (pp. 140–159):** work conditions, transport, frequency expressions, venir, saber vs conocer, giving opinions and agreeing/disagreeing.
- **Lección opcional (pp. 160–169):** the **only past tense** in the book, the pretérito indefinido, at p. 160 of 173, and the book marks the lesson as optional. Regular verbs plus hacer, venir, ir/ser, estar.
- **Never taught as a lesson:** ordering in a café or restaurant (no menu, waiter or bill dialogue anywhere; only the Lesson 2 word list), asking for and giving directions, the near future ir a + infinitive, querer/poder/tener que as modals (quería and ¿puedo ver…? appear only as set phrases in the shop), the perfect tense, direct object pronouns (me lo llevo is a set phrase), the imperative (only mira/mire as fixed openers), the imperfect, the conditional.
- **Pace:** slow and even. Each lesson is 10 pages (Lessons 5 and 10 run to 12), with a closing "Recuerda" summary page, two or three pages of extra games ("Materiales complementarios") and a culture page ("Descubre España y América Latina"). Reviews after Lessons 5, 10 and 14 are the natural checkpoints. Free-time talk, likes and routines come long before any past tense, and shopping comes long before any café.

## Spain vs Mexico across the book

- **Vosotros** is taught in every present-tense table, in gustar (os), in reflexives (os) and in the preterite (tomasteis). Mexico uses **ustedes** for every plural "you", with 3rd-person plural verbs: ¿Qué hacéis? → ¿Qué hacen?; os gusta → les gusta; os acostáis → se acuestan.
- **Usted** is used more in Mexico than in Spain, including with people you know a little. The book says so itself (Lesson 4 culture page).
- **Pronunciation:** the book teaches /θ/ for z, ce, ci (Lesson 7). Mexico has seseo: all of these sound like s.
- **Vale** (OK) → está bien, va, sale (MX).
- **Coger** (take: a bus, the metro) → **tomar** (MX). In Mexico coger is vulgar; Afuera should never model it and should recast it gently if a learner says it.
- **Pronto** for "early" (me acuesto pronto) → **temprano** (MX). In Mexico pronto means "soon/quickly".
- **Quedar con alguien** → verse con / quedar de verse con (MX).
- **Sobre las diez** (at about ten) → como a las diez / a eso de las diez (MX).
- **Son las cinco menos diez** → son diez para las cinco / faltan diez para las cinco (MX).
- **A menudo** → seguido (MX, colloquial; a menudo is understood but sounds bookish).
- **Ir de copas** → ir por unos tragos / salir a tomar algo (MX).
- **Hacer la compra** → hacer el súper (MX).
- **Montar en bicicleta** → andar en bici (MX).
- **Estar en paro** → estar desempleado / no tener trabajo (MX).
- **Home:** piso → departamento; salón → sala; dormitorio / habitación (bedroom) → recámara; frigorífico → refrigerador / refri; ascensor → elevador; alquiler → renta; chalé → casa.
- **Things:** ordenador → computadora; móvil → celular; gafas → lentes; bolígrafo → pluma; vaqueros → jeans / pantalones de mezclilla; bocadillo → torta (closest Mexican equivalent) or sándwich.
- **People and jobs:** camarero → mesero; azafata → sobrecargo / aeromoza; instituto (secondary school) → prepa / secundaria.
- **Colours:** marrón → café (ojos cafés).
- **Getting around:** coche → carro (coche is understood); conducir → manejar; andando → caminando; aparcamiento → estacionamiento.
- **City:** barrio → colonia (for a city neighbourhood).
- **Money and shops:** euros and céntimos → pesos and centavos; estanco → no equivalent.
- **Daily life:** ducharse → bañarse is the everyday Mexican word; Spain's long midday shop closure and mid-morning bar snack are Spain customs.
- **Not Spain-only, but less natural in Mexico** (left unmarked in the tables): lengua → idioma is the usual word for a language; sitio preferido → lugar favorito; papá/mamá are the everyday words for parents; regresar is as common as volver.
- **Lucky overlaps:** the preterite (Mexico's everyday past) is the book's only past tense; comer for the main midday meal and cenar for the evening meal match Mexico.
