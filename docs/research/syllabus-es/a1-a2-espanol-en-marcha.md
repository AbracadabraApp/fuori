# Nuevo Español en marcha Básico (A1–A2): syllabus summary

A summary of the book's progression, for Afuera's own syllabus. *Nuevo Español en marcha Básico* (nueva edición) is published by SGEL (Spain; authors Francisca Castro Viúdez, Pilar Díaz Ballesteros, Ignacio Rodero Díez and Carmen Sardinero Francos). It puts levels A1 and A2 of the Common European Framework in one volume: a short starter section (*Antes de empezar*) and 18 units of 10 pages each. Every unit has three teaching sections (A, B, C), a culture section (D, *Comunicación y cultura*) and a self-assessment page (*Autoevaluación*). Written in our own words; the Spanish phrases are ordinary everyday chunks, not copied dialogue. Page numbers are the printed student-book pages. In the CSV, unit 0 = *Antes de empezar*.

The book does not label units A1 or A2. The level given for each unit is our reading of its content against the usual A1/A2 split: Units 1–9 are A1 (Unit 8 brings in the first past tense and is the bridge), Units 10–18 are A2.

The book teaches the Spanish of Spain, with Latin American texts mixed in. Where a form in the book is one a Mexican speaker wouldn't use, the frames table gives the book form, then an arrow and the Mexican form, e.g. "¿Os gusta bailar? → ¿Les gusta bailar? (MX)". The CSV uses the Mexican form. Each unit also notes Spain-only vocabulary that isn't in a frame. The section "Spain vs Mexico across the book" at the end collects them.

## Antes de empezar

**Pages:** Student book pp. 8–14  
**Level:** A1

**What the learner can do**

- Say your name and where you are from on the first day of class
- Spell a word letter by letter and ask how something is written
- Ask what a word means and how to say something in Spanish
- Ask the teacher to repeat
- Name Spanish-speaking countries and recognise their nationalities

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me llamo… ¿Y tú? | My name is… And you? |
| ¿De dónde eres? | Where are you from? |
| Soy brasileño, de Porto Alegre. | I'm Brazilian, from Porto Alegre. |
| ¿Cómo se escribe? | How do you spell it? |
| ¿Con be o con uve? → ¿Con be grande o con ve chica? (MX) | With a B or a V? |
| ¿Qué significa…? | What does … mean? |
| ¿Cómo se dice laptop en español? | How do you say laptop in Spanish? |
| ¿Puede repetir, por favor? | Could you repeat that, please? |
| ¿Así está bien? | Is this right? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Alphabet, letter names and sounds (c/qu/z, g/gu/j, h, ll, ñ, r/rr)
2. Word stress and the written accent (first look)
3. Classroom questions: ¿cómo se escribe / se dice / se pronuncia?, ¿qué significa?

**Vocabulary themes:** Letters and sounds; Spanish words known worldwide (fiesta, tango, paella, siesta); countries of Spain and Latin America and their nationalities.

**Settings and situations:** A teacher asking new students their names; spelling surnames; maps of Spain and Latin America; famous places (Machu Picchu, the Alhambra, Cartagena de Indias).

**Spain vs Mexico:** The book itself says that in Latin America b and v are told apart as *be larga / be corta*; in Mexico people also say *be grande / ve chica*. For laptop the book's answer is *portátil*; in Mexico *laptop* or *computadora portátil* is normal.

## Unidad 1: Saludos

**Pages:** Student book pp. 15–24  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A ¡Encantado! · B ¿A qué te dedicas? · C ¿Cuál es tu número de móvil? · D Comunicación y cultura: tú y usted

**What the learner can do**

- Greet people and introduce someone, informally and formally
- Say your nationality and job and ask other people theirs
- Choose between tú and usted
- Give and ask for a phone number, an email and an address
- Count from 0 to 20
- Give your details at a reception desk or a gym

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¡Hola!, ¿qué tal? | Hi, how's it going? |
| Bien, ¿y tú? | Fine, and you? |
| Mira, esta es Ana, una compañera nueva. | Look, this is Ana, a new classmate. |
| ¡Encantado! / ¡Encantada! | Pleased to meet you! |
| Buenos días, señor Prado, ¿cómo está usted? | Good morning, Mr Prado, how are you? (formal) |
| Le presento a la señora Ruiz. | May I introduce Mrs Ruiz? (formal) |
| Mucho gusto. | Nice to meet you. |
| ¿A qué te dedicas? | What do you do for a living? |
| Soy médica. ¿Y tú? | I'm a doctor. And you? |
| ¿De dónde son ustedes? | Where are you (all) from? |
| Somos argentinos, de Buenos Aires. | We're Argentinian, from Buenos Aires. |
| ¿Dónde vive usted? | Where do you live? (formal) |
| Vivo en la calle Goya, número 87. | I live at 87 Goya Street. |
| ¿Cuál es tu número de móvil? → ¿Cuál es tu número de celular? (MX) | What's your mobile number? |
| ¿Cuál es tu correo electrónico? | What's your email? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Gender of nationality adjectives (-o/-a; consonant + a; -e and -í invariable)
2. Gender of job nouns (camarero/camarera, estudiante, economista)
3. Present of regular -ar, -er, -ir verbs (trabajar, comer, vivir), all persons
4. Present of ser and tener
5. Tú vs usted
6. Question words ¿qué?, ¿dónde?, ¿cómo?, ¿cuál?
7. Numbers 0–20
8. Pronunciation: question intonation

**Vocabulary themes:** Greetings; nationalities; jobs (hairdresser, taxi driver, waiter, lawyer, IT worker); personal details on a form (name, two surnames, address, floor and door, mobile, email).

**Settings and situations:** A classroom, a hotel reception, an office, a café; signing up at a gym; famous Hispanic people (Benicio del Toro, Jennifer López, Shakira); how people greet in different countries, including vos in Argentina.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Móvil → celular (MX). Camarero → mesero (MX). The book's vosotros forms (trabajáis, sois, tenéis) are not used in Mexico: ustedes trabajan, son, tienen. Spanish addresses with floor and door (tercero izquierda) are Spain; Mexican addresses give the street, number, interior number and colonia.

## Unidad 2: Familias

**Pages:** Student book pp. 25–34  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A ¿Estás casado? · B ¿Dónde están mis gafas? · C ¿Qué hora es? · D Comunicación y cultura: la familia hispana, celebraciones

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about your family: names, ages, jobs, marital status
- Say where things are in a room
- Say who things belong to
- Ask and tell the time; say when meals are and when shops and banks open
- Count to 5,000

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Estás casado? — No, estoy soltero. | Are you married? — No, I'm single. |
| ¿Tienes hijos? | Do you have children? |
| Tengo un hermano y una hermana. | I have a brother and a sister. |
| Mi padre se llama Manuel y tiene 58 años. → Mi papá se llama Manuel y tiene 58 años. (MX) | My father's name is Manuel and he's 58. |
| Estos son mis padres. → Estos son mis papás. (MX) | These are my parents. |
| ¿Dónde están mis gafas? → ¿Dónde están mis lentes? (MX) | Where are my glasses? |
| Están encima de la mesa. | They're on the table. |
| El móvil está al lado del ordenador. → El celular está al lado de la computadora. (MX) | The phone is next to the computer. |
| ¿Esta es tu chaqueta? → ¿Esta es tu chamarra? (MX) | Is this your jacket? |
| ¿Qué hora es? | What time is it? |
| Son las tres y media. | It's half past three. |
| Son las dos menos cuarto. → Es cuarto para las dos. (MX) | It's a quarter to two. |
| ¿A qué hora coméis en tu país? → ¿A qué hora comen en tu país? (MX) | What time do you have lunch in your country? |
| Los bancos abren por la mañana. | Banks open in the morning. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Plural of nouns (-s, -es)
2. Present of estar; estar for marital status and location
3. Prepositions and adverbs of place: encima de, debajo de, al lado de, delante de, detrás de, entre, a la derecha / izquierda de; contractions al, del
4. Possessive adjectives mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro (singular and plural)
5. Demonstrative pronouns este, esta, estos, estas (introducing people)
6. Telling the time (es la una / son las…, y cuarto, y media, menos cuarto)
7. Numbers 21–5,000
8. Pronunciation: word stress

**Vocabulary themes:** Family members; marital status; objects in a room (umbrella, slippers, laptop, picture, sofa, lamp, glasses, phone); meals (desayunar, comer, cenar); opening and closing; numbers.

**Settings and situations:** A woman in Fuenlabrada and a medical student in Mérida describe their families; a messy bedroom; world opening hours and meal times; train departures board; the Hispanic family, first communions and quinceañeras; two surnames.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Gafas → lentes (MX). Ordenador → computadora (MX). Chaqueta → chamarra (MX, casual jacket) or saco (suit jacket). Padre/madre are fine but papá/mamá are the everyday words in Mexico (the book's Luis already says *mis papás*). Time: menos cuarto → cuarto para (MX).

## Unidad 3: El trabajo

**Pages:** Student book pp. 35–44  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A Rosa se levanta a las siete · B ¿Estudias o trabajas? · C ¿Qué desayunas? · D Comunicación y cultura: hábitos y horarios de los españoles

**What the learner can do**

- Describe your daily routine with times
- Talk about your job: where you work, your hours, your days off, how you get there
- Say what people in different jobs do
- Order breakfast in a café and say what you usually have

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿A qué hora te levantas? | What time do you get up? |
| Me levanto a las siete y me ducho. → Me levanto a las siete y me baño. (MX) | I get up at seven and shower. |
| Los domingos me levanto muy tarde. | On Sundays I get up really late. |
| Me acuesto pronto. → Me acuesto temprano. (MX) | I go to bed early. |
| Salgo de casa a las ocho. | I leave home at eight. |
| Voy al trabajo en autobús. → Voy al trabajo en camión. (MX) | I go to work by bus. |
| Trabajo de ocho a tres. | I work from eight to three. |
| ¿Qué horario tienes? | What are your hours? |
| ¿Tienes algún día libre? — Sí, los domingos. | Do you get a day off? — Yes, Sundays. |
| ¿Qué desayunas normalmente? | What do you usually have for breakfast? |
| Buenos días, ¿qué desean? | Good morning, what would you like? (to a group) |
| Un café con leche y una tostada, por favor. → Un café con leche y pan tostado, por favor. (MX) | A coffee with milk and some toast, please. |
| Yo solo quiero un zumo de naranja. → Yo solo quiero un jugo de naranja. (MX) | I just want an orange juice. |
| Me gusta el café solo y sin azúcar. → Me gusta el café negro y sin azúcar. (MX) | I like my coffee black, no sugar. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Reflexive verbs in the present (levantarse, acostarse, ducharse)
2. Irregular present: empezar (e→ie), volver (o→ue), ir, salir (salgo)
3. Prepositions of time: a las…, de la mañana, por la tarde, de… a, desde… hasta
4. Quantifiers todos, la mayoría, muchos, algunos
5. Prepositions con / sin / de with food (café con leche, zumo de naranja)
6. Days of the week
7. Pronunciation: the /g/ sound (g, gu)

**Vocabulary themes:** Daily actions; days of the week; jobs and workplaces (nurse, cashier, shop assistant, cook, flight attendant, firefighter); breakfast foods and drinks; a café menu with breakfast and afternoon-snack deals.

**Settings and situations:** A Havana ballet school's timetable; a sound engineer in Valencia and a firefighter in Toledo; ordering breakfast at a café counter; Spanish meal times and shop hours; a woman describing life in Buenos Aires.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Ducharse → bañarse (MX). Pronto (early) → temprano (MX). Zumo → jugo (MX). Tostada in Spain is toast; in Mexico a *tostada* is a crisp fried tortilla, so ask for *pan tostado*. Café solo → café negro / americano (MX). Autobús is understood; in Mexico City the city bus is usually *camión*. Bollo / bollería → pan dulce (MX).

## Unidad 4: La casa

**Pages:** Student book pp. 45–54  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A ¿Dónde vives? · B Interiores · C Visita a Córdoba · D Comunicación y cultura: tipos de vivienda en España

**What the learner can do**

- Describe your home: type, floors, rooms, what it has
- Say which floor you live on
- Say what there is in a room and where things are (hay vs está)
- Book a hotel room by phone: type of room, nights, price, payment
- Write a postcard or email describing where you are staying

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Dónde vives? — En un piso en el centro. → ¿Dónde vives? — En un departamento en el centro. (MX) | Where do you live? — In a flat in the centre. |
| ¿En qué piso vives? — En el tercero. → ¿En qué piso vives? — En el tercer piso. (MX) | Which floor do you live on? — The third. |
| ¿Cuántos dormitorios tiene? → ¿Cuántas recámaras tiene? (MX) | How many bedrooms does it have? |
| Mi casa es pequeña, pero tiene mucha luz. | My place is small, but it gets lots of light. |
| ¿Tenéis garaje? — No, aparcamos en la calle. → ¿Tienen cochera? — No, nos estacionamos en la calle. (MX) | Do you have a garage? — No, we park on the street. |
| En el salón hay dos sillones. → En la sala hay dos sillones. (MX) | There are two armchairs in the living room. |
| ¿Hay leche en la nevera? → ¿Hay leche en el refri? (MX) | Is there any milk in the fridge? |
| Perdone, ¿hay un supermercado cerca de aquí? | Excuse me, is there a supermarket near here? |
| Las llaves están en la mesa de la cocina. | The keys are on the kitchen table. |
| ¿Hay habitaciones libres para el fin de semana? | Do you have rooms free for the weekend? |
| Una doble, por favor. ¿Qué precio tiene? | A double, please. How much is it? |
| ¿Para cuántas noches? — Para dos. | For how many nights? — Two. |
| Hágame la reserva, por favor. → ¿Me hace la reservación, por favor? (MX) | Please book it for me. |
| ¿Admiten tarjetas de crédito? → ¿Aceptan tarjeta de crédito? (MX) | Do you take credit cards? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Ordinal numbers 1st–10th; primer, tercer before a masculine noun
2. Definite and indefinite articles, singular and plural, and when to use each
3. Hay + un/una/unos/unas, numbers, mucho/poco + noun
4. Definite article + noun + está(n) for location; está + name (¿Está Juan?)
5. Pronunciation: the /k/ sound (c, qu)

**Vocabulary themes:** Types of home (flat, terraced house, studio, loft, penthouse); rooms and floors; furniture and appliances (fridge, washing machine, microwave, sink, shower, wardrobe, mirror); hotel facilities (pool, gym, parking, bike hire, spa); household verbs.

**Settings and situations:** A family living in a terraced house outside Madrid; a friend's new flat; ads for homes for sale; a phone booking at the Córdoba parador; the Córdoba patio festival; house styles in Andalucía, the north, the cities and the coast; a holiday email.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Piso → departamento (MX); salón → sala (MX); dormitorio → recámara (MX); nevera / frigorífico → refrigerador / refri (MX); garaje → cochera (MX, at home); aparcar → estacionarse (MX); ascensor → elevador (MX); chalé adosado has no single Mexican word (casa en condominio, casa dúplex). Reserva → reservación (MX) for hotels and restaurants. Admitir tarjetas → aceptar tarjeta (MX). Floors: Mexico also counts a planta baja, so the first floor is the one above it, as in Spain.

## Unidad 5: Comer

**Pages:** Student book pp. 55–64  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A Comer fuera de casa · B ¿Te gusta el cine? · C Receta del Caribe · D Comunicación y cultura: comidas de España e Hispanoamérica

**What the learner can do**

- Order a set lunch in a restaurant: first course, main, drink, dessert
- Ask the waiter for something you need and for the bill
- Say what you like and dislike and how much; agree and disagree
- Understand and give simple instructions, such as a recipe
- Name typical dishes from Spain and Latin America

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué van a tomar de primero? | What will you have for your first course? |
| De primero quiero una sopa. | For my first course I'd like soup. |
| ¿Y de segundo? — Pollo a la plancha. | And for your main? — Grilled chicken. |
| ¿Y para beber? — Agua mineral, por favor. | And to drink? — Mineral water, please. |
| ¿Y de postre? — Un helado de vainilla. | And for dessert? — Vanilla ice cream. |
| ¿Me trae una servilleta, por favor? | Could you bring me a napkin, please? |
| ¿Me trae la cuenta, por favor? — Sí, ahora mismo. | Could I have the bill, please? — Right away. |
| ¿Te gusta el cine? | Do you like films? |
| Me gustan las películas de terror. | I like horror films. |
| Me encanta viajar. | I love travelling. |
| No me gusta nada ir de compras. | I don't like shopping at all. |
| A mí también. / A mí tampoco. | Me too. / Me neither. |
| ¿Os gusta bailar? → ¿Les gusta bailar? (MX) | Do you (all) like dancing? |
| Corta los plátanos y mézclalos con la leche. | Slice the bananas and mix them with the milk. |
| Habla más bajo, por favor. | Talk more quietly, please. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Gustar and encantar with me, te, le, nos, os, les (gusta + singular or infinitive, gustan + plural)
2. Degrees of liking (me encanta, me gusta mucho / bastante, no me gusta nada); también / tampoco / sí / no
3. Querer in the present (quiero is normal when ordering in a restaurant)
4. Regular affirmative imperative, tú and usted (corta / corte, come / coma, abre / abra)
5. Pronunciation: b and v sound the same

**Vocabulary themes:** Spanish dishes (gazpacho, tortilla de patatas, paella); a set menu (primeros, segundos, postres, bebidas); table items (cup, fork, spoon, glass, napkin, tablecloth, jug); film genres; free-time activities; recipe verbs and ingredients; foods that came from the Americas.

**Settings and situations:** A set lunch at a mesón with a waiter and two diners; eating out in Spain; a dating-style website of people's hobbies; a Caribbean banana smoothie; restaurant ads; dishes by region (guacamole from Mexico, ceviche, carne asada, fabada).

**Spain vs Mexico:** Camarero → mesero (MX). Os gusta → les gusta (MX). Patatas → papas (MX); gambas → camarones (MX); tarta → pastel (MX); zumo → jugo (MX); natillas are not a Mexican dessert (flan is). Mexico's equivalent of the menú del día is the *comida corrida* (soup, rice or pasta, a main, a small dessert and agua fresca), served at midday. Montar en bicicleta → andar en bici (MX); andar (walking for pleasure) → caminar (MX).

## Unidad 6: El barrio

**Pages:** Student book pp. 65–74  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A ¿Cómo se va a Goya? · B Cierra la ventana, por favor · C Mi barrio es tranquilo · D Comunicación y cultura: ciudades mexicanas

**What the learner can do**

- Ask how to get somewhere by metro, which ticket to buy and how much it costs
- Give metro directions (take line 8, change to line 4, it's the third stop)
- Give instructions and ask for favours, directly or politely (¿puedes…?)
- Describe your neighbourhood: quiet or noisy, near or far, well connected
- Write a short description of a city

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Perdone, ¿cómo se va de aquí a Goya? | Excuse me, how do I get from here to Goya? |
| Tome la línea 8 hasta Mar de Cristal. | Take line 8 to Mar de Cristal. |
| Cambie a la línea 4. Es la tercera parada. → Transborde a la línea 4. Es la tercera estación. (MX) | Change to line 4. It's the third stop. |
| ¿Qué billete tenemos que comprar? → ¿Qué boleto tenemos que comprar? (MX) | Which ticket do we need to buy? |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta? | How much is it? |
| ¿Puede darme un plano del metro? → ¿Me puede dar un mapa del metro? (MX) | Could you give me a metro map? |
| Cierra la ventana, por favor, tengo frío. | Close the window, please, I'm cold. |
| ¿Puedes venir un momento? | Can you come here a moment? |
| Pásame la sal. | Pass me the salt. |
| ¿Se puede? — Sí, pase y siéntese. | May I come in? — Yes, come in and sit down. |
| Mi barrio es tranquilo y está cerca del centro. → Mi colonia es tranquila y está cerca del centro. (MX) | My neighbourhood is quiet and close to the centre. |
| Está bien comunicado. | It has good transport links. |
| Esa calle es muy ruidosa. | That street is very noisy. |
| El banco está enfrente de mi oficina. | The bank is opposite my office. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Asking the way formally and informally (perdone / perdona, ¿puede(s) decirme cómo se va a…?)
2. Irregular affirmative imperatives: haz, pon, ven, sigue, tuerce, cierra, siéntate, di (and usted forms)
3. Asking favours: ¿puede(s) + infinitive? as the softer alternative to the imperative
4. Ser vs estar: ser for what something is like, estar for where it is and for open / closed, bien / mal
5. Connectors y, pero, porque
6. Pronunciation: r and rr

**Vocabulary themes:** Metro words (line, station, stop, direction, ticket, tourist pass, map); transport; adjectives for places (quiet, noisy, fast, slow, cheap, central, narrow); opposites; requests (lend, switch on / off, open / close).

**Settings and situations:** Two tourists at Madrid airport asking how to get to their hotel by metro; the Madrid metro and its little libraries; an office boss and his employee; a friend's new flat on a noisy street; La Condesa in Mexico City; San Miguel de Allende; Old Havana and El Vedado.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Billete → boleto (MX). Plano (city or metro map) → mapa (MX). Barrio → colonia (MX) for a city neighbourhood; the book itself uses *colonia* for La Condesa. In the Mexico City metro you *transbordas* or *cambias de línea*; the stops are *estaciones*. Torcer (turn) → dar vuelta (MX): tuerce a la derecha → da vuelta a la derecha. Vale → sale / va / está bien (MX). Useful for Afuera: this unit's culture pages are Mexican (La Condesa, San Miguel de Allende).

## Unidad 7: Salir con los amigos

**Pages:** Student book pp. 75–84  
**Level:** A1  
**Sections:** A ¿Dónde quedamos? · B ¿Qué estás haciendo? · C ¿Cómo es? · D Comunicación y cultura: el tiempo libre de los jóvenes

**What the learner can do**

- Suggest a plan, arrange a time and a place to meet
- Accept, or say no and suggest something else
- Make a phone call: ask for someone, take or leave a message
- Say what someone is doing right now
- Describe what someone looks like and what they are like
- React with short exclamations

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Vamos al centro esta tarde? | Shall we go into town this afternoon? |
| ¿Por qué no vienes con nosotros? | Why don't you come with us? |
| ¿Dónde quedamos? → ¿Dónde nos vemos? (MX) | Where shall we meet? |
| ¿A las cinco en la puerta del metro? | Five o'clock at the metro entrance? |
| No, mejor a las seis. ¿Te parece bien? | No, six is better. Is that OK with you? |
| Vale. / De acuerdo. → Va. / Sale. / Está bien. (MX) | OK. |
| Lo siento, no puedo, tengo que trabajar. | Sorry, I can't, I have to work. |
| Diga. / ¿Dígame? → ¿Bueno? (MX) | Hello? (answering the phone) |
| ¿Puedo hablar con el señor Álvarez? | Could I speak to Mr Álvarez? |
| No está. ¿Quiere dejarle un recado? | He's not in. Would you like to leave a message? |
| Sí, dígale que lo llamo mañana. | Yes, tell him I'll call him tomorrow. |
| ¿Qué estás haciendo? — Estoy viendo una serie. | What are you doing? — I'm watching a series. |
| Es alto, tiene el pelo corto y lleva gafas. → Es alto, tiene el pelo corto y usa lentes. (MX) | He's tall, has short hair and wears glasses. |
| Es muy simpática y habladora. | She's really nice and chatty. |
| ¡Qué bien! / ¡Qué va! | Great! / No way! |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Estar + gerund for actions in progress; regular and irregular gerunds (leyendo, durmiendo)
2. Estar + gerund with reflexive pronouns, before or after (me estoy lavando / estoy lavándome)
3. Physical description: ser + adjective, tener + hair / eyes, llevar + glasses, beard
4. Character adjectives and their opposites
5. Pronunciation: exclamation intonation

**Vocabulary themes:** Going out (cinema, a drink, dinner, dancing, basketball); inviting, accepting, declining; phone messages; beach activities; hair, eyes, beard, moustache, glasses; character (mean / generous, rude / polite, quiet / chatty, serious / fun).

**Settings and situations:** Two friends planning an afternoon; an invitation to lunch in the country turned down for a mother's birthday; meeting at Barcelona's beach and squares; a call to an estate agency; a busy beach scene; the song *Guantanamera*; young people's Saturday nights in Spain, Peru and Costa Rica.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Quedar (to arrange to meet) → verse / quedar de verse (MX): ¿Dónde quedamos? → ¿Dónde nos vemos? Vale → va, sale, está bien (MX). Answering the phone: ¿Diga? / ¿Dígame? → ¿Bueno? (MX). Gafas → lentes; llevar gafas → usar lentes (MX). Rubio is understood; *güero* is the everyday Mexican word for fair-haired or fair-skinned. Os → les (¿Qué tal vosotros? → ¿Qué tal ustedes?).

## Unidad 8: De vacaciones

**Pages:** Student book pp. 85–94  
**Level:** A1 (first past tense; bridges to A2)  
**Sections:** A Por favor, ¿para ir a la catedral? · B ¿Qué hizo Rosa ayer? · C ¿Qué tiempo hace hoy? · D Comunicación y cultura: de vacaciones por España

**What the learner can do**

- Ask the way on foot and follow or give directions with a map
- Say which shop sells what (pharmacy, post office, market)
- Say what you did yesterday, last weekend or on holiday
- Talk about the weather today and yesterday, months and seasons
- Write about your last holiday

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Perdone, ¿puede decirme cómo se va a la plaza de Armas? | Excuse me, can you tell me how to get to the main square? |
| Por favor, ¿para ir a la catedral? | Excuse me, which way to the cathedral? |

| Gire a la izquierda. → Dé vuelta a la izquierda. (MX) | Turn left. |
| Tome la tercera calle a la derecha. | Take the third street on the right. |
| ¿Dónde se compran los sellos? — En el estanco. → ¿Dónde se compran timbres? — En el correo. (MX) | Where do you buy stamps? — At the post office. |

1. Directions with formal and informal imperatives: sigue / siga, toma / tome, gira / gire
2. Pretérito indefinido of regular -ar, -er, -ir verbs
3. Irregular indefinido of ir / ser and estar (and hizo / hizo frío as set forms)
4. Weather: hace + noun, está nublado, llueve / nieva, and their past forms
5. Months and seasons
6. Pronunciation: stress that changes meaning (hablo / habló)

**Spain vs Mexico:** Seguir recto → seguir derecho (MX). Girar / torcer → dar vuelta (MX); girar is also understood. Mexicans count streets in *cuadras* (a dos cuadras), not *manzanas*. Sellos → timbres (MX), bought at the post office (*el correo*); there is no estanco. Coger el autobús → tomar el camión (MX). ## Unidad 12: Costumbres

**Pages:** Student book pp. 125–134  
**Level:** A2  
**Sections:** A El cumpleaños de mi abuela · B ¿Cómo te ha ido hoy? · C Costumbres · D Comunicación y cultura: tradiciones, experiencias

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about your extended family and what each person is like
- Tell character (ser) from mood (estar)
- Say what you have done today or this week (Spain usage)
- Ask about and tell experiences (have you ever…?)
- Say what you must and mustn't do in a place or culture

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Mi abuela cumple ochenta años. | My grandmother is turning eighty. |
| No me apetece nada ir. → No tengo nada de ganas de ir. (MX) | I really don't feel like going. |
| Es muy cariñosa y divertida. | She's very affectionate and fun. |
| Mi prima es muy maja. → Mi prima es muy buena onda. (MX) | My cousin is really nice. |
| Hoy está muy serio. | He's very serious today. |
| ¿Cómo te ha ido hoy? → ¿Cómo te fue hoy? (MX) | How did your day go? |
| Hoy he tenido mucho trabajo. → Hoy tuve mucho trabajo. (MX) | I had a lot of work today. |
| He llevado a los niños al colegio. → Llevé a los niños a la escuela. (MX) | I took the kids to school. |
| ¿Has hecho las maletas? — No, todavía no. → ¿Ya hiciste las maletas? — No, todavía no. (MX) | Have you packed? — No, not yet. |
| ¿Has estado alguna vez en Buenos Aires? | Have you ever been to Buenos Aires? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Ser + adjective for character, estar + adjective for mood; adjectives only used with estar (contento, cansado, harto, preocupado)
2. Pretérito perfecto (he, has, ha… + participle); regular and irregular participles (hecho, visto, escrito, vuelto, dicho, abierto, muerto)
3. Perfect with hoy, esta mañana, esta semana; ya / todavía no
4. Experiences: perfect in the question (¿has… alguna vez?), perfect or indefinido in the answer depending on whether a time is given
5. Hay que / no hay que + infinitive; (no) se puede + infinitive
6. Pronunciation: stressed syllable, agudas / llanas / esdrújulas

**Vocabulary themes:** Extended family (cousin, nephew, niece, brother- and sister-in-law, grandchildren); character adjectives (affectionate, selfish, sociable, lazy, shy, generous); housework (washing up, ironing, taking out the rubbish, walking the dog); travel etiquette; wedding traditions around the world.

**Spain vs Mexico:** This is the unit where Spain and Mexico differ most. The book teaches the perfecto for anything that happened today or this week (¿Cómo te ha ido hoy? He tenido mucho trabajo). Mexico uses the indefinido for these (¿Cómo te fue hoy? Tuve mucho trabajo), and with ya / todavía no too (¿Ya comiste? — Todavía no). Mexico keeps the perfecto for experiences up to now (¿Has ido a…?, nunca he estado) and for things that are still going on (he estado muy ocupado). The Oaxaca diary on p. 133 is set in Mexico but written in Spain's usage (*Hoy he llegado a Oaxaca*); a Mexican would write *Hoy llegué a Oaxaca*. Apetecer → tener ganas / antojarse (MX). Majo → buena onda (MX). Colegio → escuela (MX). Aparcar → estacionarse (MX).

**Level:** A2

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Me gustaría vivir en el campo. | I'd like to live in the country. |
| Estoy buscando un piso pequeño en el centro. → Estoy buscando un departamento pequeño en el centro. (MX) | I'm looking for a small flat in the centre. |
| ¿Dónde le gustaría vivir? | Where would you like to live? (formal) |

(very informal).

| Pónganos dos cañas y un vino, por favor. → Nos trae dos cervezas y un vino, por favor. (MX) | Two small beers and a wine, please. |
| ¿Quieren algo de tapa? → ¿Quieren algo de botana? (MX) | Would you like something to snack on? |
| ¿Me cobra, por favor? / La cuenta, por favor. | Can I pay, please? / The bill, please. |
| Deja, hoy invito yo. | Leave it, it's on me today. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Indefinites: alguien / nadie, algo / nada, algún(o) / ningún(o), -a, -os, -as; double negative (no hay nadie)
2. Impersonal se for instructions and recipes (se lavan, se fríe)
3. Requests in a bar: póngame / pónganos, traiga; asking for the bill
4. Mediation: reporting what you saw to someone else (he visto…, hay una…, quizá es…)
5. Pronunciation: diphthongs and hiatus (día, río, país)

**Vocabulary themes:** Second-hand ads (condition, mileage, guarantee, free); fruit and vegetables; weights and quantities; cooking verbs (chop, crush, fry, boil, add, leave to stand); paella ingredients; bar orders (caña, tapa, ración); Mediterranean diet.

**Settings and situations:** A second-hand website with a motorbike, a vintage phone and a dining table; a chat between buyer and seller in Valencia; choosing a backpack for mum; a market stall; a seafood paella; a bar, a restaurant and a café; a vegetarian cook's profile; the world's most eaten foods; a Mexican salad recipe.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Ponga / póngame (ordering) → me da / me trae / ¿me regala…? (MX; *me regala* is very common at a market or counter). Caña → una cerveza (de barril) (MX). Tapa → botana (MX), and botanas often come free with drinks in a cantina. La vuelta → el cambio (MX). Venir bien → quedar bien (MX). Gambas → camarones; patatas → papas; judías verdes → ejotes; melocotón → durazno; zumo → jugo (MX). The book teaches the perfect for *¿Ha llamado alguien?*; Mexico says *¿Llamó alguien?* Pay attention to *la propina*: in Mexico a 10–15% tip is expected in restaurants.

Bañarse (swim in the sea) is fine in Mexico, but *bañarse* also means to shower, so context matters. The present subjunctive for wishes works the same in Mexico.

## Unidad 18: Predicciones

**Pages:** Student book pp. 185–194  
**Level:** A2  
**Sections:** A ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas esperando? · B ¿Qué pasará? · C ¿Qué te parece este…? · D Comunicación y cultura: jóvenes latinos que inspiran a Estados Unidos

**What the learner can do**

- Say how long you have been doing something
- Make predictions and promises about the future
- Say what will happen if…
- Give your opinion of pictures, problems and ideas; agree and disagree
- Describe a photo on your phone and pass on why a friend likes theirs

**Key frames**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas esperando? | How long have you been waiting? |
| Llevo más de una hora aquí. | I've been here over an hour. |
| Llevo seis meses estudiando español. | I've been learning Spanish for six months. |
| ¿Dónde crees que estarás dentro de veinte años? | Where do you think you'll be in twenty years? |
| Yo creo que habrá menos contaminación. | I think there'll be less pollution. |
| Mañana iré al cine contigo. | I'll go to the cinema with you tomorrow. |
| Si no llueve, iré a tu casa. | If it doesn't rain, I'll come to your place. |
| Si puedes, ven a mi casa. | Come over if you can. |
| ¿Qué te parece esta foto? | What do you think of this photo? |
| Me parece preciosa. / A mí no me gusta nada. | I think it's beautiful. / I don't like it at all. |
| A mí me preocupa la contaminación. | Pollution worries me. |
| No me interesa la política. — A mí tampoco. | I'm not interested in politics. — Neither am I. |
| Yo también lo creo. / Yo no lo creo. | I think so too. / I don't think so. |
| Mira, esta foto es de un viaje que hice. | Look, this photo is from a trip I took. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Llevar + gerund + time (llevo un año estudiando), and llevar + gerund + desde
2. Simple future, regular and irregular (habré, haré, podré, tendré, saldré, pondré); for predictions and promises
3. Conditional sentences: si + present, + present / imperative / future
4. Opinion verbs: me parece, creo / pienso que, (no) me interesa, me preocupa, me molesta; agreeing (yo también lo creo)
5. Pronunciation: the /θ/ sound in Spain and seseo in Latin America and southern Spain

**Vocabulary themes:** Duration; the future of the planet (pollution, space travel, cities, disease); promises in adverts and election speeches; adjectives for pictures (stunning, relaxing, romantic, weird, ugly); young people's worries (unemployment, housing, drugs, the environment, corruption).

**Settings and situations:** People waiting in a queue and new at work; the children of Chinese and British parents in Toledo; predictions for the 21st century; adverts and politicians' promises; choosing a laptop wallpaper; two 17-year-olds on what worries them; young Latinos in the US (including a Mexican-American food-tech founder); a survey about the future.

**Spain vs Mexico:** Llevar + gerund is understood in Mexico; very colloquially Mexicans also say *¿Cuánto tienes esperando?* or *¿Hace cuánto que esperas?* For plans, Mexico prefers ir a + infinitive (voy a ir) to the simple future (iré); the simple future is more for predictions and for guessing (¿Qué hora será?). Seseo: the book's own pronunciation page notes that most of Latin America pronounces z / ce / ci as s; that is the model for Afuera.

## Order and pacing

- **Antes de empezar and Unit 1 (pp. 8–24):** alphabet, classroom questions, greetings, nationalities, jobs, tú vs usted, phone numbers and addresses. Unlike ELE Actual, the full present tense of regular verbs (all six persons) and ser / tener come at once, in Unit 1.
- **Units 2–4 (pp. 25–54):** family, location of things, the time, daily routine with reflexive and the main irregular verbs (empezar, volver, ir, salir), work, the home, hay vs está.
- **Ordering in a café comes very early: Unit 3C, pp. 40–41**, where the learner orders breakfast (café con leche, tostada, zumo) from a café menu and a waitress asks *¿qué desean?* The first **restaurant meal is Unit 5A, pp. 56–57**: a set menu with first course, main, drink and dessert, the waiter's questions, and asking for the bill. A **hotel booking by phone** is Unit 4C, p. 50. Ordering in a **bar** (*póngame, ¿quieren algo de tapa?, ¿me cobra?*) comes back at A2 in Unit 15C, p. 161, with a **market stall** in 15B, p. 158.
- **Shopping for things:** clothes shopping with trying on, fit, price and paying is Unit 9A, p. 96. A food stall is 15B; buying second-hand online is 15A.
- **Directions come in three steps.** Unit 6A, pp. 66–67: getting around by **metro** (which ticket, take line X, change to line Y, the third stop), formal and informal. Unit 8A, pp. 86–87: **walking directions** with a map (siga recto, gire a la derecha, tome la tercera calle). Unit 14C, p. 150: places in a street (en la esquina, en el cruce, detrás de la iglesia) and polite *¿podría decirme…?*
- **Imperative:** regular tú / usted affirmative in Unit 5C (recipes), irregular affirmative and *¿puedes + infinitive?* for favours in 6B, negative imperative in 16A.
- **Present progressive** (estar + gerund) in Unit 7B; *ir a + infinitive* not until Unit 10C, p. 110.
- **The past tenses, in this order:**
  1. **Pretérito indefinido, Unit 8B, p. 88** (regular verbs plus ir / ser and estar; *hizo* in weather): what you did yesterday, on Saturday, on holiday. Expanded in **Unit 11B, p. 119** for biographies and more irregulars.
  2. **Pretérito imperfecto, Unit 10B, p. 108**: how life used to be.
  3. **Pretérito perfecto, Unit 12B, p. 128**: what you have done today or this week, *ya / todavía no*, and experiences (*¿has… alguna vez?*).
  4. **Perfecto vs indefinido, Unit 13B, p. 138**: unfinished time (este fin de semana) vs finished time (el sábado).
  5. **Imperfecto vs indefinido, Unit 14A, p. 147**, and **estaba + gerund vs indefinido, Unit 17B, p. 179**.
- **Perfecto vs indefinido and Mexico.** The book follows Spain: anything inside today, this week or this year takes the perfecto (*Hoy he tenido mucho trabajo; ¿Qué has hecho este fin de semana?*). Mexico prefers the **indefinido for the recent past** too (*Hoy tuve mucho trabajo; ¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana?; ¿Ya comiste?*) and keeps the perfecto for experiences up to now (*¿Has ido a Oaxaca?*, *nunca he estado*) and for things still going on (*he estado muy ocupado*). The book's order suits Afuera well, because it teaches the indefinido first, four units before the perfecto. When porting Units 12, 13 and 15, Afuera should swap the "today" perfectos for indefinidos and keep the experience perfectos.
- **Units 15–18 (pp. 155–194):** impersonal se, indefinites (algo, nadie, ningún), negative imperative, ser / estar for moods, the present subjunctive (only for wishes: *¡que te mejores!, espero que…*), reported speech, llevar + gerund, the simple future and si-clauses, giving opinions.
- **Pace:** fast and even. Every unit is exactly 10 pages and packs three grammar points; there are no review units, only a one-page self-assessment per unit. By the end of Unit 9 (p. 104) the learner has the present, estar + gerund, the imperative, gustar, direct object pronouns, comparatives and the indefinido. Services come early (café in Unit 3, hotel in 4, restaurant in 5, metro in 6, phone calls in 7, walking directions in 8, clothes shop in 9).

## What it adds beyond ELE Actual A1

ELE Actual A1 (see `a1-ele-actual.md`) covers roughly the same topics as this book's Units 1–9 (identity, family, home, town, likes, routine, work), but it is slower and narrower in what the learner can *do*. Español en marcha adds:

- **Service encounters ELE Actual never teaches:** ordering breakfast in a café (3C), a set restaurant meal and asking for the bill (5A), booking a hotel room (4C), ordering in a bar with tapas (15C), buying at a market stall (15B), trying clothes on and paying (9A), seeing a doctor (16A), a job interview (17A), buying second-hand online (15A).
- **Getting around:** metro directions (6A), walking directions (8A, 14C), places in a street, means of transport and preferences. ELE Actual has no directions at all.
- **Social talk:** making plans, accepting and declining, arranging where and when to meet (7A); phone calls, asking for someone and leaving a message (7A); excuses (17C); good wishes (*¡que te mejores!, ¡que tengas buen viaje!*, 16C); borrowing and lending (13C); giving and asking opinions (18C).
- **Grammar ELE Actual A1 leaves out:** estar + gerund; the imperative (affirmative and negative, tú and usted, with pronouns); *¿puedes + infinitive?* for favours; direct and indirect object pronouns together (*me lo regaló, se lo di*); comparatives and superlatives; doler; ir a + infinitive; the imperfect, the perfect and their contrasts with the indefinido; estaba + gerund; hay que / se puede; impersonal se; indefinites; ser / estar with moods; the present subjunctive for wishes; reported speech; llevar + gerund; the simple future; si-clauses; me gustaría.
- **The past early and in full:** the indefinido at p. 88 of 194 (ELE Actual: p. 160 of 173, optional), then all the A2 past tenses.
- **More Mexico:** La Condesa and San Miguel de Allende (6D), Mexican fiestas including Día de Muertos (8C), a camping blog from the Barrancas del Cobre written in Mexican Spanish (10D), a diary from Oaxaca with mole and the Zócalo (12D), *Roma* and a Mexican telenovela (13D), an enchiladas ad and a Mexican salad (14B, 15), Leona Vicario (17D), a Mexican-American founder (18D), and the seseo note (18C).
- **Mediation tasks** (passing on information in your own words: 9D, 15A, 18C), which fit Afuera's habit of having one character tell another what the learner said.
- **Beyond A1 topics:** health and the body, childhood and the past, biographies, customs and etiquette, renting a home, films and series, news stories, work conditions, the future of the planet.

What ELE Actual has that this book leaves out or treats lightly: a slow, person-by-person build-up of the present tense; frequency expressions (*una vez a la semana, cada tres días*) as a topic; and saber vs conocer with abilities (*¿sabes nadar?*), which this book never teaches as a point.

## Spain vs Mexico across the book

- **Vosotros** is taught in every verb table, in gustar (os), in reflexives, imperatives and all past tenses. Mexico uses **ustedes** for every plural "you", with 3rd-person plural verbs: ¿Qué queréis? → ¿Qué quieren?; os gusta → les gusta; ¿tenéis…? → ¿tienen…?
- **Perfecto for today** (he comido, ¿qué has hecho hoy?) → **indefinido** in Mexico (comí, ¿qué hiciste hoy?). Keep the perfecto for experiences and ongoing situations. See "Order and pacing".
- **Simple future for plans** (iré) → **ir a + infinitive** is far more common in Mexico (voy a ir).
- **Vale** → va, sale, está bien (MX).
- **Coger** (take, grab) → **tomar / agarrar** (MX). Coger is vulgar in Mexico; Afuera should never model it and should recast it gently.
- **Pronto** for "early" → **temprano** (MX).
- **Quedar** (to meet up) → verse / quedar de verse (MX).
- **Apetecer** → tener ganas, antojarse (MX). **Venir bien** → quedar bien (MX). **Dejar** (lend) → prestar (MX). **Apuntarse** → inscribirse (MX).
- **Telephone:** ¿Diga? / ¿Dígame? → ¿Bueno? (MX).
- **Time:** las dos menos cuarto → cuarto para las dos (MX).
- **Directions:** seguir recto → seguir derecho; girar / torcer → dar vuelta; manzana → cuadra (MX).
- **Ordering:** póngame / pónganos → me da / nos trae / ¿me regala…? (MX); caña → cerveza; tapa → botana; la vuelta → el cambio; camarero → mesero; ¡que aproveche! → ¡provecho! (MX). Tip (*propina*) of 10–15% is expected in Mexico. The menú del día's Mexican counterpart is the *comida corrida*.
- **Food:** zumo → jugo; patata → papa; gambas → camarones; tarta → pastel; judías verdes → ejotes; melocotón → durazno; tostada (toast) → pan tostado (in Mexico a tostada is a fried tortilla); bollo → pan dulce; café solo → café negro / americano.
- **Home:** piso → departamento; salón → sala; dormitorio → recámara; nevera / frigorífico → refri; garaje → cochera; ascensor → elevador; alquilar → rentar; aparcar → estacionarse; chalé → casa.
- **Things:** ordenador → computadora; móvil → celular; gafas → lentes (usar lentes); bolígrafo → pluma; goma → borrador; billete (ticket) → boleto; plano → mapa; sellos → timbres; estanco → no equivalent.
- **Clothes:** vaqueros → jeans / pantalones de mezclilla; jersey → suéter; camiseta → playera; zapatillas (deportivas) → tenis; chaqueta → chamarra (casual) / saco (suit); americana → saco; bolso → bolsa; bañador → traje de baño; marrón → café. Mexicans often say *trae* for "is wearing".
- **Getting around:** coche → carro; autobús → camión (colloquial in Mexico City); andando → caminando; conducir → manejar; carné de conducir → licencia de manejo; autocar → autobús foráneo.
- **People and places:** barrio → colonia (city neighbourhood); colegio → escuela; majo → buena onda; enfadado → enojado; rubio → güero (everyday); padre / madre → papá / mamá (everyday).
- **Feelings:** encontrarse bien / mal → sentirse bien / mal; agobiado → estresado; ¡qué rollo! → ¡qué flojera!
- **Work:** jornada completa / media jornada → tiempo completo / medio tiempo.
- **Attention:** oiga → disculpe / perdone (oiga can sound abrupt in Mexico).
- **Pronunciation:** the book models Spain's /θ/ for z, ce, ci but itself explains seseo (Unit 18C). Afuera should model seseo and never treat it as an error.
- **Lucky overlaps:** the indefinido is taught before the perfecto; usted is used for strangers and service, as in Mexico; Mexican settings appear in several culture sections; the Barrancas del Cobre blog already uses Mexican forms.
