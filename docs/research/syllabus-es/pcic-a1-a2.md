# PCIC A1–A2: what a beginner can do in Spanish, and what it means for Afuera

2026-10-19 · From the *Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes* (PCIC, *Niveles de referencia para el español*), the official level reference for Spanish. Summary in our own words; Spanish frames are short everyday phrases, mostly written for this note. The full text is on Josh's laptop, not in the repo.

Companion file: `level-words-es.csv` (1,304 lemmas, A1 654, A2 650; format below).

## How the PCIC is built, and its one big bias

The PCIC splits each level into inventories: Gramática, Pronunciación, Ortografía, Funciones, Tácticas pragmáticas, Géneros, Nociones generales (abstract notions: quantity, space, time, quality) and Nociones específicas (topic vocabulary), plus three cultural ones. Each inventory sets A1 and A2 side by side.

**Its vocabulary assumes central and northern Spain.** The introduction to Nociones específicas says so plainly. It also says a school in Latin America should swap in its own words, and gives the example that the A2 fruit (plátano, manzana, naranja) might be durazno or mango elsewhere. The Nociones específicas A1–A2 inventory has no *[Hispanoamérica]* marks at all. Grammar, Funciones and Nociones generales do mark some American usage, and Grammar marks Mexico specifically: tuteo only (no voseo) except in Chiapas, and *ustedes* only, no *vosotros*.

So for Afuera, the list is a fair map of *what* a beginner needs (topics, how many words, the order), but every word still needs a Mexico check. The `variety` column does a first pass.

## What an A1 and an A2 learner can do

**A1:** introduce yourself and others; give and ask for name, origin, age, job, address, phone; say where things are; order and ask the price in a simple way; talk about the present, daily routine and likes (*me gusta*); say what you want (*quiero*) and plans with the present (*mañana trabajo*); greet, thank, apologise, say goodbye. Everything happens in the present tense, with *ser*, *estar*, *tener*, *haber* (*hay*) and regular verbs.

**A2:** everything above with more control, plus the past (*he comido*, *comí*, *comía*); plans with *ir a*; obligation and advice (*tengo que*, *hay que*, imperative); comparisons; asking for things and help politely (*¿me trae...?*, *¿puedes...?*); accepting and turning down invitations; making arrangements (*¿quedamos...?*); the phone; feelings and physical states (*tengo hambre*, *me duele*).

A point that surprises: in the PCIC, **no past tense is A1.** All three pasts arrive at A2. Fuori's Italian books bring in *passato prossimo* mid-A1.

## Grammar by level

In the PCIC's own order (noun → adjective → article → ... → complex sentences), not teaching order.

### A1

1. **Nouns:** gender in *-o / -a*, other endings learned word by word (*el árbol, la catedral*), *la foto, la moto, la radio*, pairs like *padre / madre*; plurals in *-s / -es*.
2. **Adjectives:** agreement in gender and number, after the noun (*un chico guapo*); nationalities (*español / española*); *muy* + adjective.
3. **Articles:** *el, la, los, las, un, una, unos, unas*, the contractions *al, del*; no article with names; required with *gustar* (*Me gusta la paella*); *hay* + no article or *un* (*Hay árboles*), *está* + *el* (*Ahí está la mesa*).
4. **Demonstratives** *este, ese, aquel* and *esto, eso, aquello* (*¿Qué es eso?*).
5. **Possessives** *mi, tu, su, nuestro*, ⚠ *vuestro* (Mexico: *su* / *de ustedes*).
6. **Quantifiers:** cardinal numbers, ordinals to 10th, *mucho, poco, bastante*; *también / tampoco*.
7. **Pronouns:** subject pronouns (⚠ *vosotros*), *usted / ustedes*; *me* with *gustar*; reflexive *se* (*levantarse*); relative *que*; question words *qué, quién, cuánto, dónde, cómo, por qué / porque*.
8. **Adverbs:** *aquí, ahí, allí* (marked: America prefers *acá, allá*); *ahora, hoy, mañana*; *mucho* vs *muy*; *bien / mal*; *sí / no*.
9. **Verbs:** the **present** of regular verbs plus *ser, estar, haber, ir*, used for now and for habits (*Vivo en Madrid*); infinitive after *querer* and *gustar*; participle as adjective (*Estoy cansado*).
10. **Ser and estar, first cut:** *ser* for identity, origin, job, the day (*Es lunes*); *estar* for location (*Está aquí*) and *bien / mal*.
11. **Sentences:** statements and negation; yes/no and *wh-* questions; *hay* (*Hay tres personas*); *y, ni, o, pero*; *porque* after the main clause; *para* + infinitive; *creo que* + indicative.

### A2

1. **Nouns:** the exceptions (*el día, la mano, el problema*), *-ista* words for both sexes, *-triz / -esa* pairs (*actor / actriz*).
2. **Comparison:** *más / menos... que, tan... como, tanto como*; *mejor, peor, mayor, menor*; **exclamations** *¡Qué bonito!*
3. **Articles:** *el aula*; generic use (*Leo el periódico*); *Juego al tenis*; *todos los días*; *unos 50 pesos* (about).
4. **Possessives, strong forms:** *mío, tuyo, suyo* (*¿De quién es? —Mío*). The PCIC notes America's *el libro tuyo*, *la casa de nosotros*.
5. **Quantifiers:** *todo, otro, demasiado, nada, nadie*.
6. **Object pronouns:** *me, te, nos, ⚠ os*; *lo, la, los, las*; *le, les*; *se lo*; position with verb phrases (*Voy a verlo / Lo voy a ver*); *a mí me gusta*; *conmigo, contigo*. The PCIC notes America's *la pasamos bien* against Spain's *lo pasamos bien*.
7. **Question words** *cuándo, cuál*.
8. **Present irregulars** (*quiero, puedo, pido*, *-go* verbs), *saber, dar*.
9. **Three pasts:**
   - **Pretérito perfecto** (*he comido*, with *hoy, este año, ya, todavía no*). This is the Spain default for today's events. Mexico uses it much less and says *comí* where Spain says *he comido*.
   - **Pretérito indefinido** (*comí, fui, tuve, hice*) for finished events.
   - **Imperfecto** (*era, tenía, iba*) for descriptions and habits.
10. **Imperative,** affirmative *tú* / ⚠ *vosotros* / *usted(es)*, irregulars *di, haz, pon, sal*, with pronouns (*Cómpralo*); fixed ones *mira, oye, perdona*.
11. **Gerund** and *estar* + gerund (*Está hablando*). **Verb phrases:** *ir a, acabar de, empezar a, volver a, tener que, hay que, poder*.
12. **Ser / estar, more:** *estar* + adjectives that only take *estar* (*Está contento*), *Estamos a tres grados*.
13. **Objects:** personal *a* (*Vi a Ana*); *Me duele la cabeza*.
14. **Sentences:** questions with *o*; exclamations; commands; reflexives (*Me ducho*); weather *hace* (*Hace frío*); *e* and *u* for *y* and *o*; real conditions *si* + present (*Si quieres, vamos*); *cuando* + present (habit); *antes de / después de* + infinitive; *desde, hasta, desde hace*; *por eso, entonces*.

### B1 and B2 in one line each

- **B1:** future and conditional (*iré, iría*), pluperfect (*había ido*), and the **present subjunctive**: wishes (*Ojalá..., Quiero que vengas*), negative commands (*No vengas*), *cuando* + future (*Cuando llegues...*), *para que, antes de que*, *es bueno que*. Real conditions with the future (*Si viene, iremos*).
- **B2:** **imperfect and perfect subjunctive**, so **hypotheticals** (*Si tuviera tiempo, te ayudaría*), past hypotheticals (*Si lo hubiera sabido, te habría llamado*), future perfect and conditional perfect, subjunctive after negated opinions (*No creo que...*), *aunque* + subjunctive. The PCIC notes America prefers the *-ra* forms (*tuviera*) and keeps *-se* for formal writing.

## For Afuera

### Vosotros, voseo, usted

- **No vosotros.** Mexico uses **ustedes** for every plural "you", friends included: *¿Qué quieren?*, *Siéntense*, *su casa*, *los veo mañana*. *Vosotros, os, vuestro* and the *-áis / -éis* forms are Spain only. The PCIC A1 grammar and many A2 frames use them, so translate every frame (*¿Qué preferís?* → *¿Qué prefieren?*). The list flags *vosotros, vuestro, os* as `es`.
- **No voseo.** Mexico uses **tú** everywhere except parts of Chiapas (the PCIC says this itself). Characters never say *vos*.
- **Usted for strangers and service.** In Mexico, *usted* is the polite default with anyone older, with strangers, and from staff to customers. Customers often use it with staff too. *Tú* comes quickly among younger people and once a character knows the learner. So a Mexican character should start with *usted* more often than a Roman barista starts with *Lei*. Characters should keep the form steady once chosen (the drift seen in the Italian trials). Note: *ustedes* is both formal and informal, so plurals carry no formality choice.
- **Mexican courtesy forms a beginner meets early:** *¿Mande?* (Sorry? / Yes?), *¿Bueno?* (phone), *Disculpe*, *Con permiso*, *Provecho*, *Con gusto*, *¿Me da...?*, *ahorita*, *Sale / Va / Órale*. None is in the PCIC A1–A2 lists.

### Using the list to measure whether a beginner can follow a line

1. **Known set per level:** A1 learner = rows with `level = A1`; A2 learner = A1 + A2. Add the Mexican twins of every `es` row at the same level, and treat `am` rows as normal Mexican vocabulary. Keep `es` rows as "understood but not Mexican" so a character who says *ordenador* or *vosotros* gets flagged as off-dialect, not just hard.
2. **Lemmatise the character's line.** Spanish verbs carry the person and tense, so match lemmas, not forms. spaCy's `es_core_news_sm` worked for this list (it lemmatises *japonesa* → *japonés*, *galletas* → *galleta*). Look for multiword rows first (`pos = phrase`: *por favor, de acuerdo, tener hambre, cajero automático*), then single words.
3. **Skip names, numbers written as digits, and the learner's own words** said back to them.
4. **Score:** the share of tokens in the known set. The research on reading suggests about 95% known words for gist and about 98% for ease. For a spoken line of 8–12 words, that means one unknown word at most, and it should be guessable from the scene or followed by help.
5. **Check grammar too.** A word count misses tense. At A1, flag any past tense, subjunctive, future or conditional. At A2, flag subjunctive, future and conditional (except fixed chunks like *me gustaría*, *¿podría...?*). Detect them from spaCy's morphology (`Mood=Sub`, `Tense=Fut`), which works in any language, not from word lists.
6. **Compare with the level judge** on the replay set, as planned for Italian with `level-words-it.csv`.

Limits: the PCIC lists are "open" by its own description. They are a base, not a full vocabulary. Nociones generales are abstract (we kept only plain words), and everyday words a character says constantly (*ahorita, oiga, mande, güero, chido*) are absent and belong to the Mexico supplement.

## About `level-words-es.csv`

Columns: `lemma,pos,level,theme,variety`. One row per lemma; `level` is the first level where the PCIC lists it; `theme` is the PCIC topic, in short English, where it first appears.

- **Nociones específicas A1–A2** (every lemma; 20 topics, 91 subtopics): split from collocations (*tomar / pedir ~ el menú* gives *tomar, pedir, menú*). Plurals and feminines go to the dictionary form (*huevos* → *huevo*, *japonesa* → *japonés*), except words normally used in the plural (*gafas, vacaciones, deberes, urgencias, medias*). Lexicalised multiword units are added as `phrase` rows (89 outside the social phrases) (*correo electrónico, cajero automático, ida y vuelta, tener hambre, hacer la compra*); their parts are rows too.
- **Nociones generales A1–A2:** plain words only, chosen by hand (*cerca, lejos, temprano, barato, fácil, ruido*). The PCIC's placeholders are expanded into the words they stand for: cardinal numbers, ordinals to 10th, days, months, seasons. Example sentences and grammar slots are skipped.
- **Added beyond the two inventories,** so the list can score real speech: 77 closed-class words from the Gramática A1–A2 inventory (articles, pronouns, possessives, demonstratives, prepositions, conjunctions, question words; theme `function words`) and 39 formulas from Funciones A1–A2 (*hola, de nada, mucho gusto, buen provecho*; theme `social phrases`). Drop these two themes to get the inventories alone (A1 583, A2 605).
- **Dropped:** proper nouns (*Madrid, Laos, Dios*), *TV*, *CD-ROM*, *Ramadán, Sabbat*, *ex*, *reencarnación*.

**Parsing notes.** The source is a PDF-to-text dump with A1 and A2 as two columns. Each row was assigned by the horizontal position of the "A2" header in each table. Where the header is squeezed together ("A1 A2") the A1 cell is empty and everything is A2; this matches the PCIC's other inventories wherever we cross-checked (for example, *tener que* is A2 in both Funciones and Gramática). Some results look odd but follow the layout: *cabeza, brazo, pierna* are A2 while *pelo, ojo, nariz* are A1 (needed to describe people); *tener hambre / sed* and *¿Cómo estás?* are A2. We could not check them against the Cervantes website from here (the site was blocked). Subheadings inside columns (*Transporte aéreo, La playa*) and cross-references were removed.
