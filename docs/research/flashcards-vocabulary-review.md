# Fuori Flashcards and Vocabulary Review: Research

2026-10-17 · Josh · Draft

Flashcards work better than their reputation, but they build words you can recall, not words you use without thinking. Conversation has to finish the job. The design that came out of this is [../diario.md](../diario.md).

## What the evidence says

**Flashcards work.** Some studies find word cards as effective as learning vocabulary in context, or more so. When reading was compared directly with word lists, learners often did better with the lists. Drilling isn't the enemy of an immersion app; it does a different job.

**Spacing is the biggest lever.** A meta-analysis of spacing in L2 learning (Kim & Webb, 2022) found a medium-to-large effect. In one study every spaced schedule beat massed practice with large effects, but longer gaps didn't beat shorter ones. Spacing and cramming can look the same on an immediate test; spacing pulls ahead at two weeks.

**Fancy schedules don't matter much.** Nakata's review found no L2 vocabulary study showing a benefit from gradually expanding intervals. Total spacing matters more than the pattern. A simple Leitner box is good enough.

**Repeated retrieval pays off, and direction matters.** In Nakata's flashcard research, the benefit of several retrievals was still there four weeks later. Productive recall (meaning → word) takes the most effort and works best for producing the word. For a speaking app: English intent → say the Italian aloud.

**Conversation alone is too slow.** Webb found 10 encounters gave large gains in recognizing a word but not enough to use it. Nation treats 12 repetitions as a reasonably safe minimum. Most words won't come up 12 times by chance. With audio plus a picture, complete beginners learned words from as few as two exposures, with the biggest gains in the first few.

**Cards build explicit knowledge, not automatic use.** Spacing words in context helped explicit knowledge but not tacit knowledge. That matches understanding a lot while struggling to respond.

## What this suggests for Fuori

- **Conversations are where words come from; a short review is where they stick.** Each card is a chunk the learner actually ran into, with the character's audio and the scene's image. No generic lists.
- **Review by speaking, not tapping.** Show the English intent, say the Italian, hear the character's line as confirmation. Both features can share one mechanism (the level screens).
- **Keep the scheduler simple.** Equal or Leitner-style intervals, a few retrievals per item, and a cap on new items per conversation (about 2–3% more each time).
- **Close the loop in the world.** Once a chunk is reviewed, upcoming scenes create situations that call for it. Cards make it recallable; using it with a character makes it automatic.
- **Keep drilling outside the game world**, so characters stay in character.
