# Functions and the grammar they need

Source: Anna Proudfoot, *Italian Grammar You Really Need to Know* (22 units, each built around a communicative function). Summary for Fuori mission design, in our own words; the example frames are new, not quoted from the book. CEFR levels are a rough judgement for the *core* of each function, not its hardest corners.

| # | Function | Sub-functions | Grammar needed | Example frames | CEFR (core) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asking for and giving personal information | Name; nationality; home city/region; job and marital status; asking others; describing others | Subject pronouns (io, tu, Lei); essere and chiamarsi (present); nationality/region adjectives and their agreement and plurals; job nouns, titles (signor, signora, dottore) | Mi chiamo Josh. (My name is Josh.) / Sono americano. (I'm American.) / Di dove sei? (Where are you from?) / Faccio il pensionato. (I'm retired.) / Lei come si chiama? (What's your name?) | **A1**: the first exchange in every course and every real encounter; fixed, short present-tense patterns. |
| 2 | Identifying people and things | Ask for something; ask/say what it is; ask/say who someone is; point things out | Noun gender and plurals; indefinite articles un/uno/una/un'; Chi è? / Cos'è?; questo/questa/questi/queste | Un caffè, per favore. (A coffee, please.) / Cos'è questo? (What's this?) / Chi è lei? (Who's she?) / Questa, grazie. (This one, thanks.) | **A1**: naming and pointing, with articles as the only real hurdle. |
| 3 | Asking about availability | Does it exist / is it here; is it available; how much there is | C'è / ci sono; partitives del/della/dei/delle; ne; alcuni/alcune, qualche, un po' di | C'è una farmacia qui? (Is there a pharmacy here?) / Avete cornetti? (Do you have croissants?) / Ne vorrei due. (I'd like two of them.) / Un po' di pane. (A bit of bread.) | **A1** for c'è/ci sono and avete...?; **A2** for confident ne and partitives, which beginners avoid for a while. |
| 4 | Talking about location | Ask where; say where | Definite articles il/lo/la/i/gli/le; Dov'è? / Dove sono?; prepositions in, a, da, su and their fused forms (nel, al, dal, sul); place phrases (in centro, a destra, al primo piano); ecco + lo/la/mi/ti | Dov'è la stazione? (Where's the station?) / È a destra. (It's on the right.) / Sono al bar. (I'm at the bar.) / Eccolo! (Here it is!) / In fondo, a sinistra. (At the end, on the left.) | **A1**: asking directions is a survival task; the articulated prepositions push full accuracy toward A2. |
| 5 | Stating choice and preference (shopping) | Ask the price; express a preference; say which item; numbers; how much/how many | questo/quello; quanto/quanti?; quale?; present of regular -are and -ere verbs (costare, prendere); numbers | Quanto costa? (How much is it?) / Quanto costano? (How much are they?) / Prendo quello. (I'll take that one.) / Quale preferisci? (Which do you prefer?) / Tre etti, per favore. (Three hundred grams, please.) | **A1**: a market or bar transaction is a core A1 can-do; numbers carry most of the load. |
| 6 | Talking about the present | What's happening now; where someone lives/works; when someone does something | Regular -are/-ere/-ire present (incl. -isc- verbs); dove? a che ora? come?; stare + gerund (sto mangiando); time expressions | Dove abiti? (Where do you live?) / Lavoro in un negozio. (I work in a shop.) / A che ora apre? (What time does it open?) / Sto aspettando un amico. (I'm waiting for a friend.) | **A1**: present tense of regular verbs is the backbone of A1; stare + gerund is easy add-on. |
| 7 | Talking about routine and habits | Daily routine; things you do for yourself; how you do things; how often | Reflexive verbs (alzarsi, vestirsi, sedersi); sequence words (prima, poi, più tardi); frequency adverbs (sempre, spesso, mai, di solito); forming -mente adverbs | Mi alzo alle sette. (I get up at seven.) / Di solito vengo a piedi. (I usually walk here.) / Non bevo mai latte. (I never drink milk.) / Poi faccio la spesa. (Then I do the shopping.) | **A1–A2**: describing a day is A1; reflexives and the non...mai negative make it fully comfortable around A2. |
| 8 | Possibility and asking permission | Ask permission; ask someone to do something for you; say you can/can't; ask if allowed | potere; impersonal si può?; è possibile?; sapere (know how to) vs potere | Posso sedermi qui? (Can I sit here?) / Si può pagare con carta? (Can you pay by card?) / Mi può aiutare? (Can you help me?) / Non so nuotare. (I can't swim.) | **A1**: posso / si può / può are high-frequency fixed frames; potere vs sapere is a light A2 refinement. |
| 9 | Giving orders and instructions | Request, order, instruct; read written instructions; tell someone not to | Imperative for tu, Lei, noi, voi, loro; negative imperative (non + infinitive for tu); infinitive in signs and recipes | Scusi, mi porta il conto? (Could you bring the bill?) / Gira a destra. (Turn right.) / Senta, scusi! (Excuse me!) / Non toccare! (Don't touch!) / Spingere. (Push.) | **A2**: learners understand imperatives in directions early, but producing tu vs Lei forms correctly is A2 work. |
| 10 | Talking about possession | Whose is it; say whose it is; ask what others have | Possessive adjectives and pronouns (il mio, la tua, il suo...), article rules with family words; proprio; di for ownership; Di chi è?; avere; quale? | Di chi è questa borsa? (Whose bag is this?) / È mia. (It's mine.) / Mia figlia studia. (My daughter studies.) / Hai una macchina? (Do you have a car?) | **A1–A2**: avere and mio/tuo are A1; full possessive agreement with articles settles at A2. |
| 11 | Events and actions in the past | Completed past events; past linked to now; ongoing actions (da + present) | Passato prossimo with avere and with essere (participle agreement); verbs taking both; reflexives in the past; negatives in the past; time phrases (ieri, la settimana scorsa); days and months; present + da; passato remoto (recognition) | Ieri sono andato a Firenze. (Yesterday I went to Florence.) / Ho già mangiato. (I've already eaten.) / Abito qui da un mese. (I've lived here a month.) / Non l'ho visto. (I didn't see it.) | **A2**: the passato prossimo is the signature A2 structure; avere/essere choice is the main difficulty. Passato remoto is B1+ recognition only. |
| 12 | Describing the past | How things were; past habits; what was going on when something happened; what had already happened; what was about to happen | Imperfetto; imperfetto vs passato prossimo; trapassato prossimo; stava + gerund, stava per; imperfetto + da; trapassato remoto (literary) | Da piccolo vivevo a Seattle. (As a kid I lived in Seattle.) / Pioveva quando sono uscito. (It was raining when I left.) / Stavo per chiamarti. (I was about to call you.) / Era già partito. (He had already left.) | **A2–B1**: imperfetto alone is late A2; choosing it against the passato prossimo and using the pluperfect is B1. |
| 13 | Talking about the future | Plans; probability; intentions | Futuro semplice; present for near future; futuro anteriore; future for guessing (sarà...); future time phrases (domani, fra due giorni); pensare di + infinitive | Domani vado al mare. (Tomorrow I'm going to the sea.) / Ci vediamo sabato? (See you Saturday?) / Penso di restare. (I'm thinking of staying.) / Saranno le otto. (It must be about eight.) / Quando tornerai? (When will you be back?) | **A2**: plans in the present tense are A1; the futuro is A2; future of probability and futuro anteriore are B1. |
| 14 | Wants and preferences | Want a thing; want to do something; want someone else to do something; prefer | volere + noun/infinitive; vorrei (polite conditional); preferire; direct object pronouns lo/la/li/le; ne; verb + a/di + infinitive; idioms (ci vuole, voler bene) | Vorrei un cappuccino. (I'd like a cappuccino.) / Voglio vedere il Colosseo. (I want to see the Colosseum.) / Preferisco il rosso. (I prefer the red.) / Lo prendo. (I'll take it.) / Ci vuole un'ora. (It takes an hour.) | **A1** for vorrei / voglio / preferisco; **B1** for wanting someone else to act (volere che + subjunctive). |
| 15 | Processes and procedures | How something is done; how it should be done; impersonal instructions; your reactions | Passive with essere, venire, andare (va fatto); si passivante; impersonal si; rimanere + participle | Come si dice...? (How do you say...?) / Qui si mangia bene. (You eat well here.) / Il biglietto va timbrato. (The ticket must be stamped.) / Si gira a sinistra. (You turn left.) / Sono rimasto sorpreso. (I was surprised.) | **B1**: impersonal si frames appear early as chunks (Come si dice?), but the passive system is B1. |
| 16 | Likes and dislikes | Your likes; someone else's; ask about them; contrast | piacere (mi piace / mi piacciono); indirect object pronouns mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi; stressed forms a me, a te; anche / neanche | Mi piace Roma. (I like Rome.) / Ti piacciono i carciofi? (Do you like artichokes?) / A me no. (Not me.) / Neanche a me. (Me neither.) / Le piace il vino? (Do you like wine?) | **A1** for mi piace / ti piace as chunks; **A2** for the full pronoun set and plural agreement. |
| 17 | Asking for and giving an opinion | Belief or opinion; ask for one; pass on a rumour; tentative view | Present subjunctive; penso che + subjunctive vs penso di + infinitive; sembrare, parere; opinion phrases (secondo me, per me); magari | Secondo me è troppo caro. (I think it's too expensive.) / Cosa ne pensi? (What do you think?) / Mi sembra giusto. (Seems fair to me.) / Penso che sia chiuso. (I think it's closed.) / Magari domani. (Maybe tomorrow.) | **B1**: secondo me works at A2, but real opinion-giving hinges on penso che + subjunctive, a B1 structure. |
| 18 | Obligation and need | Your obligations; others'; your needs; others'; necessity; what's needed | dovere; avere bisogno di; bisogna; c'è bisogno di; è necessario; occorre / ci vuole / ci vogliono; impersonal + infinitive or che + subjunctive | Devo partire domani. (I have to leave tomorrow.) / Ho bisogno di aiuto. (I need help.) / Bisogna prenotare? (Do you need to book?) / Ci vogliono due ore. (It takes two hours.) / Mi serve una penna. (I need a pen.) | **A2**: devo and ho bisogno di are early; the impersonal-plus-subjunctive versions are B1. |
| 19 | Emotions and uncertainty | Feelings; doubt; possibility and probability; wishes for others | Subjunctive after emotion verbs (sono contento che, ho paura che), impersonal expressions (è possibile che), and conjunctions (benché, prima che, perché); subjunctive vs indicative | Sono contento che tu sia qui. (I'm glad you're here.) / Spero che venga. (I hope he comes.) / Non sono sicuro. (I'm not sure.) / Può darsi. (Could be.) / Ho paura di perdermi. (I'm afraid of getting lost.) | **B1**: simple feelings are A1–A2, but the core of this unit is the subjunctive, which is B1 (choosing it confidently is B2). |
| 20 | Wishes and polite requests | Wish for yourself; wish involving others; polite request; allow; get someone to do; order, suggest, invite, encourage | Conditional (vorrei, potrebbe, dovresti); imperfect subjunctive and vorrei che...; chiedere/dire a qualcuno di + infinitive; fare and lasciare + infinitive | Potrebbe ripetere? (Could you repeat that?) / Mi farebbe un favore? (Would you do me a favour?) / Dovresti provare. (You should try it.) / Lasciami pagare. (Let me pay.) / Vorrei che tu venissi. (I'd like you to come.) | **A2** for polite conditional chunks (vorrei, potrebbe); **B1–B2** for vorrei che + imperfect subjunctive and fare + infinitive. |
| 21 | Regrets | Regrets and wishes about the past; say sorry; what would have happened; hearsay; reported speech | Past conditional (avrei fatto); pluperfect subjunctive (se fossi venuto); conditional for hearsay (sarebbe...); reported speech and tense shifts | Mi dispiace tanto. (I'm so sorry.) / Avrei dovuto chiamare. (I should have called.) / Magari fossi rimasto! (If only I'd stayed!) / Ha detto che veniva. (She said she'd come.) / Sarebbe chiuso. (Apparently it's closed.) | **B1–B2**: mi dispiace is A1, but the past conditional is B1 and the pluperfect subjunctive is B2. |
| 22 | Conditions | Real conditions; unlikely conditions; impossible (past) conditions | se + present/future; se + imperfect subjunctive + conditional; se + pluperfect subjunctive + past conditional; a condizione che, a meno che, purché | Se piove, resto a casa. (If it rains, I'll stay in.) / Se vuoi, ti accompagno. (If you like, I'll walk you.) / Se avessi tempo, verrei. (If I had time, I'd come.) / Se l'avessi saputo... (If I'd known...) | **B1**: real conditions are A2; the hypothetical se + imperfect subjunctive is the classic B1 milestone; past conditions are B2. |

## A complete beginner's first week living in Italy, in order

Ordered by what daily life forces on a newcomer, not by the book's unit order. Each step leans on chunks first and grammar second.

1. **Unit 2: Identifying and asking for things** (un caffè, questo, per favore). Needed from the first hour at the bar and the shop.
2. **Unit 5: Prices, numbers, choosing** (quanto costa, prendo questo). Needed at the bar, the market, the tabaccheria.
3. **Unit 1: Personal information** (mi chiamo, sono americano, abito qui). Every neighbour, landlord and barista asks.
4. **Unit 4: Location and directions** (dov'è, a destra, vicino). Finding the pharmacy, the bus stop, the new flat.
5. **Unit 3: Availability** (c'è, avete...?). "Do you have...?" and "Is there a... near here?" cover most errands.
6. **Unit 8: Permission and asking for help** (posso, si può, mi può aiutare). Paying by card, sitting down, asking someone to repeat.
7. **Unit 14 (core only): Wants** (vorrei, voglio, preferisco). The polite way to order anything; overlaps 2 and 5.
8. **Unit 16 (core only): Likes** (mi piace, ti piace). The first real small talk with people you'll see again.
9. **Unit 6: Present and times** (dove lavori, a che ora apre). Opening hours, schedules, simple "what do you do" chat.

By the end of week one, also start Unit 7 (routine) and the present-for-future part of Unit 13 (domani vado..., ci vediamo sabato?), which lets the learner make a plan. Units 9 (understanding directions as imperatives) and 18 (devo, ho bisogno di) come in as listening and fixed chunks rather than taught grammar. The past (11), opinions (17) and everything subjunctive or conditional-based (19–22) can wait until weeks two to four or later, apart from set phrases like mi dispiace and potrebbe ripetere?
