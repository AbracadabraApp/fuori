# Nuovissimo Progetto italiano 2 (B1–B2): syllabus summary

A summary of the course's progression, taken from the **workbook** (Quaderno degli esercizi) only, for Fuori's own syllabus. Written in our own words; the Italian phrases are ordinary everyday chunks, not copied exercises. Page numbers are the printed workbook pages (they match the PDF page numbers). Each unit's range includes its end-of-unit test ("Test finale", 1–2 pages). Where the workbook points to the student book, the student-book (SB) page is given.

**What the workbook contains:** a review unit ("Prima di… cominciare", pp. 5–8), Units 1–11, four review tests (one every three units), a general final test, and two board games (Units 0–5 and Units 0–11). Exercises follow the student book's sections (A, B, C…) but the OCR doesn't keep the section letters, so the grammar below is listed in the order the exercises practise it.

**B1 or B2?** The workbook calls itself one B1–B2 volume and never marks a 2a/2b split. The only break inside it is the first board game, which reviews Units 0–5. The grammar fits that break: Units 1–5 finish the B1 core (combined pronouns, relatives, comparisons, passato remoto, present and past subjunctive), and Units 6–11 are the B2 load (formal imperative, imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive, all three hypothetical types, the passive, reported speech, implicit clauses). So below, **Units 1–5 = B1** and **Units 6–11 = B2**. Unit 6 is a transition unit: its grammar (Lei imperative, indefinites) is lighter, and the B2 grammar really starts in Unit 7.

## Prima di… cominciare (review of A2)

**Pages:** 5–8 · **Level:** A2 review

**Themes:** the Italian bar; family; everyday exchanges (directions, shopping, asking the price, the cinema); a remembered love story; a street-art festival in Rome.

**Grammar practised:** definite and indefinite articles; possessives with and without the article; adjective opposites; presente, passato prossimo, imperfetto and trapassato prossimo in one story; direct and indirect pronouns; simple future and simple/compound conditional; simple and articulated prepositions; tu/voi imperative with negatives; volerci/metterci, mettersi and impersonal si.

**Functions:** a check that the A2 survival exchanges are in place: introductions, thanks, ordering, directions, sizes and colours in a shop, guessing an age ("Avrà trent'anni").

## Unità 1: Esami… niente stress!

**Pages:** 9–19 · **Level:** B1 · SB grammar appendix p. 205

**Themes:** university life: enrolling, booking an exam session, lectures, notes, the canteen; dropping out of school and young people leaving Italy for work abroad; student flat and job adverts.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Combined pronouns (me lo, te la, glielo, ce ne…) with simple tenses
2. Combined pronouns with ne (gliene, ve ne)
3. Combined pronouns in compound tenses, with the participle agreeing (me l'ha regalata, te li ho dati)
4. Position with modal verbs: before the modal or attached to the infinitive (te lo posso dare / posso dartelo)
5. Interrogatives: chi, che cosa, quale/quali, quanto, come mai, perché, dove, da quanto tempo
6. Prepositions in short adverts

**Vocabulary themes:** university (faculty, degree course, exam session, student number, grade average, lecturer, enrolment, sitting an exam, taking notes, chapter, syllabus); professions (surgeon, engineer, architect, lawyer, dentist, teacher); lending and giving everyday objects.

**Communicative functions**

- Offer to lend, give or bring something, and answer offers ("Sì, te li porto domani")
- Say who did what for whom without repeating the noun
- React to surprising news with disbelief or amazement ("Davvero?!", "Ma va!", "Non ci posso credere!", "Chi l'avrebbe mai detto?")
- Apologise and answer an apology ("Scusami", "Non importa", "Figurati!", "Non si preoccupi")
- Ask the questions of a job interview or a getting-to-know-you chat

## Unità 2: Soldi e lavoro

**Pages:** 20–31 · **Level:** B1 · SB grammar appendix p. 207

**Themes:** banks and money: current accounts, cards, loans, interest, online banking; job hunting: adverts, cover letters, CVs, phone and in-person interviews; young Italians living with their parents into their thirties because work is insecure.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Relative pronoun che joining two sentences
2. Il quale / la quale / i quali / le quali as an alternative to che
3. Cui with prepositions (a cui, con cui, di cui, in cui, per cui, su cui), and della quale etc. instead of di cui
4. In cui vs dove
5. Il cui / la cui (whose)
6. Chi (whoever) vs che, in proverbs; quello che / ciò che / coloro che / tutti quelli che / il che
7. Connectives of cause and result: perché, siccome, visto che, perciò, quindi, così, però, come mai
8. Imperative (tu) for tips, in an article on phone interviews
9. Stare + gerund (happening now) and stare per + infinitive (about to happen)
10. Prepositions in course adverts

**Vocabulary themes:** banking (bancomat, current account, loan, interest rate, withdrawing, paying in cash, cheque, cash machine, mortgage); work (unemployed, candidate, position, interview, human resources, CV, temporary vs permanent job, qualifications, strengths and weaknesses); formal letter openings and closings.

**Communicative functions**

- Open a bank account and ask what a service offers
- Read a job advert and write a short one
- Write a formal email or cover letter: the right opening ("Gentile…", "Egregio…", "Spett.le…") and closing ("Cordiali saluti", "Distinti saluti"), and the informal equivalents ("Caro…", "Un abbraccio")
- Introduce yourself at an interview and talk about your strengths, weaknesses and goals
- Say what you're doing right now or about to do ("Sto per uscire", "Sto guardando un film")
- Use and recognise common proverbs

## Unità 3: In viaggio per l'Italia

**Pages:** 32–43 · **Level:** B1 · SB dialogue p. 40

**Themes:** choosing where to travel in Italy (Rome, Florence, Venice, Naples); booking hotels and B&Bs; online hotel reviews; Italian food records; an American city compared with an Italian one; the people of each region.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Comparisons of more, less and equal (più… di, meno… di, come / quanto, tanto… quanto)
2. Di or che in comparisons (che before an adjective, verb, adverb or prepositional phrase)
3. Relative superlative (il più… di/tra) and absolute superlative (-issimo)
4. Irregular comparatives (migliore, peggiore, maggiore, minore, superiore, inferiore) and superlatives (ottimo, pessimo, massimo, minimo)
5. Pronominal verbs with la/ne: farcela, cavarsela, prendersela, andarsene (in the tests and the first review test)
6. Prepositions in a travel complaint

**Vocabulary themes:** hotel services (shuttle, pool, spa, breakfast, balcony, pets allowed, double room, extra bed, half board); reviews and complaints; luggage, booking, passport; famous Italian sights (Palazzo Ducale, underground Naples, Bernini).

**Communicative functions**

- Compare cities, people, prices and ways of travelling
- Book a hotel room by phone and ask about the price, services and pets ("Vorrei prenotare una camera matrimoniale", "È possibile aggiungere un letto?")
- Write or read a short hotel review, praising and complaining
- Talk about bests and worsts ("È il più bello della città", "una pessima idea")
- Encourage someone or say you'll manage ("Ce la farai!", "Me la cavo")

**1st review test (Units 1–3):** pp. 44–45.

## Unità 4: Un po' di storia

**Pages:** 46–56 · **Level:** B1 · SB pp. 55, 63; grammar appendix pp. 211–213

**Themes:** periods of Italian history (ancient Rome, Middle Ages, Renaissance, Risorgimento, post-war boom); Romulus and Remus, Caesar, Garibaldi and Italian unification; biographies (Gianni Rodari, Oriana Fallaci, Virgil); Pinocchio and a Dolomites legend; Asterix; Roman names; the history of the Italian language and its dialects.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Passato remoto, regular verbs (all persons)
2. Passato remoto, irregular verbs (fu, ebbe, diede, disse, nacque, lesse, venne, fece, stette…)
3. Passato remoto vs imperfetto in tales and legends
4. Trapassato remoto (dopo che ebbe finito, non appena fu arrivato)
5. Historic present rewritten in the passato remoto
6. Roman numerals and centuries
7. Adverbs in -mente
8. Prepositions in a history text

**Vocabulary themes:** history (empire, republic, emperor, dictator, enemy, to fight, to found, to conquer, volunteers); fairy-tale language ("C'era una volta…"); biography; language and dialect.

**Communicative functions**

- Tell a story, a legend or a fable in a literary past tense
- Recognise the passato remoto in reading and in southern speech
- Tell the main events of a life or a historical period
- Contradict someone firmly ("Ma non è vero niente!", "Niente affatto!", "Neanche per sogno!")
- Explain what you mean ("cioè…", "nel senso che…", "mi spiego meglio")

## Unità 5: Stare bene

**Pages:** 57–69 · **Level:** B1 · SB pp. 70–71; grammar appendix pp. 214, 216

**Themes:** keeping fit, sport and exercise; diet and healthy living; stress and a stay at a wellness retreat; how foreigners see Italians; how Italians do sport; the Paralympic fencer Bebe Vio.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Congiuntivo presente: regular and irregular forms
2. After verbs of opinion, belief, hope, fear and doubt (penso che, credo che, spero che, temo che, non sono sicuro che)
3. After impersonal phrases (è necessario che, è meglio che, è probabile che, può darsi che, sembra che, bisogna che)
4. Congiuntivo vs indicativo (è vero che, sono sicuro che, secondo me, probabilmente take the indicative)
5. Subjunctive vs di + infinitive when the subject is the same
6. Congiuntivo passato
7. Conjunctions that take the subjunctive: benché, sebbene, nonostante, a patto che, purché, a meno che (non), prima che, senza che, nel caso (in cui), perché (purpose)
8. Indefinites with the subjunctive: chiunque, qualunque/qualsiasi, ovunque, comunque
9. Subjunctive after a relative superlative ("il più simpatico che io conosca")
10. Choosing among the subjunctive tenses

**Vocabulary themes:** fitness (running, abs, training, a sedentary life, keeping in shape); food and health (diet, junk food, nutritionist, sleep, stress, loneliness); sports (cycling, swimming, football, yoga, pilates, fencing); national stereotypes.

**Communicative functions**

- Give an opinion and say what you believe, hope, fear or doubt
- Give advice on healthy habits ("È importante che tu dorma almeno sette ore")
- Agree or disagree with stereotypes
- Set conditions and make concessions ("Ti presto la macchina purché tu guidi piano")
- Reassure someone and answer apologies or requests ("Non fa niente!", "Certo, nessun problema", "Faccia pure con calma", "Fa' come vuoi")

**Board game, Units 0–5:** pp. 152–153.

## Unità 6: Andiamo all'opera

**Pages:** 70–79 · **Level:** B2 (transition) · SB grammar appendix p. 219

**Themes:** opera and La Scala (an evening when the tenor walked off stage, Verdi's birthplace, Maria Callas); going to the doctor; a Roman taxi driver's day; office life with a boss and a secretary; gym rules.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Formal imperative (Lei): regular and irregular forms (venga, vada, faccia, dica, abbia, sia)
2. Turning tu commands into Lei commands and the other way round
3. Formal imperative with pronouns, which come before the verb (mi dica, si accomodi, me lo porti)
4. Negative imperative, informal and formal (non preoccuparti / non si preoccupi)
5. Indefinite adjectives and pronouns: qualche, alcuni, ogni, ognuno, ciascuno, nessuno, niente/nulla, qualcosa/qualcuno di…, tanto, troppo, parecchio, certo, diverso
6. Prepositions in a museum article
7. Connectives to join several short facts into one sentence

**Vocabulary themes:** opera (aria, act, stage, tenor, soprano, orchestra, libretto, applause, costume, audience); health (flu, fever, cough, symptoms, antibiotics, prescription, eye drops, plasters, muscle pain, surgery, check-up, pills, ointment); gym etiquette.

**Communicative functions**

- Give instructions, advice and directions politely to someone you call Lei ("Giri alla prima a destra", "Si accomodi", "Mi dica")
- Describe symptoms and make a doctor's appointment
- Reassure someone formally ("Non si preoccupi!")
- Talk about an opera or a show you saw
- Talk about amounts vaguely ("qualche amico", "nessuna risposta", "parecchi giorni")

**2nd review test (Units 4–6):** pp. 80–81.

## Unità 7: Andiamo a vivere in campagna

**Pages:** 82–91 · **Level:** B2 · SB grammar appendix pp. 221–222

**Themes:** city life vs life in the country or the suburbs; flat and house adverts; building materials; the environment: pollution, a farmer who planted a forest, the school climate strike, the WWF; leaving the city for an eco B&B; how much of your pay should go on rent.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Congiuntivo imperfetto (regular and irregular forms)
2. Present or imperfect subjunctive, depending on the tense of the main verb (penso che venga / pensavo che venisse)
3. Subjunctive or indicative after past main clauses
4. Congiuntivo trapassato
5. Sequence of tenses with all four subjunctive tenses
6. More conjunctions: affinché, malgrado, come se, senza che, nel caso in cui
7. Subjunctive in relative clauses ("cerco una casa che abbia un giardino"), after l'unico che, after magari ("Magari fossi venuto!")
8. Indirect yes/no questions with se (indicative or subjunctive)
9. Prepositions in a blog post about rent

**Vocabulary themes:** housing (studio flat, square metres, kitchenette, furnished, renovated, parking space, sea view, estate agency, moving house); materials (marble, wood, stone, iron, ceramic, concrete, glass); environment (traffic, smog, noise, pollution, ecologist, recycling, renewable energy, cycle lanes, plastic, global warming).

**Communicative functions**

- Say what you used to think or expect, and that you were wrong ("Credevo che fosse più facile")
- Describe the home you want and match a flat to a person
- Compare city and country living
- Argue about the environment in an online thread: what each of us should do and what governments should do
- Wish that things were or had been different ("Magari!")

## Unità 8: Tempo libero e tecnologia

**Pages:** 92–105 · **Level:** B2 · SB grammar appendix p. 224

**Themes:** social networks, selfies and influencers; tech dependence (a quiz); a man without a mobile phone; a computer that won't work; how Italians spend their free time (DIY, gardening, sport, cooking); video-game addiction; Italy's best-known tech-help blogger; what would have happened in history with mobile phones.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Hypothetical period, type 1 (real: se + present/future, then present/future/imperative)
2. Hypothetical period, type 2 (possible: se + imperfect subjunctive, then conditional)
3. Turning type 1 into type 2
4. Hypothetical period, type 3 (impossible, past: se + pluperfect subjunctive, then past conditional)
5. Mixed hypotheticals (a past condition with a present result: "se avessi seguito il consiglio, ora non sarei qui")
6. Recognising real, possible and impossible conditions
7. Direct vs indirect pronouns in compound tenses (review)
8. Ci: as a place, as "us", with verbs (pensarci, riuscirci, metterci, volerci), and ci vs ne (review)

**Vocabulary themes:** social media (post, follower, likes, influencer, selfie); computers and phones (download an app, save a file, press a key, send an email, video call, laptop, keyboard, charger, battery, screen, printer, Wi-Fi, install); free time and hobbies (DIY, gardening, cooking).

**Communicative functions**

- Say what you'll do if something happens, what you would do if things were different, and what you would have done
- Express regret and imagine other outcomes
- Explain a tech problem and suggest fixes ("Hai provato a spegnere e riaccendere?")
- Congratulate someone ("Congratulazioni!", "Che brava!") and react with outrage or approval ("Ma è assurdo!", "Questa sì che è una bella idea!")
- Talk about how you spend your free time

## Unità 9: L'arte… è di tutti

**Pages:** 106–117 · **Level:** B2

**Themes:** Leonardo, Michelangelo, Caravaggio, Lorenzetti; museums, exhibitions and restoration; the Mona Lisa and the stress of Louvre staff; a lost Leonardo rediscovered; newspaper headlines; inventions in Italian history.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Passive with essere, in all tenses and in the subjunctive
2. Passive with venire (simple tenses only), as an alternative to essere
3. Passive with dovere and potere ("deve essere letto", "può essere preso in prestito")
4. Passive with andare for obligation ("Il curriculum va inviato per email")
5. Andare or venire in the passive
6. Si passivante ("Si vede un panorama fantastico", "Si sono spesi molti soldi")
7. Si impersonale vs si passivante
8. Connectives to join several short facts into one sentence

**Vocabulary themes:** art (painting, sculpture, fresco, mosaic, still life, chiaroscuro, masterpiece, reproduction, chapel, painter, sculptor, architect); nouns from verbs (construction, invention, restoration, inauguration); museums (collections, guided tours, accessibility); proverbs.

**Communicative functions**

- Describe a work of art: who made it, when, where it is now
- Read and write news headlines and short reports in the passive
- State rules and obligations impersonally ("Il biglietto va convalidato")
- Express certainty, doubt and disbelief ("Ti posso garantire che…", "Non c'è dubbio che…", "Ma sul serio…?")
- Use a few common proverbs

**3rd review test (Units 7–9):** pp. 118–119.

## Unità 10: Paese che vai, problemi che trovi

**Pages:** 120–132 · **Level:** B2 · SB grammar appendix pp. 228, 230

**Themes:** burglaries and scams aimed at older people; drugs and teenagers; arrests and house arrest in a local paper; the ecomafia; Italian emigration a century ago and immigration today (a young Egyptian in Rome); the falling birth rate; insecure work, internships and mortgages.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Reported speech: statements with the main verb in the present or the past, and the changes of tense this brings
2. Changes of person, time and place words (domani → il giorno dopo, oggi → quel giorno, qui → lì)
3. Reported speech with the subjunctive and the conditional (future in the past: "ha detto che sarebbe venuto")
4. Reported questions (chiese se…, mi hanno chiesto chi…)
5. Reported commands with di + infinitive ("Mi ha detto di andare")
6. Hypothetical sentences in reported speech
7. Reported to direct speech (rebuilding an interview)
8. Connectives to join sentences

**Vocabulary themes:** crime (theft, burglar alarm, security cameras, police station, reporting a crime, scam, fake technician, loot, house arrest, drug dealing, sentence); drugs and addiction; the mafia; migration (emigrant, immigrant, integration, racism, xenophobia, homesickness); demography and work (birth rate, unemployment, internship, temporary contract, precarious work, mortgage).

**Communicative functions**

- Pass on what someone said, asked or told you to do
- Tell a news story about a crime or a social problem
- Talk about migration, integration and homesickness
- Show indifference or dismiss something ("E allora?", "E con ciò?", "Non mi interessa affatto", "Me ne infischio")
- React to outrageous news ("Che faccia tosta!", "È il colmo!")

## Unità 11: Che bello leggere!

**Pages:** 133–143 · **Level:** B2 · SB pp. 166–167; grammar appendix p. 230

**Themes:** books, reading and book reviews; audiobooks (an interview with a novelist); personality and character; star signs, horoscopes and why people read them; public signs and instructions.

**Grammar, in order**

1. Gerund (present) to say how, why, when or on what condition ("Studiando molto, ho passato tutti gli esami")
2. Rewriting clauses with poiché, se, mentre, anche se as a gerund
3. Past gerund (avendo letto, essendo partito)
4. Gerund with pronouns attached (parlandone, conoscendolo)
5. Infinitive for signs, instructions and orders ("Non fumare", "Spingere")
6. Present and past infinitive (dopo aver mangiato, credo di aver capito, da vedere)
7. Present participle used as a noun or adjective (amante, passante, cantante, interessante)
8. Past participle in absolute clauses ("Finita la lezione, …") and as an adjective
9. Choosing among gerund, infinitive and participle
10. Altered nouns: -ino, -etto, -ello (small), -one (big), -accio (bad), and false alterations (bambino, magazzino)

**Vocabulary themes:** books (author, protagonist, plot, novel, short story, review, publisher, crime fiction); personality adjectives (punctual, generous, rational, faithful, patient, optimistic, romantic, stubborn, eccentric, impulsive, reliable, independent); horoscope language; instructions and prohibitions.

**Communicative functions**

- Talk about a book you've read and write a short review
- Describe someone's character
- Read signs and instructions, and give written instructions
- Say how or why something happened in one compact sentence
- Use everyday colloquial reactions ("Meno male!", "Mannaggia!", "In che senso?", "Ho un vuoto di memoria", "Ti facevo più…")
- Make a word smaller, bigger or worse ("un regalino", "una giornataccia")

**4th review test (Units 10–11):** pp. 144–145 (reported speech, gerund, infinitive and participle, altered nouns).

**General final test:** pp. 146–150 (joining sentences, cloze texts, horoscope matching, reading comprehension, a short advice letter to write). **Board game, Units 0–11:** pp. 154–155.

## Order and pacing

- **The subjunctive arrives in Unit 5 (pp. 57–69):** present and past together, with all the usual triggers (opinion, emotion, impersonal phrases), the conjunctions and the indefinites in one unit. The **imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive** follow two units later, in **Unit 7 (pp. 82–91)**, with the sequence of tenses. From then on the subjunctive is in every unit: in hypotheticals (8), in the passive (9) and in reported speech (10).
- **The conditional is not taught again:** it's A2 material (1b Unit 11) and only gets a review exercise in "Prima di… cominciare". It comes back as the result clause of hypotheticals in Unit 8 and as the future in the past in reported speech in Unit 10.
- **The hypothetical period is Unit 8 (pp. 92–105):** all three types in one unit, type 1 first, then 2, then 3, then mixed past/present. Hypotheticals in reported speech come in Unit 10.
- **The passive is Unit 9 (pp. 106–117):** essere, venire, andare, with modals, and si passivante, all together.
- **Reported speech is Unit 10 (pp. 120–132):** statements, then questions, then commands, then hypotheticals.
- **Other milestones:** combined pronouns open the book (Unit 1); the full relative-pronoun system is Unit 2; stare + gerund / stare per + infinitive come in Unit 2 as a side topic; comparisons and superlatives are Unit 3; the **passato remoto** and trapassato remoto are Unit 4; the **formal Lei imperative** is Unit 6; the implicit moods (gerund, infinitive, participle) close the book in Unit 11.
- **Reviews:** four review tests, one after every three units (pp. 44–45, 80–81, 118–119, 144–145), plus a general test (pp. 146–150). Each test is mostly the newest grammar, with a few exercises recycling earlier units.
- **Pace:** 10–14 workbook pages per unit, and almost every unit is built around one new verb mood or tense. Since the subjunctive gets two units (5 and 7), the B2 half of the course is in effect a long subjunctive course: Units 7–10 all depend on it.
- **Vocabulary:** each unit has one or two topic areas, and they move from personal life (university, money, travel, health) in the B1 half to society (environment, technology, art, crime, migration, literature) in the B2 half.

## What B1–B2 adds beyond A2

- The rest of the pronoun system: combined pronouns, ne with them, every position, participle agreement, and the full use of ci
- A complete set of relative pronouns (il quale, cui with prepositions, il cui, chi, quello che / ciò che), so sentences can be joined instead of strung together
- Full comparisons and superlatives, with the irregular forms
- A literary past (passato remoto, trapassato remoto) for reading history, biography and fiction, and for understanding speakers from the south
- The **subjunctive** in all four tenses, with its triggers, its conjunctions and the sequence of tenses: the biggest new structure
- The formal **Lei imperative**, so the learner can give and follow instructions politely
- **Hypotheticals** of all three types, for imagining, regretting and setting conditions
- The **passive** (essere, venire, andare, si passivante), for news, rules and history
- **Reported speech**, for passing on what others said, asked or ordered
- Implicit clauses with the gerund, infinitive and participle, and word formation (adverbs in -mente, altered nouns)
- New settings beyond daily survival: university, banks, job hunting, hotels, the doctor, the opera, the housing market, the environment, technology, art and museums, crime and social problems, books
- New functions: formal emails and cover letters, job interviews, contradicting and arguing, expressing opinions, hopes and doubts, reassuring, congratulating, showing indifference, hypothesising, reporting
