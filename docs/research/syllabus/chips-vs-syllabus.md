## Fuori's beginner chips against the A1–A2 books

Josh's 10 beginner chips (`tests/experiments/missions-it.json`) checked against *Nuovissimo Progetto italiano* 1a (A1) and 1B (A2), and against the function map from *Italian Grammar You Really Need to Know*.

| Fuori chip | Where the book teaches it | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greet and say goodbye | Intro, Unit 1 (formal and informal, times of day) | A1, first week | Matches. Unit 1 pairs it with *tu / Lei* from day one. |
| Introduce yourself | Intro, Unit 1 (name, nationality, why and how long you're here, where you live) | A1, first week | Matches closely: "Sono qui da…", "Abito in…" are Unit 1 frames. "Ask her phone number" needs numbers to 2,000 and contact words (Units 2–3). |
| Order food and drink | Unit 4 (bar), Unit 6 (full restaurant meal, A2) | A1 late | The book teaches it at the half-way point; Fuori needs it on day one. Fine as long as *vorrei* is a fixed phrase, which is how the book treats it too. |
| Ask the price and pay | Unit 3 (prices), Unit 9 (ways to pay, discounts; A2) | A1 / A2 | "Can I pay by card?" (*Si può pagare con la carta?*) is A1 as a chunk. |
| Buy at the market | Unit 8 (deli counter, etti, chili, partitive *ne*; A2) | **A2** | The most advanced chip on the list. "Two etti of cheese" and the seller's "Quanto ne vuole?" need *ne* and quantities, taught in 1B. Keep it, but expect the seller's side to be the hard part. |
| Ask for directions | Unit 1 as a set phrase ("Scusa, per andare…?"); Unit 10 in full (imperative; A2) | A1 to ask, **A2** to understand | Asking is easy; understanding "giri a destra, poi prenda la seconda…" needs the imperative. Characters should keep directions short and point. |
| Say what you don't understand | Not a unit; classroom phrases in the intro, "I don't know / I'm not sure" in Unit 3 | A1 | Fuori-specific and right to put first. Nothing in the book contradicts it. |
| Talk about time | Unit 2 (time, days), Unit 3 (opening and closing times, months) | A1 | Matches. |
| Get around | Unit 1 (bus, "per andare…"), Unit 5 (train tickets, platforms, changes) | A1 | Matches. "Ask the driver to tell you your stop" (*Mi può dire quando scendere?*) is a stretch for A1; fine as a chunk. |
| Make a small plan | Unit 2 (invite, accept, decline, arrange a day) | A1 | Matches; Unit 2 is where the book first lets the learner arrange something. |

### What the books' A1 has that the chips don't

- **Free time and likes** (Unit 2: *mi piace*, hobbies). The most natural small talk with any character.
- **Home and address** (Unit 2): fits Fuori's newcomer who has just moved in.
- **Keeping in touch** (Unit 3): phone numbers, messages; already half in "Ask her phone number".
- **The weekend, in the past** (Unit 4, *passato prossimo*): "What did you do yesterday?" is something every regular will ask.
- **Weather and travel plans** (Unit 5).
- **Describing people** (Unit 1).

Candidates for new beginner chips: *Talk about what you like*, *Where you live*, *What you did*, *The weather*. The first two are A1 from week one; *What you did* is late A1 (the first past tense).

### Order

The book and Fuori agree on the first steps (greet, introduce, numbers, time) and differ on the café: the book delays ordering to Unit 4, while Fuori's world starts there. The function map's "first week in Italy" order (ask for things, prices and numbers, personal information, location, availability, permission, wants, likes, time) is closer to Fuori's needs than the textbook's classroom order.

Grammar that Fuori characters will produce early but the books treat as A2: the imperative (directions, "prego, si sieda"), *ne* (market), object pronouns (*lo prendo*). Characters can use these, but the Beginner block should keep them to short, guessable lines.
