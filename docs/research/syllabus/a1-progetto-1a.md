# Nuovissimo Progetto italiano 1a (A1): syllabus summary

A summary of the book's progression, for Fuori's own syllabus. Written in our own words; the Italian phrases are ordinary everyday chunks, not copied dialogue. Page numbers are the printed book pages. In the CSVs, unit 0 = Unità introduttiva.

## Unità introduttiva: Benvenuti!

**Pages:** Student book pp. 5–14 · Workbook pp. 95–100

**What the learner can do**

- Spell a word or name letter by letter
- Greet someone informally and introduce yourself or another person
- Say your nationality and ask someone theirs
- Ask and say a name and an age
- Count from 0 to 30

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Ciao, io sono… | Hi, I'm… |
| Mi chiamo… | My name is… |
| Come ti chiami? | What's your name? |
| Piacere! | Nice to meet you! |
| E tu? | And you? |
| Questo è… / Questa è… | This is… (introducing someone) |
| Si chiama… | His/her name is… |
| Sono italiano / italiana. | I'm Italian. |
| Sei spagnolo? | Are you Spanish? |
| Quanti anni hai? | How old are you? |
| Ho trent'anni. | I'm thirty. |
| Come si scrive? | How do you spell it? |
| Chi è? | Who is it? / Who's that? |
| Ecco le chiavi! | Here are the keys! |
| Davvero? | Really? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Alphabet and spelling
2. Pronunciation: c/g (hard and soft), s/sc, gn/gl/z, double consonants
3. Nouns: masculine/feminine, singular/plural (-o/-i, -a/-e, -e/-i)
4. Adjectives in -o/-a and agreement with the noun
5. Subject pronouns; present of essere
6. Definite article (il, lo, l', la, i, gli, le)
7. Numbers 0–10
8. Present of avere; chiamarsi (io, tu, lui/lei)
9. Numbers 11–30

**Vocabulary themes:** Italian words known abroad (food, coffee, music, fashion); nationalities; family members; everyday objects (keys, bag, book, car); numbers.

**Settings and situations:** First-day classroom introductions; meeting friends; a street and a bus ('is this the bus for the centre?'); at home looking for keys.

## Unità 1: Un nuovo inizio

**Pages:** Student book pp. 15–28 · Workbook pp. 101–108

**What the learner can do**

- Answer the phone and ask how someone is
- Greet and say goodbye at different times of day, formally and informally
- Ask for and give simple information: how to get somewhere, where someone is from, why and how long they are here, where they live
- Switch between tu and the polite Lei
- Describe a person's looks and character
- Name Italian regions and major cities

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Pronto? | Hello? (on the phone) |
| Come stai? / Come sta? | How are you? (informal / formal) |
| Bene, grazie. E tu? | Fine, thanks. And you? |
| Scusa, per andare in centro? | Excuse me, how do I get to the centre? |
| Prendi il 22 e scendi… | Take the 22 and get off… |
| Di dove sei? | Where are you from? |
| Sono di… | I'm from… |
| Sei qui per lavoro? | Are you here for work? |
| Sono qui per studiare l'italiano. | I'm here to study Italian. |
| Da quanto tempo sei qui? | How long have you been here? |
| Dove abiti? | Where do you live? |
| Scusi, sa dov'è…? | Excuse me, do you know where … is? |
| Parli molto bene l'italiano! | You speak Italian very well! |
| Com'è? | What's he/she like? |
| È alta e simpatica. | She's tall and nice. |
| A presto! | See you soon! |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Present tense of regular verbs: -are, -ere, -ire, including -isc- verbs (finire, capire, preferire)
2. Indefinite article (un, uno, una, un')
3. Adjectives in -e (same form for masculine and feminine)
4. Formal address with Lei (third person singular)
5. Simple prepositions in everyday chunks: di, a, in, da, per (sono di…, abito a/in…, sono qui da…, per studiare)

**Vocabulary themes:** Starting a new job; city transport (metro, bus, stop); greetings by time of day; hair, eyes, height, build; character adjectives; parts of the face; regions and cities of Italy.

**Settings and situations:** A phone call before a first day at work; a newspaper office; text messages arranging a pizza; a bus stop where a newcomer asks the way; a polite exchange with a stranger in the street.

## Unità 2: Tempo libero

**Pages:** Student book pp. 29–42 · Workbook pp. 109–116

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about free time and what you like doing
- Invite someone, and accept or turn down an invitation
- Ask permission, say what you can, want or have to do
- Describe a flat or house; ask for and give an address
- Count to 2,000 and use ordinals (floors)
- Say the day of the week and arrange a day
- Ask and tell the time

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Cosa fai nel tempo libero? | What do you do in your free time? |
| Mi piace… / Non mi piace… | I like… / I don't like… |
| Vieni con noi? | Are you coming with us? |
| Che ne dici di…? | How about…? |
| Perché non andiamo…? | Why don't we go…? |
| Volentieri! | I'd love to! |
| Mi dispiace, non posso. | Sorry, I can't. |
| Ho già un impegno. | I already have plans. |
| Magari un'altra volta. | Maybe another time. |
| Scusi, posso entrare? | Excuse me, may I come in? |
| Può ripetere, per favore? | Could you repeat that, please? |
| Sei libero sabato? | Are you free on Saturday? |
| Abito al primo piano. | I live on the first floor. |
| Che ore sono? | What time is it? |
| Sono le tre e mezzo. | It's half past three. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Irregular present: andare, venire, dare, sapere, stare, uscire, fare (and spelling in giocare/pagare)
2. Modal verbs potere, volere, dovere + infinitive
3. Numbers 30–2,000; ordinal numbers
4. Simple prepositions a, in, da, con, per (a casa / in centro / da Marco / in metro / a piedi)
5. Days of the week (lunedì = next Monday, il lunedì = every Monday)
6. Telling the time (sono le…, è l'una, mezzogiorno, e un quarto, meno venti)

**Vocabulary themes:** Hobbies and sports; going out (cinema, theatre, concerts, exhibitions); rooms and features of a flat; rent and floors; days and times; urban transport and tickets (tram, metro, tabaccheria, newsstand).

**Settings and situations:** Interviews about free time; invitations to the beach, a club, the theatre; a phone call about a new flat on the edge of the city; a weekly planner; clocks; buying and stamping a bus ticket.

## Unità 3: In contatto

**Pages:** Student book pp. 43–56 · Workbook pp. 119–128

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about ways of keeping in touch (messages, email, video calls, post)
- Ask and give opening and closing times
- Say you're unsure or don't know
- Say where things are in a room
- Say who something belongs to
- Thank someone and reply to thanks
- Use months, seasons, big numbers and prices
- Write a short informal email or letter; know common texting and tech words

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Che succede? | What's going on? |
| A che ora apre / chiude? | What time does it open / close? |
| È aperto dalle 9 alle 13. | It's open from 9 to 1. |
| Non so. | I don't know. |
| Non sono sicuro. | I'm not sure. |
| Non credo. | I don't think so. |
| Dov'è…? | Where is…? |
| È accanto alla posta. | It's next to the post office. |
| C'è… / Ci sono… | There is… / There are… |
| Di chi è? | Whose is it? |
| È mio / È tuo. | It's mine / It's yours. |
| Grazie mille! | Thanks a lot! |
| Prego. / Di niente. | You're welcome. / Not at all. |
| Figurati! | Don't mention it! (informal) |
| Quanto costa? | How much is it? |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Articulated prepositions (al, nel, dal, del, sul…)
2. Generic vs specific places: in banca vs alla Banca…, a teatro vs al teatro…
3. Partitive article (dei panini, delle amiche, del latte)
4. Place expressions: davanti a, dietro, sopra, sotto, accanto a, vicino a, tra, intorno a, dentro
5. C'è / ci sono
6. Possessives mio, tuo, suo (with article; casa mia)
7. Months and seasons; numbers 2,000–1,000,000

**Vocabulary themes:** Phones, email, chat, video calls, parcels; computer terms (download, share, folder); shops and services (pharmacy, bank, post office, library); living-room furniture; months and seasons; letter openings and closings.

**Settings and situations:** An office where the server is down; a café with wi-fi next to the post office; meeting outside the university; asking about train times and opening hours; a furnished living room; an email to friends about a trip.

## Unità 4: Buon fine settimana!

**Pages:** Student book pp. 57–72 · Workbook pp. 129–138

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about what you did at the weekend
- Tell a short story in order (first, then, in the end)
- Place events in the past and give a date (ago, last…, born in…)
- Order food and drinks at a bar
- Say what you prefer

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Come hai passato il fine settimana? | How was your weekend? |
| Che cosa hai fatto di bello? | Did you do anything nice? |
| Sono andato al cinema. | I went to the cinema. |
| Sono rimasta a casa. | I stayed at home. |
| Peccato! | What a shame! |
| Prima… poi… alla fine… | First… then… in the end… |
| Che cosa è successo? | What happened? |
| Due giorni fa. | Two days ago. |
| Quando sei nato? | When were you born? |
| Cosa prendi? | What are you having? |
| Per me un caffè, grazie. | A coffee for me, thanks. |
| Vorrei un cornetto. | I'd like a croissant. |
| Ho fame. / Ho sete. | I'm hungry. / I'm thirsty. |
| Preferisco il tè al caffè. | I prefer tea to coffee. |
| Non sono mai stato a… | I've never been to… |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Past participle, regular (-ato, -uto, -ito)
2. Passato prossimo with avere and with essere (agreement with essere)
3. Choosing essere or avere (movement/state verbs vs verbs with an object)
4. Irregular participles (fatto, preso, visto, detto, scritto, letto, bevuto, rimasto, venuto, nato…)
5. Ci = there (ci sono andato)
6. Adverbs with passato prossimo: già, mai, appena, ancora, sempre, più
7. Time expressions: fa, scorso, nel 2002, il 20 marzo, a giugno
8. Modal verbs in the passato prossimo (ho dovuto lavorare, sono potuto venire)
9. Story connectors: prima, poi, dopo, più tardi, alla fine

**Vocabulary themes:** Weekend activities; cinema, concerts, exhibitions, museums; university life (lecture, canteen); bar menu (coffee types, cornetto, tramezzino, juices, beer, aperitivo); dates and time words.

**Settings and situations:** Monday chat with a colleague; cinema and a café where the waiter mixes up the order; a food festival; a police officer asking a student about his day; a museum's concert season; ordering at a bar counter or table.

## Unità 5: Tempo di vacanze

**Pages:** Student book pp. 73–86 · Workbook pp. 139–148

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about plans, predictions, promises and guesses
- Check in at the airport
- Buy a train ticket and ask about times, platforms and changes
- Say what will happen after something else is done
- Talk about the weather and plan a day out around it
- Wish people a good trip and happy holidays; talk about Christmas and New Year

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Cosa farai a Capodanno? | What will you do for New Year's? |
| Buon viaggio! | Have a good trip! |
| Un biglietto per Firenze. | A ticket to Florence. |
| Andata e ritorno? | Return? |
| A che ora parte il prossimo treno? | When does the next train leave? |
| Da quale binario parte? | Which platform does it leave from? |
| Bisogna cambiare a Bologna. | You have to change at Bologna. |
| È libero questo posto? | Is this seat free? |
| Che tempo fa? | What's the weather like? |
| Fa freddo. / Fa caldo. | It's cold. / It's hot. |
| Piove. / C'è il sole. | It's raining. / It's sunny. |
| Domani pioverà. | It'll rain tomorrow. |
| Se farà bel tempo, andremo al mare. | If the weather's nice, we'll go to the beach. |
| Sarà in piscina. | He's probably at the pool. |
| Appena avrò finito, ti chiamo. | As soon as I've finished, I'll call you. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Future simple, regular (including cercherò, mangerò spelling)
2. Future simple, irregular (sarò, avrò, farò, andrò, verrò, potrò, vorrò…)
3. Uses of the future: plans, predictions, promises, guesses (sarà…)
4. First conditional: se + future, future
5. Future perfect after quando, dopo che, (non) appena
6. Weather expressions (fa caldo/freddo, c'è il sole, piove, nevica, tira vento)

**Vocabulary themes:** Airport words (luggage, boarding, gate); train travel (ticket office, platform, carriage, seat, inspector, one-way/return, regional vs high-speed trains); weather and forecasts; Christmas and New Year customs (nativity scene, panettone, presents, tombola, Christmas markets); holiday types.

**Settings and situations:** Airport check-in desk; train station ticket office and platform; a phone call about picking someone up; leaving keys before a ski week; a weather forecast; Christmas in Italy.

## Order and pacing

- **Unità introduttiva (pp. 5–14):** sounds, spelling, noun/adjective endings, articles, essere and avere, numbers to 30. Only the io/tu/lui-lei forms of chiamarsi; no other verbs are conjugated yet.
- **Unit 1 (pp. 15–28):** the whole present tense of regular verbs arrives at once, with the indefinite article and the polite Lei. Simple prepositions show up as set phrases (sono di…, abito in…, sono qui da…) before they are taught.
- **Unit 2 (pp. 29–42):** irregular presents and the three modals; telling the time, days, and numbers to 2,000. Simple prepositions are taught here. This is the first unit where the learner can arrange something (invite, accept, refuse).
- **Unit 3 (pp. 43–56):** articulated prepositions, the partitive, c'è/ci sono and the first possessives (mio/tuo/suo only; the full set is deferred to the 1b volume). Months, seasons, prices, numbers to a million.
- **Unit 4 (pp. 57–72):** the **passato prossimo** is the first past tense, at about the halfway point (book p. 59). It comes with essere/avere choice, irregular participles, ci, adverb placement and modals in the past. Ordering at the bar also comes this late, so a learner can't order in a café until Unit 4.
- **Unit 5 (pp. 73–86):** **future simple** and **future perfect**, the first conditional (se + future), weather and train travel.
- **Not yet in 1a:** imperfect, reflexive verbs (only in a reference note), direct/indirect object pronouns, the full set of possessives, the conditional mood (vorrei is learned as a fixed phrase), the imperative (only fixed forms like scusi, senti, prendi… scendi…).
- **Pace:** fast. Each unit is about 14 pages with roughly 8 pages of workbook. Two review tests, after Unit 2 and after Unit 5, mark the natural checkpoints. Free-time talk and invitations come before ordering at a bar; directions are only in the form "Scusa, per andare…? / prendi… e scendi…".
