# Italian Prepositions: A Summary

Source: *Le preposizioni italiane*, Francesca Felici & Juana Vélez (Italiano al caffè, free guide, 2020). Summarized in our own words; all examples are ours.

Prepositions are short, fixed words (they never change form on their own) that link a noun, pronoun or verb to the rest of the sentence. Each one has several jobs, and swapping one changes the meaning: *Vengo da Milano* (I'm coming from Milan) vs *Vengo a Milano* (I'm coming to Milan). There are rules for the common cases, but many uses simply have to be learned as phrases.

## The simple prepositions

| Prep | Main uses | Examples (Italian / English) |
|---|---|---|
| **di** | origin with *essere*; possession; topic; author; part of the day; partitive "some" (with article); after verbs like *decidere, pensare, finire* + infinitive | *Sono di Torino* / I'm from Turin · *la bici di Anna* / Anna's bike · *Parliamo di cinema* / We're talking about film · *Ho deciso di restare* / I've decided to stay |
| **a** | cities, towns, small islands; going somewhere to do something; exact time; age; holidays; distance; "to" someone (indirect object); fixed places (*a casa, a scuola*, *al bar*); style of a dish; after *vicino, davanti, uguale, simile* | *Abito a Napoli* / I live in Naples · *Vado a prendere un caffè* / I'm going to get a coffee · *Ci vediamo alle sette* / See you at seven · *Scrivo a Marco* / I'm writing to Marco |
| **da** | from (starting point); at or to someone's place or shop; since / for (ongoing time); start of a range (*da… a*); purpose of a thing (*occhiali da sole*); "by" in the passive; "different from" | *Arrivo da Firenze* / I'm arriving from Florence · *Ceno da Lucia* / I'm having dinner at Lucia's · *Vado dal medico* / I'm going to the doctor's · *Sono qui da un mese* / I've been here for a month |
| **in** | countries, regions, large islands, continents; rooms; transport (singular, no article); shops ending in *-ia* and a set list (*in banca, in centro, in ufficio*); year (with article); time taken to do something | *Vivo in Italia* / I live in Italy · *Andiamo in treno* / We're going by train · *Sono in cucina* / I'm in the kitchen · *Ci arrivo in dieci minuti* / I'll get there in ten minutes (it takes ten) |
| **con** | with someone or something; tool; transport with an article; description (*una stanza con balcone*) | *Esco con Giulia* / I'm going out with Giulia · *Pago con la carta* / I'll pay by card · *Vado con la macchina di Paolo* / I'm going in Paolo's car · *Un caffè con latte* / A coffee with milk |
| **su** | on, on top of; about (books, talks, documentaries) | *Le chiavi sono sul tavolo* / The keys are on the table · *Un libro su Roma* / A book about Rome · *Siediti su questa sedia* / Sit on this chair |
| **per** | purpose ("to, in order to"); "for" someone; duration (finished stretch); destination with *partire*; cause; "by" a time; through | *Studio per lavorare in Italia* / I study to work in Italy · *Questo è per te* / This is for you · *Ho abitato lì per due anni* / I lived there for two years · *Parto per Roma* / I'm leaving for Rome |
| **tra / fra** | (same word; pick whichever sounds better, e.g. *tra fratelli*, *fra tre*) between; in (time from now); a stretch of time or distance ahead | *Arrivo tra cinque minuti* / I'll be there in five minutes · *Tra le due e le tre* / Between two and three · *La farmacia è fra il bar e la banca* / The pharmacy is between the bar and the bank |

## Articulated prepositions

Five prepositions merge with the definite article that follows them into one word. The article depends on the noun (gender, number, first letter), so knowing *il / lo / l' / i / gli / la / le* comes first.

| | il | lo | l' | i | gli | la | le |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **di** | del | dello | dell' | dei | degli | della | delle |
| **a** | al | allo | all' | ai | agli | alla | alle |
| **da** | dal | dallo | dall' | dai | dagli | dalla | dalle |
| **in** | nel | nello | nell' | nei | negli | nella | nelle |
| **su** | sul | sullo | sull' | sui | sugli | sulla | sulle |

*Con, per, tra, fra* don't merge in everyday Italian: *con il*, *per la*, *tra i*.

**When to merge.** Use the combined form whenever the noun needs an article:
- Most single countries take no article after *in*: *in Italia, in Francia*. Plural or modified ones do: *negli Stati Uniti, nei Paesi Bassi, nell'Italia del sud*.
- Clock times always do: *alle otto, dalle nove alle cinque*. Years too: *nel 2024*.
- Specific or described places do: *nel centro di Roma*, *nella cucina di Marta*, *al bar sotto casa*.

**When not to.** A set of everyday places goes bare: *a casa, a scuola, a letto, a teatro, a pranzo*; *in centro, in città, in banca, in ufficio, in piazza, in cucina, in vacanza*; transport *in treno, in bus*. Cities never take an article: *a Roma*.

## Classic beginner confusions

**a vs in for places.**
- City or town: **a**. *Vado a Bologna.*
- Country, region, big island: **in**. *Vado in Toscana, in Sicilia, in Spagna.*
- Shops ending in *-ia*: **in**. *in farmacia, in pasticceria, in libreria, in panetteria.*
- Many everyday venues use **a + article**: *al bar, al mercato, al ristorante, al cinema, al supermercato, alla stazione, all'aeroporto.*
- A person or professional's place: **da**. *dal medico, dal parrucchiere, da Mario* (to/at Mario's, his home or shop).
- When in doubt, these are fixed: learn them as whole chunks rather than deriving them.

**da: three meanings.**
- From: *Vengo da Milano.*
- At / to someone's place: *Sono da mia sorella.*
- Since / for, with the present tense when the action is still going: *Abito qui da maggio*, *Studio italiano da sei mesi.* English uses "have been… for", Italian uses the plain present + *da*.

**di: origin and possession.**
- "Where are you from?" uses **di** with *essere*: *Di dove sei? Sono di Seattle.* With *venire* it's **da**: *Vengo da Seattle.*
- Possession: Italian has no 's. *La casa di Paolo* (Paolo's house), *il caffè del signore*.

**per vs a for purpose.**
- After verbs of motion (*andare, venire, uscire, passare*) Italian prefers **a** + infinitive: *Vado a fare la spesa, Esco a comprare il pane.*
- Elsewhere "in order to" is **per**: *Lavoro per vivere, Ti chiamo per sapere a che ora arrivi.*
- "For a person": always **per**. *Un regalo per te.*

**Time words: tra / fra, da, per, in, fa.**
- *tra / fra* = in (future, from now): *Parto tra una settimana.* (A common error is *in una settimana*, which means "it takes a week".)
- *da* = since / for, still going: *Sono a Roma da una settimana.*
- *per* = for, a finished stretch: *Sono stato a Roma per una settimana.*
- *fa* (not a preposition, but part of the same confusion) = ago: *Sono arrivato una settimana fa.*
- *a* = clock time and holidays: *alle tre, a Natale.* Days and parts of the day need nothing: *lunedì, la sera* (or *di sera*).

## By level

**A1 first (what Fuori's beginner chips need):**
- At the bar and ordering: *al bar*, *un caffè con latte*, *per me un cornetto*, *da portare via*.
- At the market and shops: *al mercato*, *in farmacia*, *in panetteria*, *dal fornaio*; paying *con la carta*.
- Getting around: *a Roma*, *in Italia*, *in treno / in bus*, *a piedi*, *alla stazione*, *per andare a…?*, *vicino a*, *davanti a*.
- Time: *alle otto*, *dalle nove alle cinque*, *tra dieci minuti*.
- Introducing yourself: *Sono di…*, *Abito a…*, *Sono qui da…*.
- The full articulated table, used as chunks before it's understood.

**A2:** partitive *del / della* ("some bread"); *da* for "since" in all its forms; *per* for duration vs *da*; verbs with fixed prepositions (*cominciare a, finire di, pensare a, decidere di*); *su* for topics; *da* for purpose (*occhiali da sole, qualcosa da bere*).

**B1 and up:** *da* in the passive (*fatto dalla nonna*); *da* for description (*la ragazza dai capelli rossi*) and for "worth doing" (*un film da vedere*); causes with *di / da / per* (*morire di freddo*); the long tail of verb + preposition pairs.

## For Fuori

- **Learn** can teach prepositions as places first, not as a grammar table: "cities take *a*, countries take *in*, people's places take *da*", then a handful of chunks from the learner's own day (*al bar*, *in farmacia*, *dal medico*). Show the articulated table only once the learner has used several of its forms. Point out the two traps English speakers hit: Italian *da* + present for "have been doing", and *tra* (not *in*) for "in ten minutes".
- **Characters** never explain prepositions. They recast in passing, in role: learner says *Vado in Roma*, the barista answers *Ah, a Roma! Bella.* A wrong preposition rarely blocks meaning, so it's a recast, not a stop.
- **Review / Diario:** wrong prepositions are good review material because the fix is one short word in a reusable chunk; log them by type (place, time, *da*) so Learn can spot a pattern.
