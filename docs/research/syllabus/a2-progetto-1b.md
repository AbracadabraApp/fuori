# Nuovissimo Progetto italiano 1b (A2): syllabus summary

A summary of the book's progression, for Fuori's own syllabus. Written in our own words; the Italian phrases are ordinary everyday chunks, not copied dialogue. Page numbers are the printed book pages. Volume 1b continues 1a, so the units run from 6 to 11; in the CSVs the unit column uses the same numbers. Every unit ends with a one-page self-check ("Che cosa hai imparato…?"), included in the student-book ranges below.

## Unità 6: A cena fuori

**Pages:** Student book pp. 5–18 · Workbook pp. 107–116

**What the learner can do**

- Say who things belong to and talk about your family
- Give someone simple advice
- Order a full meal at a restaurant (starter, first and main course, side, dessert, drinks; how you want it cooked)
- Say what you like, don't like or would prefer
- Talk about the day's meals and snacks, and say how long something takes
- Name what's on a laid table and the basic kitchen tools and cooking verbs

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| È il mio piatto preferito. | It's my favourite dish. |
| Questa è mia sorella. / Questi sono i miei genitori. | This is my sister. / These are my parents. |
| Hai fratelli o sorelle? | Do you have brothers or sisters? |
| Di chi è questa bici? – È di mio fratello. | Whose bike is this? – It's my brother's. |
| Prendi il risotto, è buonissimo! | Have the risotto, it's really good! |
| Scusi, possiamo ordinare? | Excuse me, can we order? |
| Come primo prendo… e come secondo… | For the first course I'll have… and for the main… |
| Cosa ci consiglia? | What do you recommend? |
| La bistecca ben cotta, per favore. | The steak well done, please. |
| Da bere? – Una bottiglia d'acqua frizzante. | To drink? – A bottle of sparkling water. |
| Mi piacciono le lasagne. | I like lasagne. |
| Non mi piace affatto. | I don't like it at all. |
| Facciamo uno spuntino? | Shall we have a snack? |
| Non faccio mai colazione. | I never have breakfast. |
| Quanto ci vuole? – Ci metto dieci minuti. | How long does it take? – It takes me ten minutes. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. The full set of possessives (mio… loro), with the article; Lei form (il Suo)
2. Possessives with family nouns: no article in the singular (mia madre), article in the plural (i miei zii) and always with loro
3. Mi piace / mi piacciono; vorrei as a polite request
4. Volerci and metterci (how long something takes in general / for a person)
5. Quello and bello before a noun (quel, quello, quell', quei, quegli; bel, begli…)

**Vocabulary themes:** Family members and relatives; restaurant menu (antipasti, primi, secondi, contorni, dolci, wine and drinks); breakfast foods and the day's meals (colazione, spuntino, pranzo, merenda, cena); table setting (tablecloth, napkin, cutlery, glass); cooking verbs and utensils (pot, pan, grater, colander, ladle); types of pasta; kinds of eating places (bar, paninoteca, osteria, trattoria, pizzeria, ristorante).

**Settings and situations:** A mother quizzing her son about dinner plans and pushing the dishes she likes; looking at birthday-party photos and explaining who is who; ordering at a Milanese restaurant; two colleagues talking about breakfast before the lunch break; a reply email recommending local dishes and cheap places to eat; the history of pasta and pizza.

## Unità 7: Al cinema

**Pages:** Student book pp. 19–32 · Workbook pp. 117–128

**What the learner can do**

- Describe and tell about past events, setting the scene and saying what happened
- Talk about memories and past habits
- Put several past actions in order, including one that happened before another
- Talk about films: genres, plot, actors, directors, reviews
- Agree and disagree

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Che genere di film ti piace? | What kind of films do you like? |
| Non è il mio genere. | It's not my kind of thing. |
| Avevo la febbre e sono rimasta a casa. | I had a temperature and stayed at home. |
| Da piccolo andavo spesso al cinema. | As a child I often went to the cinema. |
| Ti ricordi di quella sera? | Do you remember that evening? |
| Non mi ricordo. | I don't remember. |
| Che bei tempi! | Those were the days! |
| Mentre parlavo al telefono, è arrivata lei. | While I was on the phone, she arrived. |
| Era una sera di giugno… | It was a June evening… |
| Quando sono arrivato, il film era già cominciato. | When I arrived, the film had already started. |
| Cosa pensi di questo regista? | What do you think of this director? |
| Secondo me, è bravissimo. | I think he's really good. |
| Sono d'accordo con te. | I agree with you. |
| Non sono d'accordo. / Non è vero! | I don't agree. / That's not true! |
| Hai ragione. | You're right. |
| Che ridere! | How funny! |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Imperfetto: regular verbs (parlavo, vivevo, dormivo)
2. Imperfetto: irregular verbs (essere, bere, dire, fare; also tradurre, porre)
3. Uses of the imperfetto: habits, descriptions, actions in progress, two things happening at once
4. Imperfetto or passato prossimo (an ongoing action interrupted by a completed one; finished and sequential actions)
5. Modal verbs in the imperfetto vs passato prossimo (dovevo andare vs sono dovuto andare)
6. Trapassato prossimo: form (imperfetto of essere/avere + participle) and use (the earlier of two past actions)
7. Expressions for agreeing and disagreeing; phrases for talking about memories (mi ricordo…, ricordo che…, non dimenticherò mai…)

**Vocabulary themes:** Film genres (comedy, action, horror, adventure, drama); cinema words (director, cast, plot, title, reviews, awards, soundtrack, trailer); feeling ill (fever); memories, childhood and university days; adjectives for people and stories (strange, nervous, exaggerated, complicated, funny); Italian cinema history (neorealism, commedia all'italiana, spaghetti western).

**Settings and situations:** A friend reporting the evening at the cinema where her companion fell asleep; old photos from a university party; a man recalling how he met someone; a picture story of a bad day; three friends who can't agree on a film and end up at a restaurant; short exchanges about directors and actors; great Italian directors and actors.

## Unità 8: Fare la spesa

**Pages:** Student book pp. 33–48 · Workbook pp. 129–139

**What the learner can do**

- Do the shopping at a supermarket and say why you choose a product
- Express joy, regret and disappointment
- Buy food at a deli counter, giving quantities (etti, chili, litri, pacchi)
- Offer help, and accept or turn it down
- Say what you have and how much of something there is
- Know which shop sells what, and the names of packages and containers

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Non la trovo. | I can't find it. |
| Lo prendiamo? | Shall we get it? |
| Cos'altro ci manca? | What else do we need? |
| Lo so. / Non lo sapevo. | I know. / I didn't know. |
| Che peccato! | What a shame! |
| Che bella notizia! | What great news! |
| Accidenti! | Oh no! / Damn! |
| Vorrei due etti di prosciutto. | I'd like 200 grams of ham. |
| Quanto ne vuole? | How much would you like? |
| Ne prendo un chilo. | I'll take a kilo. |
| Desidera altro? – No, basta così. | Anything else? – No, that's all. |
| Dove li hai comprati? | Where did you buy them? |
| Ti posso aiutare? / Vuoi una mano? | Can I help you? / Do you want a hand? |
| Grazie, sei molto gentile! | Thanks, that's very kind of you! |
| Grazie, faccio da solo. | Thanks, I can manage. |
| Ce l'hai? – Sì, ce l'ho. | Have you got it? – Yes, I have. |
| Ce n'è poco. | There's not much left. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Direct object pronouns (mi, ti, lo, la, La, ci, vi, li, le), placed before the verb
2. Lo so / lo sapevo / lo saprò (lo standing for a whole sentence)
3. Partitive ne with quantities (ne prendo due etti; non ne conosco nessuno) vs lo/la/li/le for the whole
4. Direct pronouns in compound tenses and participle agreement (l'ho vista, li ho comprati, ne ho mangiate poche)
5. Sapere and conoscere: imperfetto vs passato prossimo (lo sapevo / l'ho saputo; la conoscevo / l'ho conosciuta)
6. Direct pronouns with modal verbs (la devo finire = devo finirla)
7. Ce l'ho / ce li ho; ce n'è / ce ne sono

**Vocabulary themes:** Supermarket products (fruit, biscuits, pasta, dairy, coffee); diet and calories; quantities and weights (etto, chilo, litro, dozzina, paio); packaging (pacco, confezione, bottiglia, lattina, tubetto, vasetto, scatoletta, bustina); shops and shopkeepers (pasticceria, fruttivendolo, fioraio, macellaio, alimentari, libreria, farmacia); baking ingredients (flour, sugar, yeast, oil); historic markets and DOP products.

**Settings and situations:** A father and daughter at the supermarket after she forgets the list; unpacking the shopping at home; short reactions to good and bad news (a strike, a concert, no milk); buying ham and milk at a deli; gossip about a new pastry shop and a party invitation; a grandmother asking her granddaughter to run to the shop; two friends checking ingredients for a carrot cake; historic markets in Palermo, Turin, Genoa, Livorno and Venice.

## Unità 9: Andiamo a fare spese

**Pages:** Student book pp. 49–64 · Workbook pp. 142–152

**What the learner can do**

- Describe your daily routine
- Talk about meeting and getting on with people (reciprocal actions)
- Talk about clothes: colour, size, shoe size, style, fabric and pattern
- Shop for clothes: try something on, ask about price, discounts and ways to pay
- Ask for and give an opinion
- Make general statements about what people do or need to do (impersonal forms)
- Tell the story of a meeting; describe what someone was wearing

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Il sabato mi sveglio tardi. | On Saturdays I wake up late. |
| Ci vediamo alle otto. | See you at eight. |
| Ci siamo divertiti un sacco. | We had a great time. |
| Ho visto una giacca in vetrina. | I saw a jacket in the window. |
| La posso provare? | Can I try it on? |
| Che taglia porta? – La 48. | What size are you? – A 48. |
| Che numero porta? | What shoe size are you? |
| C'è anche in blu? | Does it come in blue too? |
| È un po' stretta. Ha una taglia più grande? | It's a bit tight. Do you have a bigger size? |
| Quanto costa? C'è uno sconto? | How much is it? Is there a discount? |
| La prendo. | I'll take it. |
| Posso pagare con la carta? | Can I pay by card? |
| Si può pagare in contanti? | Can you pay cash? |
| Che ne pensi? / Che te ne pare? | What do you think? |
| Lo trovo un po' caro. | I think it's a bit expensive. |
| Bisogna provarlo prima. | You need to try it on first. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Reflexive verbs in the present (mi sveglio, ti alzi, si veste…)
2. Reciprocal verbs (ci vediamo, si conoscono, vi amate)
3. Reflexive verbs in compound tenses: always with essere and agreement (mi sono svegliato, ci siamo divertiti)
4. Reflexive verbs with modals (mi devo fermare = devo fermarmi; essere/avere choice in the past)
5. Impersonal si (si paga, si può); ci si for reflexive verbs (ci si ferma); plural adjective after si è (si è felici)
6. Impersonal expressions: bisogna, è possibile, è necessario, è meglio, è utile/inutile, è importante, è facile/difficile + infinitive

**Vocabulary themes:** Morning routine and weekend habits; clothes and accessories (jacket, skirt, trousers, shirt, coat, jumper, belt, hat, scarf, gloves, heels); colours; fabrics (linen, silk, cotton, wool, leather) and patterns (stripes, checks, polka dots, plain); sizes and fit (tight, loose, long, short); prices, discounts, sales, cash, card; Italian fashion houses and "made in Italy".

**Settings and situations:** A couple planning Saturday shopping at a mall; a chat message about a cold and a cancelled tennis match; a clothes shop where a man tries on a linen-and-silk jacket and pays by card; short exchanges about whether a coat or jumper looks good; a "ten rules for the sales" article; a woman describing two thieves to the police; Benetton and Prada.

## Unità 10: Che c'è stasera in TV?

**Pages:** Student book pp. 65–80 · Workbook pp. 153–162

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about TV programmes and genres, and explain your preferences
- Borrow something, ask a favour, give an opinion, say you're sorry for someone
- Give orders, instructions and advice; forbid something
- Make a request or an invitation with the imperative
- Ask for and give directions in a town
- Know the main Italian newspapers, magazines and TV channels

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Che c'è stasera in TV? | What's on TV tonight? |
| Su che canale lo danno? | Which channel is it on? |
| Cambia canale, per favore! | Change the channel, please! |
| Non mi piacciono i reality. | I don't like reality shows. |
| Mi presti la tua macchina? | Can I borrow your car? |
| Mi fai un favore? | Can you do me a favour? |
| Mi dai una mano? | Can you give me a hand? |
| Mi dispiace, ma non ti posso aiutare. | Sorry, but I can't help you. |
| Mi sembra giusto. | That seems fair. |
| Gli ho telefonato ieri. | I phoned him yesterday. |
| Prendilo pure! | Go ahead, take it! |
| Non preoccuparti! | Don't worry! |
| Dimmi! | Tell me! |
| Scusi, per il Duomo? | Excuse me, which way to the cathedral? |
| Va' sempre dritto. | Go straight on. |
| Al primo incrocio gira a destra. | Turn right at the first junction. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Indirect object pronouns (mi, ti, gli, le, Le, ci, vi, gli/loro)
2. Indirect pronouns in compound tenses: no participle agreement (le abbiamo regalato…)
3. Mi piace / mi piacciono in the passato prossimo (mi è piaciuto, ci sono piaciuti)
4. Indirect pronouns with modals (le voglio comprare = voglio comprarle)
5. Imperative (tu, noi, voi) of regular verbs (ascolta!, vinci!, scopri!, finisci!)
6. Negative imperative (non invitare!, non perdete!)
7. Imperative with pronouns (prendilo, alzati, lasciami) and in the negative (non prenderlo = non lo prendere)
8. Irregular imperatives: va', da', di', fa', sta' (and sii, abbi) and doubled consonants with pronouns (vacci, dimmi, fallo; but dagli)

**Vocabulary themes:** TV genres (documentary, talent show, quiz, reality, soap, series, news, cartoons, talk show); TV words (channel, remote control, episode, advert, viewers, presenter, licence fee, streaming); borrowing and favours; advertising slogans; newspapers and magazines (daily, weekly, monthly, article, editorial office); street directions (junction, straight on, turn left/right, the third street).

**Settings and situations:** A brother and sister arguing over the remote; a friend's view of a talent show; short scenes of borrowing a car or a magazine and asking for a hand; a comic TV guide from ancient Rome; TV ratings; advertising slogans; lending a magazine, forwarding an email, waking someone up; asking the way around central Rome; a blindfolded treasure hunt; the Italian press and television, the Sanremo festival.

## Unità 11: A ritmo di musica

**Pages:** Student book pp. 81–94 · Workbook pp. 163–173

**What the learner can do**

- Talk about music: genres, singers, instruments, concerts
- Ask a favour and justify yourself; suggest alternatives
- Express a wish you can still fulfil, and one that didn't come true
- Ask for something politely
- Give advice (now and about the past)
- Make a guess; give your own opinion; report someone else's opinion or unconfirmed news
- Talk about the future as seen from the past (he said he would…)

**Key frames**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Cosa gli chiederesti? | What would you ask him? |
| Mi piacerebbe sapere… | I'd like to know… |
| Potresti aiutarmi? | Could you help me? |
| Ti dispiacerebbe chiudere la finestra? | Would you mind closing the window? |
| Mi porteresti con te? | Would you take me with you? |
| Al posto tuo, ci andrei. | If I were you, I'd go. |
| Dovresti ascoltarlo. | You should listen to it. |
| Faresti bene a riposarti. | You'd do well to rest. |
| Potremmo guardarlo insieme. Ti va? | We could watch it together. Fancy it? |
| Preferirei di no. | I'd rather not. |
| Avrei voglia di fare quattro passi. | I feel like going for a little walk. |
| Dovrebbe arrivare alle otto. | He should arrive at eight. |
| Secondo i giornali, sarebbe già partito. | According to the papers, he's already left. |
| L'avrei visto volentieri, ma… | I'd have loved to see it, but… |
| Avresti dovuto chiamarmi. | You should have called me. |
| Ha detto che sarebbe venuto. | He said he would come. |

**Grammar, in the order introduced**

1. Conditional (condizionale semplice): regular verbs, with -care/-gare and -ciare/-giare spelling
2. Conditional: irregular verbs (same stems as the future: sarei, avrei, farei, andrei, dovrei, potrei, vorrei, verrei, berrei…)
3. Uses of the conditional: wishes, polite requests, advice, opinions and guesses, unconfirmed news
4. Past conditional (condizionale composto): conditional of essere/avere + participle
5. Uses of the past conditional: unfulfilled wishes, advice about the past, unconfirmed past news
6. Future in the past (ha detto che sarebbe venuto)
7. Review: simple or past conditional?

**Vocabulary themes:** Music genres (pop, rock, rap, blues, R&B); the music industry (producer, hit, charts, downloads, streaming, social media, talent shows); instruments and equipment (guitar, drums, keyboard, microphone, headphones); songs (lyrics, author, singer-songwriter, tour, festival); polite requests and advice; a disastrous evening (broken TV, slow connection, moving house, traffic).

**Settings and situations:** A journalist preparing interview questions with a friend; a newcomer asking which Italian music to listen to and planning to watch the Sanremo festival together; the evening when the TV wouldn't turn on; newspaper headlines reported as rumours; advice for everyday situations (a loud phone call on a train, a friend learning a language, a wedding present); Italian singers past and present.

## Order and pacing

- **Unit 6 (pp. 5–18):** finishes the possessives that 1a only started (mio/tuo/suo) and adds the family-noun rule. Restaurant ordering arrives here, after bar ordering in 1a Unit 4; "vorrei" is still treated as a fixed phrase, and the conditional isn't taught until Unit 11. Volerci/metterci and quello/bello are short side topics.
- **Unit 7 (pp. 19–32):** the **imperfetto** arrives (second past tense), at once contrasted with the passato prossimo, then the **trapassato prossimo**. This is the unit that lets a learner tell a story with background and events. Agreeing and disagreeing comes with it.
- **Unit 8 (pp. 33–48):** **direct object pronouns** in every position (simple tenses, compound tenses with agreement, with modals), plus **ne**, ce l'ho and ce n'è. The biggest grammar load in the book, tied to everyday shopping.
- **Review test 3** (workbook pp. 140–141) closes Units 6–8.
- **Unit 9 (pp. 49–64):** **reflexive and reciprocal verbs**, which 1a only mentioned in a note, in present, past and with modals; then the **impersonal si** and impersonal expressions. Clothes shopping is the setting.
- **Unit 10 (pp. 65–80):** **indirect object pronouns** (completing the pronoun set), piacere in the past, and the **imperative** for tu/noi/voi with and without pronouns. Directions are taught properly here for the first time (1a had only "scusa, per andare…?").
- **Unit 11 (pp. 81–94):** the **conditional**, simple and past, with all its uses, and the future in the past. Polite requests ("potresti…?", "ti dispiacerebbe…?") and advice ("al posto tuo…", "dovresti…") only become productive here.
- **Review test 4** (workbook pp. 174–175) closes Units 9–11; then a final general test (pp. 176–179) and a board game (pp. 180–181). The student book also has a general self-assessment (pp. 95–96), video activities for each unit (pp. 97–102) and answers (p. 104). The grammar appendix (pp. 182–193) adds the possessive pronoun, imperfetto for polite requests (volevo…), and essere/avere/sapere imperatives.
- **Not yet in 1b:** the formal imperative for Lei (directions to a stranger use "deve andare…" or fixed forms like scusi), combined pronouns (glielo, me lo), the subjunctive, the passato remoto, and the gerund (stare + gerund appears only in a few chunks such as "sto facendo zapping").
- **Pace:** still fast. Each unit is 14–16 pages with about 10–12 pages of workbook, and nearly every unit adds a major structure. Pronouns are spread over three units (8, 9, 10), so the learner can't use the whole pronoun system until the second half.

## What 1B adds beyond 1a

- Talking about the past with background and habits (imperfetto) and with an earlier past (trapassato prossimo), not just completed events
- Pronouns: direct, indirect, reflexive, partitive ne, ce l'ho / ce n'è, and their position with modals and in compound tenses
- Reflexive verbs for daily routine and reciprocal verbs for relationships
- The imperative for giving instructions, advice, permission and directions
- The conditional for polite requests, wishes, advice, guesses and reported news; the past conditional for regrets and missed chances
- Impersonal si and expressions like bisogna / è meglio / è possibile
- New everyday settings: a full restaurant meal, the supermarket and deli counter, clothes shops, TV and the press, music, asking the way
- Social functions: agreeing and disagreeing, giving and asking opinions, offering, accepting and refusing help, borrowing, asking favours, reacting to good and bad news
