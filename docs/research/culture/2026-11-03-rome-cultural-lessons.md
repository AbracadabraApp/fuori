# Cultural lesson trial: Roma (2026-11-04)

## Items

### The quinto quarto (food) · Claudio Marini · fact judge: pass
Testaccio grew around the city slaughterhouse, which opened in the 1890s and closed in 1975. The best cuts went to the wealthy and the butchers' own sales. The workers traditionally took home the leftovers, the offal, which Romans call the quinto quarto, the fifth quarter. From those humble parts came coda alla vaccinara (oxtail stew), trippa alla romana and pajata. Testaccio's trattorie still cook them, and neighbours treat them as the real taste of the quarter.
Words: quinto quarto (fifth quarter, offal), coda alla vaccinara (Roman oxtail stew)

### Prati and the Palazzaccio (history) · Dott.ssa Silvia Bruni · fact judge: pass
Prati was open meadow near Castel Sant'Angelo until Rome became the capital of Italy in 1871. Then it was laid out as a tidy grid of streets for ministries, offices and the civil servants who came to work in them. At the edge of the district stands the huge Palazzo di Giustizia, built between 1889 and 1911. Romans call it the Palazzaccio, the big ugly palace, and the name stuck so firmly that people use it for the court even today.
Words: Palazzaccio (big ugly palace (nickname of the law courts)), ricetta (prescription, recipe)

### Gnocchi on Thursday (folklore and traditions) · Mohamed Hassan · fact judge: pass
Roman trattorie follow a weekly rhyme: giovedì gnocchi, venerdì pesce, sabato trippa. Thursday brings potato gnocchi, often with tomato sauce and pecorino. Friday is fish because Catholic families traditionally avoided meat that day. Saturday is tripe, a dish with humble roots in the city's offal cooking. Regulars still expect the pattern, and a trattoria with no gnocchi on Thursday gets questioned.
Words: gnocchi (potato dumplings), trippa (tripe)

### Ceiling spilling out (art) · Franca Mattei · fact judge: pass
The nave ceiling of Il Gesù, the mother church of the Jesuits, was painted by Giovanni Battista Gaulli, called Baciccio, in the late 1670s. It shows the Triumph of the Name of Jesus. Painted figures and stucco angels by Antonio Raggi seem to tumble out over the frame into the church. The effect was made to overwhelm and move worshippers in the Baroque age, when the Church wanted faith to be felt as well as understood. Franca says to stand still and look up for a full minute.
Words: affresco (fresco), soffitto (ceiling)

### Romanisti versus laziali (rivalry with other towns or neighbourhoods (campanilismo)) · Sandro Pieri · fact judge: pass
Rome has two big football clubs, and every regular at the bar belongs to one camp. Lazio, founded in 1900, plays in sky blue and white, colours taken from the Greek flag. Roma was founded in 1927 by merging three older clubs, and its yellow and red are the colours of the city. The two share the Stadio Olimpico and meet in the Derby della Capitale. Families split over it, and on the Monday after a derby the counter talk can get loud.
Words: romanista (Roma supporter), laziale (Lazio supporter)

### Noantri festival (folklore and traditions) · Mirella · fact judge: pass
Every July Trastevere holds the Festa de' Noantri, a festival of the Madonna del Carmine. A statue of the Madonna is carried in procession through the narrow streets, with music, stalls and tables outside. Noantri is Roman for noi altri, we others, and it shows how Trasteverini see themselves: a quarter apart from the rest of Rome. The festival goes back centuries, and older families like Mirella's treat it as their own.
Words: noantri (we others (Roman dialect)), processione (procession)
