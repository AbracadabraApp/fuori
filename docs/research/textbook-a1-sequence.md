# How an A1 Textbook Sequences the First Lessons

2026-10-19 · Josh · From the publisher's free specimen of *Nuovissimo Progetto italiano 1* (Edilingua, A1–A2): introductory unit "Benvenuti!" and Unit 1 "Un nuovo inizio" (https://flip.edilingua.it/NVSSU01/). Pages 14–16, 18, 20–21 couldn't be read (rate-limited), so the end of the introductory unit and the opening of Unit 1 come from the unit goal lists. See also `teaching-on-screen.md`.

## The order, every time

1. Warm-up: a photo or a guess.
2. Listen, with a light task (match to a photo, put in order).
3. Listen again: fill gaps, check.
4. Notice: underline the expressions, find the forms in blue.
5. Generalise: finish a part-blank table or rule.
6. Controlled practice from a model in blue.
7. Guided speaking: A/B roles with prompts and a phrase table.
8. Freer task.

A phrase is always heard in a dialogue before it's seen in a table, and never produced before both.

## Meaning without English

No English on any student page. Meaning comes from photos (nearly every section), cognates chosen on purpose (*espresso, opera, cappuccino*), a pictured situation, and the learner's own life (own name, a classmate). Marks: **blue** for examples and target forms, function-labelled phrase boxes ("Chiedere informazioni / Dare informazioni"), register tags (informale / formale), tables with blanks.

## Sequence

- **Benvenuti!** Cognates and photos ("Cosa è l'Italia per voi?"); alphabet and spelling your own name; sounds through food and place words; noun gender; "Ciao, io sono Alice" mini-dialogues; *essere* and nationalities; articles; numbers 1–10.
- **Un nuovo inizio.** Opinions on photos ("Secondo me…"); a first-meeting dialogue; "Di dove sei?" and A/B exchanges; greetings by register; *tu* vs *Lei* side by side; describing people ("Com'è?"); Italian regions; self-assessment; a video episode recycling the unit.

## Core phrases (introductory unit and Unit 1)

- Name: Mi chiamo…, Io sono…, Questa è…, Piacere, E tu? / E Lei?
- Origin: Di dove sei? / Di dov'è?, Sono di…, Sono italiano/a, Sei straniero, vero?
- Here and now: Sono qui da due giorni, Da quanto tempo sei qui?, Abiti qui vicino?, Sei qui in vacanza?
- Attention, directions: Scusa / Scusi, per andare in centro?, È questo l'autobus per il centro?
- Greetings: Ciao, Salve, Buongiorno, Buonasera, Buonanotte, Ci vediamo (domani), Arrivederci, ArrivederLa
- Describing: Com'è?, È alta / simpatica, Ha i capelli lunghi, Sembra…
- Opinion: Secondo me…, Anche per me…
- Compliment: Parli molto bene l'italiano!

## Speaking

Always on a model just heard: pairs remake the dialogue with their own details; "Sei A / Sei B" cards with prompts (nationality, how long here, where you live); a picture plus "fate dei mini dialoghi". The teacher's note: keep the role-play going and correct afterwards, without singling anyone out.

## Grammar and review

Inductive and after the dialogue: observe, fill a table, complete the rule; paradigms start with io / tu / lui-lei. Review after two units ("Che cosa ricordi…?"), written; a video episode per unit recycles its phrases.

## What it suggests for Fuori

- **Hear it in a scene before you say it.** The character's line is the "dialogue first"; the frame comes back later for the learner to use.
- **Pictures and cognates carry meaning, not English.** The portrait and the place art are the photo; beginner characters lean on cognates and what's visible.
- **Phrases by function, with your own details swapped in.** The A/B cards are exactly "the character asks, the learner answers with their own facts" (name, from where, how long here, where you live).
- **Register from day one.** *Tu* and *Lei* are already per character (`{formality}`).
- **Fluency first, correction after.** Matches recasts in role and corrections in the review, not mid-scene.
- **The Unit 1 phrase list is a ready check for the Beginner chips** ("Meeting people" especially).
