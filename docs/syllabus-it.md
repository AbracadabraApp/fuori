**How it was made:** every Italian course book was read in full, one reader per book (notes on Josh's laptop in `books-private/syllabus-working/`); the books were lined up and the units ordered for a newcomer's first weeks at A1 and by the books' progression above; each level was written in full; a second teacher reviewed every Italian line and its English and fixed what was wrong.

**Size:** A1 15 units, A2 14 units, B1 13 units, B2 13 units, C1 14 units; 611 key phrases; 3906 words in the word list; grammar inventory of 304 points.

**Each unit has:** what you can do after it, situations, key phrases (each to *say* or only to *understand*), 2–3 model exchanges, the grammar the phrases need with the trap for English speakers, vocabulary across parts of speech (with flashcard image kind), pronunciation, common mistakes, one culture point, what it recycles, the evidence that you have it, and when to review it. ## A1: the first weeks

### a1-01 · Greet and take leave

**You can:** Greet people and say goodbye at any time of day, with tu or Lei as the moment calls for.  
**Situations:** entering and leaving a shop or bar, meeting a neighbour on the stairs, greeting an older person or an official, stopping a stranger in the street  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Buongiorno!* | Good morning! | say |
| *Buonasera, signora.* | Good evening, madam. | say |
| *Salve!* | Hello! | say |
| *Ciao! Come stai?* | Hi! How are you? | say |
| *Buongiorno, come sta?* | Good morning, how are you? | say |
| *Bene, grazie. E Lei? / E tu?* | Fine, thanks. And you? | say |
| *Senta, scusi...* | Excuse me... | say |
| *Arrivederci, buona giornata!* | Goodbye, have a good day! | say |
| *Grazie, anche a Lei! / Altrettanto!* | Thanks, you too! / Same to you! | say |
| *A domani! / Ci vediamo!* | See you tomorrow! / See you! | say |

**Grammar:** tu and Lei as two sets of chunks; stare for how you are; Greeting by time of day  
**Words:** 20 (buongiorno, buonasera, buonanotte, salve, ciao, arrivederci, stare, bene, male, grazie, …)  
**Culture:** Italians greet when they walk into a shop, a bar, a lift or a waiting room, and say goodbye when they leave; walking in or out in silence comes across as rude. Buonasera starts surprisingly early, around 3 or 4 p.m.  
**Evidence:** Without being prompted, the learner greets on arrival with the right word for the time of day, uses Lei with an older or unfamiliar character and tu with a friend, answers 'come sta?' with 'bene, grazie, e Lei?', and leaves with a goodbye in the matching register.

### a1-02 · When you don't understand

**You can:** Say you didn't understand, ask someone to repeat or slow down, and ask what a word means or how to say something.  
**Situations:** any conversation that goes too fast, a counter where you missed the price, meeting a word you don't know, a language class  
**After:** a1-01  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Scusi, non ho capito.* | Sorry, I didn't understand. | say |
| *Può ripetere, per favore?* | Could you repeat that, please? | say |
| *Più piano, per favore.* | More slowly, please. | say |
| *Cosa vuol dire “scontrino”?* | What does “scontrino” mean? | say |
| *Come si dice “receipt” in italiano?* | How do you say “receipt” in Italian? | say |
| *Come si scrive?* | How do you spell it? | say |
| *Può dirlo in inglese?* | Could you say it in English? | say |
| *Parlo solo un po' d'italiano.* | I only speak a little Italian. | say |
| *Ah, ho capito, grazie!* | Ah, got it, thanks! | say |
| *Ha capito?* | Did you understand? | understand |

**Grammar:** non goes right before the verb; può + infinitive as a polite request; ho capito / non ho capito as fixed forms; come si dice? / come si scrive? as fixed chunks  
**Words:** 19 (capire, ripetere, parlare, dire, voler dire, scrivere, parola, correzione, lingua, italiano, …)  
**Culture:** To say 'Sorry? / Pardon?' Italians say 'Come?' or 'Scusi?'; a bare 'Cosa?' can sound blunt. Most people are pleased you're trying, but many speed up or switch to English, so a friendly 'più piano, per favore' is normal and expected.  
**Evidence:** When a character goes too fast or uses an unknown word, the learner repairs in Italian (non ho capito, può ripetere?, più piano, cosa vuol dire...?, come si dice...?) instead of going silent or switching to English, then confirms with 'ho capito'.

### a1-03 · Introduce yourself as a newcomer

**You can:** Say who you are, where you're from, where you live, why you're here and for how long, and ask the same back.  
**Situations:** meeting a new neighbour, first chat with someone who asks where you're from, the first day of a language course  
**After:** a1-01  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Mi chiamo … E Lei, come si chiama?* | My name's … And what's your name? | say |
| *Piacere!* | Nice to meet you! | say |
| *Sono americano, di …* | I'm American, from … | say |
| *Di dove sei? / Di dov'è?* | Where are you from? (tu / Lei) | say |
| *Abito qui, in via …* | I live here, in … Street. | say |
| *È qui in vacanza? — No, abito qui!* | Are you here on holiday? — No, I live here! | say |
| *Sono in Italia da tre settimane.* | I've been in Italy for three weeks. | say |
| *Sono qui per imparare l'italiano.* | I'm here to learn Italian. | say |
| *Sono in pensione. / Lavoro come …* | I'm retired. / I work as a … | say |
| *Ben arrivato! / Ben arrivata!* | Welcome! | understand |

**Grammar:** essere in the present: sono, sei, è; chiamarsi as a chunk: mi chiamo, ti chiami, si chiama; -are verbs for io, tu and Lei; Nationality adjectives agree; di, a and in with places; da + present for 'have been ... for'  
**Words:** 21 (chiamarsi, essere, abitare, lavorare, imparare, piacere, americano, inglese, italiano, città, …)  
**Culture:** 'Di dove sei?' is one of the first things Italians ask, because where you come from matters a lot here. Saying you live here, not that you're on holiday, changes the conversation: people start treating you as a neighbour, and they'll often compliment your Italian (just say 'Grazie, ma sto imparando!').  
**Evidence:** Asked where they're from, the learner answers with di + city, says they live here (abito a/in ...) and how long they've been here with da + present, asks the same back in the right register, and the adjectives agree with their own gender.

### a1-04 · Order at the bar

**You can:** Order a coffee and something to eat for yourself or a friend, at the counter or at a table.  
**Situations:** breakfast at a bar, a coffee standing at the counter, an aperitivo with a friend, ordering for two  
**After:** a1-01  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Un caffè, per favore.* | An espresso, please. | say |
| *Per me un cappuccino e un cornetto.* | A cappuccino and a croissant for me. | say |
| *Vorrei una spremuta d'arancia.* | I'd like a fresh orange juice. | say |
| *Cosa prendi? / Cosa prende?* | What are you having? (tu / Lei) | say |
| *Prendo anch'io un caffè.* | I'll have a coffee too. | say |
| *Prego! Cosa Le do?* | Yes? What can I get you? | understand |
| *Al banco o al tavolo?* | At the counter or at a table? | understand |
| *Offro io!* | It's on me! | say |
| *Un bicchiere d'acqua, per favore.* | A glass of water, please. | say |
| *Naturale o frizzante?* | Still or sparkling? | understand |

**Grammar:** Noun gender and the indefinite article; Plurals; vorrei as a fixed polite phrase; prendere for food and drink  
**Words:** 22 (caffè, cappuccino, macchiato, cornetto, spremuta, acqua, frizzante, bicchiere, tramezzino, panino, …)  
**Culture:** In many bars you pay first at the cassa, then hand the scontrino to the barista and order at the counter. Standing at the banco is cheaper than sitting at a table, where there may be table service at a higher price. Cappuccino is a morning drink; after lunch Italians order an espresso.  
**Evidence:** The learner orders at least two items with the right articles (un/uno/una/un'), uses vorrei or per me rather than voglio, asks a companion 'cosa prendi?', answers 'al banco' or 'al tavolo', and pluralises correctly when ordering for two.

### a1-05 · Prices, numbers and paying

**You can:** Ask and understand prices, count well past a hundred, and pay by card or cash.  
**Situations:** paying at a till, a market stall, a bakery, a newsstand  
**After:** a1-04  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Quanto costa? / Quanto costano?* | How much is it? / How much are they? | say |
| *Quant'è?* | How much is that (altogether)? | say |
| *Sono quattro euro e cinquanta.* | That's four euros fifty. | understand |
| *Posso pagare con la carta?* | Can I pay by card? | say |
| *Pago in contanti.* | I'll pay cash. | say |
| *Ecco a Lei.* | Here you are. | say |
| *Ha moneta?* | Have you got any change (coins)? | understand |
| *Mi dispiace, non ho spiccioli.* | Sorry, I haven't got any change. | say |

**Grammar:** Numbers 0–100, then cento, mille, duemila; euro never changes; costa with one thing, costano with several; posso + infinitive  
**Words:** 20 (costare, pagare, potere, prezzo, euro, centesimo, contanti, carta, bancomat, resto, …)  
**Culture:** Shops are required to accept cards, but small shops and market stalls still prefer cash for small amounts, so keep some coins: cashiers often ask 'Ha moneta?' to avoid breaking a big note. Take the scontrino when it's handed to you.  
**Evidence:** The learner asks 'quanto costa / quanto costano' with the right number, understands a spoken price well enough to pay or check it back, asks to pay by card, and handles 'Ha moneta?' without switching to English.

### a1-06 · Ask for things and whether they have them

**You can:** Ask whether something is available or nearby, point to what you want, and ask for it politely.  
**Situations:** a bakery, a small shop, asking a passer-by what's nearby, a bar when you need the toilet  
**After:** a1-04, a1-05  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Avete il pane integrale?* | Do you have wholemeal bread? | say |
| *C'è una farmacia qui vicino?* | Is there a pharmacy near here? | say |
| *Scusi, c'è un bagno?* | Excuse me, is there a toilet? | say |
| *Cos'è questo?* | What's this? | say |
| *Mi dà due di questi, per favore?* | Could you give me two of these, please? | say |
| *Quello lì, grazie.* | That one there, thanks. | say |
| *Un po' di focaccia, per favore.* | A bit of focaccia, please. | say |
| *Mi dispiace, è finito.* | Sorry, it's sold out. | understand |
| *Sì, ce n'è una in piazza.* | Yes, there's one in the square. | understand |

**Grammar:** c'è / ci sono; avete...?; The definite article: il, lo, l', la, i, gli, le; questo and quello as pronouns; un po' di and del / della for 'some'; mi dà...? as a fixed polite request  
**Words:** 20 (pane, integrale, focaccia, pizza al taglio, grissini, biscotto, pasta, farmacia, bagno, piazza, …)  
**Culture:** In bakeries and pastry shops you don't serve yourself: you ask and point, and the assistant picks. Where there's a ticket dispenser, take a number and wait to be called. Pizza al taglio and bread are often sold by weight, so the assistant cuts a piece and asks 'Così va bene?'.  
**Evidence:** The learner asks whether a shop has something (avete...?) or whether there is something nearby (c'è...?), uses ci sono for plurals, points with questo / quello lì when the word is missing, and asks with mi dà...? or un po' di....

### a1-07 · Where is it? Asking the way

**You can:** Ask where a place is and follow short directions, helped by gestures and landmarks.  
**Situations:** lost in a new neighbourhood, looking for the station, a church or a museum, a passer-by who points  
**After:** a1-02, a1-06  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Scusi, dov'è la stazione?* | Excuse me, where's the station? | say |
| *Scusi, per andare al Duomo?* | Excuse me, how do I get to the cathedral? | say |
| *È lontano?* | Is it far? | say |
| *È qui vicino, a due passi.* | It's close by, just round the corner. | understand |
| *Sempre dritto, poi a destra.* | Straight on, then right. | understand |
| *Giri a sinistra al semaforo.* | Turn left at the traffic lights. | understand |
| *È in fondo alla strada, accanto alla chiesa.* | It's at the end of the street, next to the church. | understand |
| *Dritto e poi a sinistra?* | Straight on and then left? | say |
| *Grazie mille, molto gentile!* | Thanks a lot, very kind! | say |

**Grammar:** dov'è / dove sono; Places with a, in and da as chunks; a + the article: al, alla, all'; Place words with a: vicino a, accanto a, davanti a, di fronte a, in fondo a; Lei commands: giri, vada, prenda (understand)  
**Words:** 23 (strada, piazza, angolo, semaforo, incrocio, chiesa, duomo, stazione, museo, dritto, …)  
**Culture:** Italians give directions with landmarks (the church, the tobacconist, the traffic lights) and plenty of gestures, so watch the hands as well as the words. 'A due passi' can mean anything from one minute to fifteen, and in old town centres street names change every few blocks.  
**Evidence:** The learner asks for a place with dov'è or per andare a...?, checks the directions back with a short summary (dritto e poi a destra?), asks if it's far, and shows they followed the key words (destra, sinistra, dritto, semaforo, in fondo).

### a1-08 · Times, days and opening hours

**You can:** Ask and tell the time, ask when somewhere opens and closes, and fix a time.  
**Situations:** finding a shop closed at lunchtime, a pharmacy's hours and the night rota, arranging to meet someone  
**After:** a1-05  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Scusi, che ore sono?* | Excuse me, what time is it? | say |
| *Sono le tre e mezza. / È l'una.* | It's half past three. / It's one o'clock. | say |
| *A che ora apre la farmacia?* | What time does the pharmacy open? | say |
| *A che ora chiude?* | What time does it close? | say |
| *Siete aperti la domenica?* | Are you open on Sundays? | say |
| *Dalle nove all'una e dalle quattro alle otto.* | From nine to one and from four to eight. | understand |
| *Il lunedì mattina siamo chiusi.* | We're closed on Monday mornings. | understand |
| *Torno tra dieci minuti.* | I'll be back in ten minutes. | say |
| *Ci vediamo alle sei?* | Shall we meet at six? | say |

**Grammar:** sono le + hour, but è l'una, è mezzogiorno, è mezzanotte; alle + hour; dalle ... alle ...; tra + time for 'in ten minutes'; Days without a preposition; The present for near plans  
**Words:** 22 (ora, minuto, mezzo, quarto, mezzogiorno, mezzanotte, aprire, chiudere, tornare, aperto, …)  
**Culture:** Many shops, especially in small towns and the south, close for lunch (roughly 1 to 4 p.m.) and have one closing half-day a week, often Monday morning. Pharmacies take turns staying open at night and on holidays: the 'farmacia di turno' is posted on every pharmacy's door. Signs and timetables use the 24-hour clock, but people usually say 'alle quattro' rather than 'alle sedici'.  
**Evidence:** The learner asks and gives the time with sono le / è l'una, asks when a place opens or closes, understands dalle ... alle ..., and fixes a meeting time with alle ... or tra ... minuti.

### a1-09 · At the market

**You can:** Buy fruit, vegetables and cheese by weight, say how much you want, and finish the purchase.  
**Situations:** an outdoor market stall, a cheese counter, buying by weight  
**After:** a1-05, a1-06  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Buongiorno, chi è l'ultimo?* | Good morning, who's last in the queue? | say |
| *Tocca a Lei.* | It's your turn. | understand |
| *Vorrei mezzo chilo di pomodori.* | I'd like half a kilo of tomatoes. | say |
| *Due etti di pecorino, per favore.* | Two hundred grams of pecorino, please. | say |
| *Quanto vengono le pesche?* | How much are the peaches? | say |
| *Sono buone le albicocche oggi?* | Are the apricots good today? | say |
| *Quanto ne vuole?* | How much would you like? | understand |
| *Altro? — Basta così, grazie.* | Anything else? — That's all, thanks. | say |
| *Sono due etti e mezzo, va bene lo stesso?* | It's 250 grams, is that OK? | understand |

**Grammar:** Quantity + di; Plurals of produce; questo / quello again: quelle lì; ne heard as a chunk; quanto vengono? for prices by weight  
**Words:** 22 (mercato, pomodoro, zucchina, pesca, albicocca, mela, pecorino, cacio, prosciutto, chilo, …)  
**Culture:** At a market stall don't touch the produce: the stallholder picks it for you, or tell them what it's for (per oggi, per il sugo) and they'll choose. Where there's no visible queue, ask 'Chi è l'ultimo?' and remember who goes before you. In supermarkets you must wear the plastic gloves to pick fruit and weigh it yourself.  
**Evidence:** The learner finds their turn with chi è l'ultimo?, asks for amounts with chilo / etto + di, asks a price with quanto costano or quanto vengono, accepts or refuses a slightly different weight, and ends with basta così.

### a1-10 · Your new home and the landlady

**You can:** Talk with your landlady or host about the flat: where things are, how things work, and what isn't working.  
**Situations:** moving into a rented flat, something breaks in the first week, learning the rubbish calendar  
**After:** a1-03, a1-06  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Abito al terzo piano.* | I live on the third floor. | say |
| *Qual è la password del wifi?* | What's the wifi password? | say |
| *Come funziona la lavatrice?* | How does the washing machine work? | say |
| *Dov'è la chiave del portone?* | Where's the key to the main door? | say |
| *Scusi, la doccia non funziona.* | Excuse me, the shower isn't working. | say |
| *Non c'è acqua calda.* | There's no hot water. | say |
| *Che giorno si butta la plastica?* | Which day does the plastic go out? | say |
| *Mando il tecnico domani.* | I'll send the repairman tomorrow. | understand |
| *Grazie, è molto gentile.* | Thank you, that's very kind of you. | say |

**Grammar:** c'è / non c'è, recycled; funziona / non funziona; qual è for 'what is' + a piece of information; Ordinal numbers for floors; Rooms take in  
**Words:** 23 (appartamento, camera da letto, cucina, bagno, soggiorno, balcone, piano, ascensore, portone, chiave, …)  
**Culture:** Each comune sorts rubbish differently, with its own calendar, colours and bins (plastica, carta, vetro, umido, indifferenziata). Putting the wrong bag out on the wrong day earns a sticker on the bag or a word from the neighbours, so ask early. Floors start from the piano terra (ground floor).  
**Evidence:** The learner says which floor they live on, asks how something works or where it is, reports a fault with non funziona / non c'è, and asks about the rubbish days, staying in Lei with the landlady.

### a1-11 · Small talk: likes, free time and the weather

**You can:** Chat about what you like, what you do in your free time, and the weather, and ask the other person.  
**Situations:** chatting with a regular at the counter, a neighbour on a bench, waiting in a queue  
**After:** a1-03  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Ti piace la città? / Le piace la città?* | Do you like the town? (tu / Lei) | say |
| *Sì, mi piace molto!* | Yes, I like it a lot! | say |
| *Mi piacciono i carciofi.* | I like artichokes. | say |
| *Anche a me! / A me no.* | Me too! / Not me. | say |
| *Cosa fai nel tempo libero?* | What do you do in your free time? | say |
| *Leggo, cammino e cucino.* | I read, walk and cook. | say |
| *Preferisco il mare.* | I prefer the sea. | say |
| *Che caldo oggi!* | It's so hot today! | say |
| *Fa freddo, eh?* | Cold, isn't it? | say |
| *Che bella giornata!* | What a lovely day! | say |

**Grammar:** mi piace + one thing or a verb; mi piacciono + several things; ti piace / Le piace; anche a me, neanche a me, a me no; The present of regular verbs, all persons; Weather with fare  
**Words:** 21 (piacere, preferire, leggere, camminare, cucinare, tempo libero, passeggiata, cinema, calcio, musica, …)  
**Culture:** Food and the weather are the safest topics with anyone in Italy, and people will happily talk about both for a long time. 'Le piace qui?' is a chance to compliment the town; Italians are proud of where they live but often complain about it themselves.  
**Evidence:** The learner says what they like with mi piace / mi piacciono matched to the noun, asks back with ti piace or Le piace, answers with anche a me / a me no, names two or three free-time activities in the present, and makes a weather remark with fa caldo / fa freddo.

### a1-12 · Make a plan: invite, accept, decline

**You can:** Invite someone, accept or turn them down politely with a reason, and fix a time and place to meet.  
**Situations:** being invited for an aperitivo, arranging a day out, turning down an invitation without offending  
**After:** a1-08, a1-11  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Ti va un caffè?* | Fancy a coffee? | say |
| *Vieni con noi sabato sera?* | Are you coming with us on Saturday evening? | say |
| *Sei libero domani? / Sei libera domani?* | Are you free tomorrow? (to a man / a woman) | say |
| *Volentieri!* | I'd love to! | say |
| *Mi dispiace, non posso: devo lavorare.* | Sorry, I can't: I have to work. | say |
| *Ho già un impegno.* | I've already got plans. | say |
| *Magari un'altra volta.* | Maybe another time. | say |
| *Ci vediamo davanti alla chiesa alle sette.* | Let's meet in front of the church at seven. | say |
| *Domani vado al mare.* | Tomorrow I'm going to the seaside. | say |

**Grammar:** Irregular present: andare, venire, fare, uscire; potere, volere, dovere + infinitive; The present for plans; ti va? as a fixed invitation  
**Words:** 21 (andare, venire, uscire, fare, potere, volere, dovere, invito, cena, aperitivo, …)  
**Culture:** An invitation for 'un caffè' means five minutes standing at a bar, and the person who invites usually pays. Italians often decline gently ('magari un'altra volta') rather than say a flat no. Arriving ten or fifteen minutes late to a social plan is normal in much of Italy, though not for appointments or trains.  
**Evidence:** The learner invites with ti va...? or vieni...?, accepts with volentieri or declines with mi dispiace, non posso + a reason using devo + infinitive, offers an alternative, and fixes a place and time.

### a1-13 · Getting around: buses and trains

**You can:** Buy a bus or train ticket, check the departure and platform, and ask where to get off.  
**Situations:** a ticket office or machine, a bus stop, a strike or a delay  
**After:** a1-05, a1-07, a1-08  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Un biglietto per Firenze, solo andata.* | A ticket to Florence, one way. | say |
| *Andata e ritorno?* | Return? | understand |
| *A che ora parte il prossimo treno?* | What time does the next train leave? | say |
| *Da che binario parte?* | Which platform does it leave from? | say |
| *Questo autobus va in centro?* | Does this bus go to the centre? | say |
| *Mi può dire dove devo scendere?* | Can you tell me where to get off? | say |
| *Dove si comprano i biglietti?* | Where do you buy tickets? | say |
| *Il treno è in ritardo di venti minuti.* | The train is twenty minutes late. | understand |
| *Bisogna convalidare il biglietto.* | You have to validate the ticket. | understand |
| *Domani c'è sciopero.* | There's a strike tomorrow. | understand |

**Grammar:** in + transport, but a piedi; per + destination; devo + infinitive; si + verb as a chunk: dove si comprano?; bisogna + infinitive (understand)  
**Words:** 22 (stazione, biglietteria, binario, treno, regionale, cambio, ritardo, autobus, fermata, autista, …)  
**Culture:** Paper tickets must be validated before you travel: on buses in the machine on board, for regional trains in the machines at the station. A regional ticket bought in the Trenitalia app or online is checked in automatically, so it needs no stamping. Inspectors fine on the spot, foreigners included. City bus tickets are sold at the tabaccheria, the newsstand or in an app, and many cities now take contactless cards at the gates. Transport strikes are announced in advance and usually leave guaranteed services at rush hour.  
**Evidence:** The learner buys a ticket with per + destination, answers andata e ritorno?, asks the time and platform, checks whether a bus goes where they want, and asks where to get off; they understand 'in ritardo' and 'sciopero' when they hear them.

### a1-14 · Health basics and emergencies

**You can:** Explain a simple health problem at the pharmacy, ask for something for it, and call for help in an emergency.  
**Situations:** a pharmacy counter, feeling ill in the first weeks, an accident in the street, a lost wallet  
**After:** a1-02, a1-06  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Ho mal di testa.* | I've got a headache. | say |

**Culture:** Italian history is still argued over at the table: the Risorgimento, the Southern Question, Fascism and the Resistance mean different things in different families and regions. ### c1-14 · Society in debate: legality, migration, belief

**You can:** Debate social questions at length and with precision: build an argument, generalise and qualify, and use every kind of subordinate clause.  
**Situations:** a serious discussion with friends, a public talk or debate, a radio interview  
**After:** c1-10, c1-13  

| Italian | English | |
|---|---|---|
| *Chiunque abbia vissuto qui sa che la legalità si costruisce dal basso.* | Anyone who has lived here knows that legality is built from the ground up. | say |

**Culture:** Italians discuss the mafia, migration and the Church openly and with strong views, and many have personal stakes in them.
