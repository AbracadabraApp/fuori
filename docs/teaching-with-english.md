# Teaching with English: patterns from the syllabus

2026-10-27 · Josh asked to review the syllabus and pull out the best examples, generalised, for how characters use English to teach (Beginner level dialogue, [beginner-scaffolding.md](beginner-scaffolding.md) → goal 5b: English used to teach, not instead of teaching). Source: the `culture`, `grammar` (explain, trap) and `common_errors` fields of the A1 units in `content/it/syllabus.json` and `content/es-mx/syllabus.json`. Ideas for the prompt and the measure (12c); nothing built from this yet.

## The shape

Every good example has the same shape: **one sentence of English that gives the why, the when or the trap → the phrase in the language → (sometimes) the learner gets to use it right away.** The English never says what the phrase itself says; it says what the learner couldn't get from the phrase alone.

What it isn't: English that does the language's job ("Here's your sparkling water, and the pizza is coming soon") or explains how people talk without giving the phrase ("Still or sparkling is how we ask it here").

## Seven patterns, with the best examples

**1. The custom, then its phrase.** A local habit a newcomer wouldn't know, which comes with words of its own.
- Market, Italy: "Where there's no visible queue, people ask who's last, and remember who goes before them." → *Chi è l'ultimo?*
- Market, Mexico: "Buy from the same stalls and you become a regular; vendors let you taste." → *¿Me da a probar?*
- Taquería, Mexico: "You pay at the end and tell the taquero how many you had." → *Fueron tres, con todo.*

**2. What you'll hear here.** A phrase said *to* the learner, so they recognise it next time.
- Pizza by weight: "They cut a piece and ask if that size is right." → *Così va bene?*
- Paying with a big note: "Cashiers often ask for coins to avoid breaking it." → *Ha moneta?*
- Mexican market: stallholders call out *¡Pásele!*, and ask whether the avocados are *para hoy o para mañana?*

**3. The one trap, with the right phrase.** The mistake an English speaker makes here, said once, then the phrase said right.
- "To say you're cold, Italians say they *have* cold." → *Ho freddo.* (not *sono freddo*)
- "You didn't understand: that's *ho capito* with *non* in front." → *Non ho capito.*
- "English 'excuse me' is three phrases here: to get past, to get attention, after a bump." → *Con permiso. / Disculpe. / Perdón.*

**4. Rebuild the English.** A different way of thinking about the sentence that makes the phrase click.
- "In Italian the thing you like does the pleasing: *to me is pleasing the pizza*." → *Mi piace la pizza.*
- "There's no 'do' or 'did' in Italian questions." → *Cosa hai fatto?*
- "Half a kilo has no 'a' in front." → *Mezzo chilo / medio kilo.*

**5. How it sounds to them (register).** Politeness and tone, which a learner can't hear yet.
- "A bare *Cosa?* can sound blunt; people say *Come?* or *Scusi?*"
- "With a shopkeeper or an older stranger, use the polite form until they offer the familiar one." → *Lei / usted.*
- "*Quiero* on its own can sound blunt; the safe way is *me da…, por favor*."

**6. When and where it's said.** Time and place customs that come with a phrase.
- "*Buonasera* starts surprisingly early, around three or four in the afternoon."
- "Cappuccino is a morning drink; after lunch Italians order an espresso." → *Un caffè, per favore.*
- "You say *provecho* to anyone eating as you walk by, even strangers."

**7. What it really means.** Words whose meaning isn't in the dictionary.
- "*A due passi* can mean anything from one minute to fifteen."
- "*Magari un'altra volta* is a gentle no." 
- "An invitation for *un caffè* means five minutes standing at a bar, and the person who invites pays."

## How this could be used

- **In the Beginner prompt:** the shape above as the rule for English ("one sentence that gives the why, the when or the trap, then the phrase"), with the seven patterns as kinds of things worth saying in English. Measured against goal 12c (each English stretch teaching or not).
- **In each place's `here` line or a premade place note** (roadmap → Beginner level dialogue → premade place notes): the syllabus's `culture` field already holds checked customs for the bar, the market, the pharmacy, the station, the taquería. A place can carry one or two of them, so the character's English is true and leads into a phrase.
- **In Learn lessons** ([teach-plan.md](teach-plan.md) → Lessons and missions): the same patterns are the "notice" step; patterns 3 and 4 are what a lesson's notice line is.
- **For the judge (12c):** an English stretch that fits one of the seven patterns and is followed by the phrase is teaching; one that doesn't lead to any of the language isn't.
