# Rome redo: today's Rome, not a tourist trip

2026-10-22 · Josh · Decided in conversation (cloud session). **Built as a second city, "Roma nuova"** (`content/it/cities/roma-nuova.ts`), beside the original Roma, which is untouched, so both can be played and compared (Josh). Rome first; the other cities follow once it plays well.

## The problem

Italy looks like a stereotype of tourist Rome (Josh). The tests showed where it comes from:
- **Not the art style.** Rome's existing scenes redrawn in fine-line pen, through Mexico's prompt builder, still came out as ivy, old men in caps at a zinc counter, striped awnings and a busker at dusk. A new style on the same scenes gives the same stereotype.
- **The scenes and the cast.** Each place's `imagePrompt` was written once by the city generator (`scripts/generate-city-content.mjs`) from the "City Pantry Formula" and the pastel template in `content/it/generation.json`, with style and scene mixed in one sentence. The people at Rome's shown places are mostly older Romans in aprons (youngest 34).
- **The place list.** Most shown places are food counters (bar, bakery, market, enoteca, trattoria, gelateria): they look alike and the conversation is the same short transaction.

What worked: the same places with **scenes generated again** from one generic instruction (an ordinary weekday today, as someone who lives in the neighbourhood sees it), drawn with Mexico's builder. Test images on the laptop in `.flashcards-tmp/rome-scene/`, `rome-fine/`, `rome-new/`, `rome-media/`.

## Decisions (Josh)

- **Places are for conversation and need a reason to be there.** Bars are fundamental. Places where you eat or drink (pizza al taglio, a trattoria at lunch) have a built-in reason. Open places (piazzas, churches, monuments, parks, a tram stop) are good for open conversation: the person there can be anyone, young, from anywhere, not running the place.
- **Counters move to missions:** the bakery, market, salumeria, cheese shop and enoteca are interesting because ordering is different, but you need a reason to go, and the person must act like a real salesperson (a taste, how many etti, how thin, "Altro?"), not ask who you are. They come back as missions from Learn (`docs/teach-plan.md` → "try it in town").
- **Language schools go away** (Magda moves into Learn, N5). Remove them when Learn ships, so Magda keeps a home until then.
- **Not stereotypes, not quotas:** people don't have to be staff; a mix of ages and of Romans and Romans from elsewhere, mostly Romans. Vespas, pizza, checked tablecloths are not banned: the problem was scenes staged for a visitor, not objects.
- **Neighbourhoods are labels**; they matter little (a phone shop or pizza place doesn't change by neighbourhood).

## What we learned from the pictures (2026-10-22, Josh)

- Shops are seen **from the street**, recognisable by their front (the green cross, the tabaccheria's awning), with the street, the buildings and the sky; an interior or a building on blank paper doesn't read as the place.
- In Rome nothing but the Pantheon stands alone: shops, bars and restaurants are **on the ground floor of a continuous row of apartment buildings** (now in Italy's `placeRules`, and the scene must say it too: a scene that says "a low building" wins over the rule).
- **Close in:** the shopfront and the person fill most of the card, a floor or two above. The farmacia, tabaccheria and trattoria were cropped to this by hand; next time the scene instruction should ask for it ("close enough that the shopfront and the person fill most of the picture, with the first floor above").
- A portrait with a white shirt and dark apron came out with no colour three times; colourful clothes and the wash named first fixed it.

## How to build it for every city (proposed)

1. **Scenes separate from style, as Mexico does** (`docs/mexico-place-images-plan.md`): `placeStyles` for Rome in `content/it/generation.json`, scene-only `imagePrompt`s, `scripts/lib/place-prompt.mjs` assembles style + scene + sign + rules.
2. **Generate the scenes and portrait prompts** for the 10 from the place data with one generic instruction (no hand-written scenes), **dated from the clock** ("Rome in October 2026, an ordinary weekday"): no prompt today says what year it is, so "today" is the model's guess.
3. **Style:** the existing pastel, or fine-line pen (tested; matches the portraits). Josh to choose.
4. New places and people into `roma.ts`, showcase updated, images generated and uploaded (`npm run images:upload`), `test:pack` passes; play it.
