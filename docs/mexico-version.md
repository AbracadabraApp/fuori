# Mexico version

**Spec and plan (2026-10-12):** [Fuori México: specification and project plan](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/2f9709ff-39b3-4935-bcc5-616681f703d0): 5 cities × 10 places × 10 personas, one codebase, about 6 days in four phases: (1) Italian out of the code, (2) content and visuals, (3) voices and deployment, (4) language quality through the test loop. No human review steps (Josh). Name: *Afuera* is the lead candidate.

**Phase 1 done (2026-10-12):**
- `content/index.ts` exports the active country pack by `COUNTRY` (Railway variable; `next.config.ts` passes it to the browser). Code imports `@/content`, never `content/<country>/`; `npm run test:language` fails on a direct import, on language or country names (Italian or Spanish) and on locale codes.
- Each pack (`content/<country>/pack.ts`): id, `language` (code, name, speechLang, `appName`, formality words), cities (with `region`), anchors, voices.
- Formality is `formal` / `informal`; the pack supplies the words (Lei / tu). Reply field `{ line, en }` (was `it`); review items use `phrase`. Character prompt 2026-10-12d, memory prompt 2026-10-12b.
- `npm run test:pack` checks every pack is complete and ids are unique across packs. Generation scripts write to `content/$COUNTRY/`.
- Italy runs unchanged with the default `COUNTRY=it`.

**Next (phase 2):** `content/es-mx/` with `language.ts` and `pack.ts`, the 10 place kinds per city in the generation script, then 5 cities, 50 places, 50 personas and 105 images.

## Visual style (worked out 2026-10-12, Josh with Claude, testing prompts by hand in Grok)

**Cities:** Ciudad de México, Oaxaca, Puebla, Guadalajara, San Miguel de Allende.

**The system**
- **City cards:** each city has its own distinct style, rooted in something local (Josh: "Oaxaca and Mexico City evoke different ideas"). What ties the deck together is the card format, border and caption, not one technique.
- **Places:** one style across Mexico. Linocut is the candidate (Josh: "might work for a place, not great for a city").
- **People:** one style across Mexico, **not decided**: saturated gouache (warm, matches the Mexico City card, busier under subtitles) or linocut (graphic, matches the places, cleaner under subtitles). The test that decides: whose face would you want to talk to every morning.

**Styles so far**
- **Mexico City card, approved (V2):** flat matte gouache, jacarandas in bloom over a Roma Norte avenue, 1920s Art Deco buildings, a tamale seller on a bicycle, a schoolgirl and her grandmother, a man in a guayabera on a bench, a dog asleep. Prompt below.
- **Oaxaca card, approved (first try):** saturated folk painting, green stone church, mountains, marigolds, papel picado, a tlayuda grill, a young man on a modern scooter.
- **Puebla card, approved (first try, Josh):** hand-painted Talavera tile panel with a tile border; tiled façades, the cathedral's twin towers, a camote cart, students with backpacks.
- **Guadalajara card, approved (V2):** mid-century modern travel poster; the cathedral's yellow spires, a modern bus and cars, agave in the planters, a woman drinking tejuino, Tlaquepaque pottery. V1 drifted to vintage cars and repeated Mexico City's jacaranda.
- **San Miguel card, approved (Josh: "keeper"):** the view from El Mirador over the town, Mexican mural / fresco style in earthy colours: the Parroquia in the centre, the Las Monjas dome, hills and the reservoir, a family, a couple taking a photo, a corn seller at the stone wall. Rejected on the way: thick impasto sunset and oil pastel (both "cotton candy"), the Jardín with mojigangas (the generator drew skeletons or giant real people; mojigangas could be a place image later), and painted tin (charming, but the square came out empty).
- **All five city cards approved (2026-10-12)** and saved in `public/images/es-mx/cities/` (`ciudad-de-mexico`, `oaxaca`, `puebla`, `guadalajara`, `san-miguel-de-allende`.jpg). The app doesn't read that folder yet (it loads `/images/cities/<id>.jpg`); phase 2 decides the per-country image path and the final city ids. All five are 1200 × 1200 squares regenerated by Josh from the approved prompts (Oaxaca: Josh picked the version with the marigold tree and the young man on a motorbike).
- **Recurring defaults to avoid:** a vendor in a straw hat (3 of the first 5 cards); sunset glow (turns to "cotton candy"); for festivals, skulls and skeletons. Give each vendor something specific (a cap, an apron, a phone).
- **Places, linocut:** bold carved black lines, wood grain, black plus hot pink and marigold on off-white paper; big simple shapes so it reads at card size.
- **People:** linocut reference is the juice seller (black, pink, marigold; her blender and oranges behind her). Gouache reference is the same woman against a rosa mexicano wall with Talavera, aguas frescas and fruit.
- **Rejected:** the storybook "folk painting" as a general style (came out as generic nostalgic painting); thick impasto oil (a common AI look). Its warmth goes into Oaxaca's card instead.

**Rules every prompt follows** (each learned from a test image or from Italy)
- **Mexico today.** Every first try drifted to the 1940s–50s (rebozos, fedoras, lantern carts). Name modern details: T-shirts, jeans, aprons, plastic stools, motorbikes, school uniforms.
- **Everyday life, not tourist views or clichés:** no sombreros, cacti, skulls. The learner lives there.
- **Mexican identity comes from colour and everyday objects, never costume:** rosa mexicano walls, oilcloth on counters, aguas frescas, Talavera, papel picado. Use one or two that belong to the place, not all of them (a test portrait with all of them read as a checklist).
- **Name the city and neighbourhood** in every place prompt (Italy: about 350 of 594 didn't, and Caffè Toledo came out as Paris).
- **State the person's gender** (Italy: 10 women got men's portraits).
- **Portraits show a glimpse of the person's place behind them**, with background detail above shoulder height and a calm lower half, because subtitles cover the bottom of the screen.
- **Lettering:** none on city cards or portraits. Places may have one exact named sign written in the prompt ("The stall's sign reads 'Jugos Lupita'"; any other lettering is everyday detail, no banners naming the neighbourhood or city). Generators misspell, and this is a language app. Untested: whether Grok spells a named sign correctly.
- **Shape: square (decided 2026-10-12),** like Italy's cards. Josh found how to get high-resolution square images from Grok; at least 1200 × 1200. Prompts keep the line "Keep the main scene in the centre of the picture; nothing important near the top or bottom edges." Whether the xAI API accepts an aspect ratio is still untested (matters for scripted places and portraits).
- **Varied faces:** generators repeat one face (three identical women in the linocut market). Describe each person differently (age, build, hair).

**How place and people prompts get written:** like Italy, Claude writes each one in the content step (`scripts/generate-city-content.mjs`) from that place's own context, as Italy's prompts did (a Naples bar "with blue-tiled walls… and a small shrine to Maradona"). Each prompt = the fixed style line + a scene written for that place + the rules above. City cards stay hand-written, as Italy's did (prescriptive city prompts worked better, 2026-10-10).

**Open before generating**
- People style (gouache or linocut).
- Variety test: a young man and an older man in the chosen people style.
- Named-sign test on one place.
- `scripts/generate-images.mjs` reads `content/$COUNTRY/` but still saves into the shared `public/images/{places,portraits}` by id, with one shared manifest: Mexican ids could overwrite Italian images. Give each country its own image folder before generating.

## Clone or generalize?

**Decided (Josh, 2026-10-11): one codebase.** Some things have to be country-specific, but improving the app should improve every country wherever possible. Corollary: when something is shared in spirit but written per language (like the character prompt), prefer a shared template with only the language-specific words filled in, so an idea like "characters can't hear your pronunciation" is added once.


- **Clone** (copy the repo, find-and-replace): fastest on day one, about a day. But every fix lands twice (voice fallback, error handling, memory, the database), and the apps drift apart within weeks.
- **Generalize** (language packs, one codebase): about half a day more up front, then every improvement benefits both.
- **Chosen:** one codebase, two Railway services, each with a `LANGUAGE` setting (`it`, `es-mx`). Separate URLs and apps to the user, shared code underneath. This is what [Architecture → Keeping other languages possible](05-architecture.md) already intends.

## Risks beyond the code

- **Language quality:** Mexican usage differs from Claude's default Spanish (when to use *usted* vs *tú*, *¿mande?*, *ahorita*). Someone who knows Mexico should play it before trusting it.
- **Persona and name:** the vision's *amante dell'Italia* needs a Mexican counterpart, and "Fuori" is Italian; the Mexico app wants its own name.

## Rough total

About 1 day of code generalization (mostly Claude Code work) plus about a day of content, images and voice vetting.
