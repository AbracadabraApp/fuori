What it had (Claude):
- **The topic came from Josh, and she built on each answer.** Reading, then history, philosophy, Machiavelli, the republic: every question was about his last answer, one step deeper.
- **One connection turned talk into a place.** "Siena was a republic" joined his Machiavelli to the town hall in front of them, and the fresco is about exactly what he cares about. The bid was the payoff of the topic, not an errand.
- **They went, then and there.** The bench became a walk, the Campo, tickets, a room, a painting. The scene moved with the talk.
- **The flirting was his, and she met it in role.** He asked about her ("tu?"), bought her ticket, kept her when she offered the goodbye. She switched to tu, came along ("more fun together") and took "bellissima" warmly. Never coy, never out of role.
- **The culture was real and local:** the shell-shaped Campo, the Palio, Porta Camollia, Lorenzetti's Good and Bad Government, Justice, the cord.
- **Josh steered too** (the walls, then "no, the Campo and the painting"), and she followed.

What went wrong:
- The devil story (53–58): Josh asked if she knew it, she asked "want to hear it?", he said yes, and she never told it.
- She offered the goodbye twice (48, 68) mid-outing; the second time it landed fine, the first time Josh had to keep her.
- A mishearing (25) got the question asked again, which was the right thing.
