Three card types

  • When you… One topic the conversation involved (introductions, ordering, asking the price), as that lesson's phrases.
  • Formal and informal. The two forms, one above the other.
  • About this place. One fact about where you were. It doesn't have to come from the conversation.

Rules

  • One topic per card, and nothing under it: no footer, no second topic.
  • Whole sentences, no blanks. The English goes under each line.
  • Lines run in the order they're said: the question, then the answer.
  • Cards are sage green.
  • Plain cards. No audio, no replayed moments, nothing about the next visit, no city or day, no red.
  • Cards are often picked by what the conversation involved, but not always. A card can stand by itself, as About this place does.
  • The first card's headline sits at the top of the sheet, level with the close button. No Done at the bottom.
  • No picture inside the sheet. After the goodbye it slides up over the person screen, which stays as it is. Closing returns to the place list.

Still open

  • A footer for the sheet. Two candidates so far: Try it, and Open the lesson. Neither is on the sheet for now.
  • How many cards a sheet can hold. Three fit comfortably under the picture.
  • The close button's exact look.
  • Whether Words and the history card ("Something she mentioned") stay as types.

Seven layouts cover all 77 lessons

A topic card is one type, but topics don't all explain the same way. Ordering is an opening you fill in; polite or friendly is a choice of two; a price is a number. Sorting every lesson that isn't a culture story by what it teaches gives seven layouts.

Polite or friendlyLesson ›
¿Cómo está?Doña Juana, anyone older, a stranger
¿Cómo estás?a friend, a child

Layout: this or that. Two ways to say one thing, and who or when each is for.

OrderingLesson ›Me da ___, por favorun tamalun atoledos de chipilín

Layout: fill it in. One opening that takes whatever you want after it.

Saying goodbyeLesson ›Que le vaya bienGoodbye, to someone you call usted. Learn it as one piece.

Layout: say it whole. A phrase to learn as one piece, not to take apart.

When they ask youLesson ›
She¿Qué le doy?What can I get you?
YouMe da un tamal.

Layout: they say, you say. A line you will hear, and what to say back.

How muchLesson ›¿Cuánto es?45 pesoscuarenta y cincoThree tamales at quince each.

Layout: numbers and letters. A price, a count, a spelling: the figure and its words.

Time hereLesson ›Abito qui da un anno.Word for word: I live here since a year. Italian keeps it in the present.

Layout: not like English. The language builds the sentence another way.

Saying it softlyLesson ›un momentitoThe -ito makes it smaller and kinder. It can still be ten minutes.

Layout: a little word. One small word or ending that changes the tone or the meaning.
